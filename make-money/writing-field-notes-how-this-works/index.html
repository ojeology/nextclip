Writing Field Notes: How I Research Sites, Write to Them, and What I Will Not Fake

Writing is the online skill that has actually paid me. This is the process I use instead of buying scripts: research a website, write to them, publish what happened. A gig is not guaranteed.

Ibrahim Sodiq2026-08-198 min read

The only method that has paid me so far

I have chased the usual shortcuts. Writing is the thing that has actually produced money. Not because writing is easy, and not because the internet owes anyone a gig. Because a website that needs words will sometimes pay a person who can deliver them.

That is the whole idea behind BRYME Writing Field Notes. I research real publications. I write to them when I am going to talk about them. I publish what happened — including silence. Then, on YouTube, I walk through about three of those notes and how the money path actually works. If you want the dates, the apply links and the leftover sites from that month, they live on the field-notes page.

A gig is not guaranteed. Anyone selling you a script that skips this part is selling hope.

What this series is not

It is not a directory of every website that has ever had a write-for-us page.

It is not a promise that publications accept writers from Nigeria, or that they pay on time, or that they still run the programme you saw in a 2023 screenshot.

It is not a course. I will not ask you to pay to unlock a list.

If a row on the field-notes page does not have a date, treat it as unfinished. If I did not email them, I will not invent how long they take to reply.

The process, in the order I actually do it

First I pick a niche I can write. Tech explainers, money explainers, entertainment — only if I can produce a piece I would put my name on. Applying for medical or legal content I cannot stand behind is how you get rejected and deserve it.

Second I find the real door: a contributor page, an editor address, a form. I copy the URL. I read two or three of their recent pieces so the pitch is not generic.

Third I record the date I applied, what I sent, and what they asked for. That log is the product. Memory is how people start lying to themselves.

Fourth I wait. I do not send five follow-ups in a week. If nothing comes back, the note says “no reply as of this date.” That is a complete note.

Fifth, if they reply, I write down the brief, the pay if they named it, and the payment method if they named it. I do not upgrade “we will be in touch” into “I landed the gig.”

Country is not a bonus filter. It is the point.

Most public lists of “sites that pay writers” are written for people with a US or UK payment setup. From Lagos, the questions that actually matter are narrower.

Do they accept work from Nigeria, or do they quietly drop those emails? Do they pay through a method that arrives here, or only through a method that does not? Did they say any of this in writing, or am I guessing from a comment section?

I start these notes from Nigeria because that is where I write from. Other countries can be added later, the same way: one writer, dated applications, no copied reply times. If I do not know whether a site takes your country, the page will say unknown. Unknown is honest. “They hire worldwide” without evidence is not.

How money actually moves — when it moves

A typical paid path looks like this: you apply, they ask for a sample or a pitch, they give a brief, you write, they edit, they publish, they pay through the method they already use.

Any of those steps can stop. A killed draft is not a payment. A published piece with “exposure” and no invoice is unpaid work, and I will label it unpaid.

I will not publish a rate I did not see on their site or in their email. I will not publish a payout timeline I did not live. If their page is silent on money, the field-notes card will say unknown.

Earnings vary. The first legitimate writing money can be small. That is not a reason to buy a script. It is a reason to treat writing as a skill you practise, not a jackpot.

What I log for every site

Name and niche. The apply URL and the method — email, form, or pitch. Whether they say they pay, do not pay, or do not say. Payment methods they have named, if any. The country I wrote from. Whether I contacted them, and on which date. Whether they replied, and on which date. Whether I landed a gig, and whether I was paid. What they want, who should skip them, and what is still unknown. The date I last checked the page.

If a field would require a guess, I leave it empty and list it under unknowns.

The YouTube half

Each video covers about three sites I have already logged. The video should still be useful if you never open BRYME: what they publish, how I applied, what they said about money, and what happened in my inbox.

The website is for the parts that are awkward on camera — the exact links, the dates, the sites I researched but did not feature, and the updates when a programme closes.

I will not make “go to my website for the real list” videos that withhold the answer. If the answer is “they did not reply,” you will hear that in the video.

Why this is better than buying a script

A bought script is usually someone else’s voice, written for a different country, sold to people who do not want to send the email.

This method is slower. You will be ignored. You will write samples that nobody uses. You will also have a dated sentence you can stand behind: I wrote to them on this day, and this is what happened.

That sentence is reusable. A script is not. If writing never pays you, you still practised. If a script never pays you, you are only out of money.

Where the live list lives

The dated cards are on the Writing Field Notes page. This article is the rulebook. When a YouTube video goes up, the same page will carry the link.

I update a row when I re-check it. I do not delete a closed programme just to look current — a dated “closed” helps the next person more than a disappearing URL.

If you want the honest version of online writing, start there. If you want a guarantee, I do not have one.
