Web hosting costs explained: the true 3-year price (and the renewal trap)

By the BRYME Tech desk · published 2026-09-09

In one line: 2026 hosting prices honestly: intro vs renewal, the real bands by plan type, hidden add-ons, and an editable 3-year true-cost calculator.

BRYME Tech desk · published 12 September 2026 · general information and directional rates — never a quote; your cart and invoice govern

The 60-second answer. The price on a hosting homepage is almost never the price you’ll pay. That $2.79–$3.99/month banner is an intro rate, conditioned on prepaying 36–48 months up front — and it renews at 2–5× (Hostinger’s own tables: $2.99 intro → $10.99 renewal). The honest 2026 bands: shared hosting really costs $9–$16/month at renewal (standard rates $9.79–$13.79 are common), managed WordPress $9–$20, VPS $12–$60, dedicated $129–$450. Add a domain at ~$15/year after year-one promos and email at $0.39–$1.99 per mailbox/month, and a “$3 a month” website is really a $150–$300/year commitment — priced honestly over three years, not from the banner.

The calculator below turns any plan into its true 3-year cost — the only number worth comparing between hosts.

Section 3 · Why the renewal trap exists (and is legal)

You’re not being scammed — you’re being given a cash-flow discount: prepay three or four years and the host funds today’s server from tomorrow’s money. The marketing problem is that the intro rate is the only number on the homepage. Three habits neutralise it:

  • Price the renewal first. If year 2+ isn’t acceptable, the intro is irrelevant — no matter how big the percentage-off badge.
  • Match term to confidence. The 48-month prepay is only a bargain if you’d genuinely stay four years; 12-month terms cost a little more and keep you free.
  • Set the exit reminder now. Calendar reminder two weeks before renewal: either negotiate (see Section 5) or migrate while the site is small.

Section 4 · How much hosting do you actually need?

  • A static site or portfolio: possibly $0 — static hosts’ free tiers are real (this very site runs on one; free vs paid, honestly).
  • A blog or small business site: shared at renewal rates — ignore unlimited-everything badges; disk and “unlimited traffic” are rarely the constraint at this size.
  • A growing store or app: the VPS jump buys guaranteed resources, not speed alone — pay it when neighbours-on-your-server actually hurt.
  • Anything else: upgrade when measured pain appears (measure slowness properly first), not when a headline scares you.

Section 5 · The add-ons that move the bill

  • Domain renewal: ~$1 first-year promos reprice to ~$15/year — and transferring away can have its own fees (what a domain name really is).
  • Email mailboxes: $0.39–$1.99 per mailbox/month — five staff mailboxes can out-cost the hosting.
  • Backups and SSL: many entry plans now include basic SSL (what the padlock really means); daily-backup add-ons are worth it only if you’d pay a human to recreate the site without them.
  • Migration: “free migration” deals exist — price it if you’re switching, and get the old host’s exit terms in writing.

Renewal-day negotiation, honestly: the retention chat can offer real discounts to stop you leaving — it works more often than not, and costs five minutes. The credible alternative in your other tab is what makes it work.

FAQ

How much does website hosting really cost per month?
At renewal in 2026: shared $9–$16, managed WordPress $9–$20, VPS $12–$60, dedicated $129–$450. The $2–$6 numbers you see advertised are intro rates on long prepays.

Why is renewal so much more expensive?
The intro is a cash-flow discount for prepaying years ahead. Hosts publish renewal rates in their pricing tables — the 2–5× jump is standard across the industry, not one company’s trick.

How do I avoid the hosting renewal trap?
Budget from the renewal price, choose the shortest term you’d happily commit to, set a reminder two weeks before renewal, and negotiate or migrate at that point.

Is a VPS worth it over shared hosting?
When you need guaranteed resources (real traffic spikes, background jobs, strict neighbours) — the renewal gap between upper shared and entry VPS is smaller than the ad gap suggests, so the decision is about predictability, not price.

What’s the cheapest way to host a small website?
A static site on a free-tier static host ($0), plus a domain at ~$15/year. Add paid hosting only when the site needs a server-side engine.
