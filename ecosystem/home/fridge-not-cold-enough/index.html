Fridge not cold enough? Five checks before you pay for a repair

BRYME Home & DIY desk · reviewed 2026-09-09 · general information, not professional advice

In one line: The settings, airflow and coil checks that fix most “warm fridge” calls, and the signs that say it really is a technician’s problem.

A fridge that runs but does not quite cool is one of the most common “repair” calls — and a decent share of them are fixed in ten minutes with no parts. Work these checks in order; stop and call a technician the moment the path says so.

1. The thermostat, honestly

Dials marked 1–5 say nothing absolute; the manual says which end is colder. Confirm the setting first — dials get nudged by grocery bags. A fridge thermometer (cheap, honest) beats any built-in dial: you want roughly at or below 4°C / 40°F on the shelf, freezer around –18°C / 0°F.

2. Airflow inside

Cold air travels through vents between freezer and fridge. Boxes jammed against the back wall and vents blocked by a bag of peas mimic a failing fridge. Create space; let air move.

3. The door seal test

Close the door on a piece of paper; if it slides out with no resistance at several points, the gasket is not sealing and cold air leaks out. Clean sticky residue off the gasket; a perished or torn one is a replaceable part on most models — cheaper than a new fridge.

4. The coils you never see

Unplug the fridge, find the coils (behind the kick plate or on the back), and brush/vacuum the dust blanket off. Coils choked with dust shed heat poorly and the cabinet warms. This is the single most common neglected maintenance on kitchen appliances — it is on the seasonal checklist for that reason.

5. Give it time, then listen

After any change, give the fridge several hours with the door kept closed. Then listen: a compressor that clicks on and off every few minutes, or hums constantly without cooling, is failing — and that, along with frost patterns that suggest a defrost-system fault, is genuinely a technician’s job. Refrigerant and sealed systems are not DIY, full stop.

When you call, say this

“Compressor runs continuously, cabinet at 10°C, coils cleaned, seal tested” gets a better visit than “it’s warm” — and sometimes a better answer: knowing the checks were done may save you the call-out entirely.
