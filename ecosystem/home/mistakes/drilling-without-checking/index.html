Drilling without checking what's behind the wall

BRYME Home & DIY desk · reviewed 2026-09-09 · general information, not professional advice

The mistake: Pipes and cables hide behind plaster, and they do not forgive. Two minutes with a detector beats a flood or a shock.

Every wall conceals a map of things that must not be drilled: water pipes, gas runs and electrical cables feeding the sockets and switches. Drilling blind is the DIY mistake with the worst instant consequences — a geyser of water, a bang and a dead circuit, or worse.

Why it backfires

Cables and pipes rarely run the way people guess. Electricians commonly run cable drops vertically above and below sockets and switches, and horizontally at switch and socket height; pipes chase vertically and horizontally between fixtures. But repairs and renovations break every rule, and previous owners were not obliged to leave you a map. Assumption is not a locating method.

The fix

Buy an inexpensive multi-scanner (stud, live-wire and metal detector) and actually use it — scan a wider area than the hole, both directions, and trust it over your memory of the room's layout. Keep fixings shallow where possible (most wall plugs need far less depth than people drill). If you do strike a pipe or cable: stop, do not pull the drill out while it is still in contact with a live conductor — switch off at the consumer unit first if anything electrical is involved — and call a professional. Water: main stopcock off, then assess.

Keep it from happening

Photograph walls before covering them with your own work, keep a simple paper map of your own socket runs, and borrow the rule the pros use: no detector, no drill. The basic toolkit list includes the detector for exactly this reason, and the breaker guide explains what the trip actually saved you from. Anything beyond a fixing hole — chasing walls, new circuits, anything near the panel — belongs to a qualified electrician, full stop.
