Governance & competitions

How are Champions League draws seeded?

The short answer

UEFA seeds its draws using club coefficients — a rolling five-year points system built from results in UEFA competitions plus bonuses for reaching certain rounds. Clubs are grouped into pots by coefficient, and the draw takes one team from each pot per group, with country-protection rules preventing clubs from the same nation meeting.

A coefficient is a five-year rolling score: clubs earn two points for a win and one for a draw in UEFA competition matches, plus bonus points for reaching the group stage, the knockout rounds and the final. Domestic league results do not count towards the coefficient — only European ones. That is why a club can have a poor domestic season and still be a dangerous seed the following year.

The league-phase draw sorts the participating clubs into pots by that coefficient, then draws opponents so each club faces a fixed number of teams from each pot, with the constraint that no club plays another from its own national association in that phase. Knockout draws are seeded differently: in the old format, group winners were seeded against runners-up and could not face a club from their own group or country.

The point of seeding is twofold — sporting fairness and commercial balance. Rewarding sustained European performance means a club cannot buy its way into a favourable draw overnight, and separating the strongest clubs in the early rounds keeps the marquee ties for later. It also creates a feedback loop: doing well in Europe makes future draws kinder, which is part of why the same clubs keep going deep.

The system is frequently criticised because it entrenches the established elite — a club's coefficient reflects its past five years, so a newcomer has to overperform for several seasons before it gets seeded. UEFA has repeatedly adjusted the bonus structure and access lists in response, but the basic five-year principle has been stable for decades.

In practice

A club that reaches the Champions League semi-final banks a large coefficient haul for that season. Five years later that run is still inside the rolling window, lifting its pot placement even if its domestic form has collapsed. Conversely, a club that wins its league from nowhere enters the competition with a thin coefficient and lands in a lower pot — which is how newly qualified champions end up in brutal groups.

Common misunderstandings

  • Seeding is based on domestic league position. It is based on European results over five years. Winning your domestic league gets you into the competition; it does not seed you well.
  • The draw is completely random. It is constrained: pots by coefficient, country-protection rules, and restrictions on rematches of certain pairings within a season.
  • Two teams from the same country can meet any time. Country protection applies in the league phase — clubs from the same association are kept apart in that draw, and the restriction also applied to the old group stage.

Quick questions

How long does a coefficient result stay on a club's record?

Five seasons. Points from a season drop out of the calculation once they are more than five years old, which is why coefficients rise and fall gradually rather than jumping.

Do Europa League results affect Champions League seeding?

They feed the same UEFA coefficient system, so strong Europa League runs do contribute to a club's overall coefficient, though the bonuses are smaller.
