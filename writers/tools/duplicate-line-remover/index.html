About this tool

How to use the duplicate line remover.

What it does. Deletes repeated lines, keeping the first occurrence of each and preserving the original order.

How to use it. Paste your list - keywords, tags, citation lines - and copy the deduplicated result. Lines count as duplicates only when they match exactly after trimming, so near-duplicates still deserve your eye.

Why it matters. Duplicated lines in a list or a data set undermine trust in everything around them. Mechanical deduplication is exhaustive in a way scanning by eye never is.

Your data. The tool runs entirely in your browser - text you paste never leaves the page, and nothing is uploaded to a server.

Good to know

Trailing spaces are trimmed before comparison, so 'item ' and 'item' count as one. Lines that differ by a single character or comma are not duplicates to the tool - scan the near-misses yourself.

Where this fits

Formatting tools are the honest kind of tidy: they change nothing you said, only how it is carried. Run them before proofreading, not after - there is no sense polishing text a cleanup pass is about to reshape, and a clean base makes every later check mean what it says.

The Duplicate Line Remover And Its Safety

The duplicate line remover finds and removes repeated lines in text — the paste duplicates, the doubled paragraphs, the copy that repeated itself somewhere in the workflow — and reports what it removed.

Paste the text and the tool identifies exact and near-duplicate lines, showing them grouped with their occurrence counts and line numbers before anything is removed. The writer chooses to keep first occurrences, keep last occurrences or review each group — the removal is always a confirmed action rather than a surprise.

The report-first design protects the cases where duplication is meaningful: repeated chorus lines in a transcript, a deliberate refrain, a template’s repeated block. The grouping view makes those visible instead of silently deduplicating a song.

The tool matches lines by text and catches near-duplicates through normalised comparison — whitespace and case tolerant — but it cannot see meaning. Two identical lines that serve different purposes in context still match. That is why nothing is removed until the writer confirms each group or a chosen rule.

The working use is cleanup after messy transfers — exporting from a CMS, consolidating notes, fixing a doubled transcription — where duplicates are accidents to be removed. For lists, logs and word banks it is the fastest janitor on the desk, and the confirmation step means it never becomes a vandal.

How the tool treats your text

  • Runs in the browser: the text never leaves the page unless the tool says otherwise
  • Free for individual use; bulk and API use by written permission
  • Every result states what was counted and how, beside the result
  • Honest limits published on the page, not buried in a footer
  • Method reviewed and dated on the desk’s editorial calendar

Background context cross-checked against the encyclopaedic record where a reference helps. The desk’s standard applies here as everywhere: the arithmetic is shown, the method is stated and the limits are named. The tool is one instrument on a working writer’s bench: it handles the mechanical layer so the writer’s attention stays on the argument, the evidence and the voice. Everything the tool counts, it counts the same way every time, which is more than any tired eye at the end of a deadline can promise. When the tool and the writer disagree, the writer wins — but the disagreement is worth having, because it means the choice was made rather than missed. The tool runs locally in the page, needs no account and forgets the text when the tab closes, which is the privacy posture a working draft deserves. Reviewed 2026-09-28.
