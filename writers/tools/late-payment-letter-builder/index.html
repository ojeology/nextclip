About this tool

How to use the late payment letter builder.

What it does. Three escalating letters for an unpaid invoice — a polite reminder, a firm follow-up, and a final notice — generated from the same invoice details. Days overdue compute from the due date, the amount formats in your currency, and every letter names one clear next step.

How to use it. Fill in what's on the invoice — who owes you, how much, when it fell due — then pick the stage you've reached. Start at stage one and wait a few working days before escalating. Read the preview, adjust anything in the form, then print the letter or copy its text straight into an email. Your details stay on this device.

Why it matters. Chasing money you're owed is the most awkward fifteen minutes in freelancing — which is exactly why the guide to [invoicing a publication](/learn/freelance-paid-writing/how-to-invoice-as-a-writer/) says to chase on a schedule, in writing, with a set escalation path. A letter that states facts — amount, days overdue, one clear ask — reads as administration rather than aggression, and most clients pay long before stage three.
