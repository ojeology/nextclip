Word counter.

Type or paste your text and BRYME counts words and characters instantly. Great for hitting a target word count before you submit or publish.

About this tool

How to use the word counter.

What it does. Counts the words in your text instantly, including how it treats contractions, hyphenated words and apostrophes.

How to use it. Paste or type your text into the box. The word and character counts update live, so you can trim to a target length as you write. Use it to hit a publication's word limit, a blog post length, or a word-count goal for a session.

Why it matters. Word count is one of the few concrete, enforceable limits in writing. Editors and guidelines state a range; a word counter lets you meet it exactly instead of counting manually.

Your data. The tool runs entirely in your browser - text you paste never leaves the page, and nothing is uploaded to a server.

Good to know

Where this fits

Counting tools answer one question precisely: how much? They matter at the boundaries - the limit on a form, the length an editor asked for, the session you promised yourself. Used once, a counter answers a question; used as a habit, it keeps your work inside the frames that get it read.

The Word Counter And The Full Count Report

The word counter reports the complete count profile of a text — words, characters, sentences, paragraphs, longest words and averages — in one live report, the standard instrument panel for writing to any specification.

Paste the text and the full report updates live: total words and characters both with and without spaces, sentence and paragraph counts, average sentence and word length, and the longest of each located in the text. The report matches the counting conventions that submission systems and style guides use.

The complete profile is what distinguishes a word counter from a length check: average word length and sentence length are the two statistics editors watch and the numbers behind every readability score. The longest-word and longest-sentence locators turn the statistics into edit targets.

Counts measure length and nothing else — the tool cannot judge quality, fit or register and does not try. The conventions it counts by are the common standards; a specific house style that counts differently is the house’s rule, and the writer checks the specification against the report.

The working use is the permanent companion: the live count while drafting to length, the full profile before submission, the average statistics during the edit. Writers keep it open the way a driver keeps the speedometer in view — not for the numbers themselves but for the limits they represent.

How the tool treats your text

  • Runs in the browser: the text never leaves the page unless the tool says otherwise
  • Free for individual use; bulk and API use by written permission
  • Every result states what was counted and how, beside the result
  • Honest limits published on the page, not buried in a footer
  • Method reviewed and dated on the desk’s editorial calendar

Background context cross-checked against the encyclopaedic record where a reference helps. The desk’s standard applies here as everywhere: the arithmetic is shown, the method is stated and the limits are named. The tool is one instrument on a working writer’s bench: it handles the mechanical layer so the writer’s attention stays on the argument, the evidence and the voice. Everything the tool counts, it counts the same way every time, which is more than any tired eye at the end of a deadline can promise. When the tool and the writer disagree, the writer wins — but the disagreement is worth having, because it means the choice was made rather than missed. The tool runs locally in the page, needs no account and forgets the text when the tab closes, which is the privacy posture a working draft deserves. Reviewed 2026-09-28.
