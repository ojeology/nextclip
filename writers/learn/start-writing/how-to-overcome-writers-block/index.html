How to overcome writer's block

Practical, non-mystical ways to get unstuck when the words will not come.

Beginner Start here — assumes no prior knowledge.

Researched & written by BRYME Editorial Desk · 2 min read · Updated 2026-09-04

← All Start writing guides

Writer's block is rarely a creative curse. It is almost always one of four concrete problems that have a concrete fix.

What it is

The feeling of being unable to write. In practice it usually means one of these: you do not know what to say, you are afraid it will be bad, you are overwhelmed by the size of the task, or you are too tired or distracted to focus.

Why it matters

Knowing the real cause lets you pick the right cure. Trying to "force inspiration" for a planning problem, or "plan harder" for a fear problem, will just make the block worse.

When to use it

Whenever you sit down to write and nothing happens for more than a few minutes.

Step-by-step guide

Name the block. Ask yourself: am I stuck on what to say, am I afraid it will be bad, am I overwhelmed, or am I tired? Pick one.

If you are stuck on what to say, plan in small pieces. Do not try to write the piece. Write the outline first, then one section at a time.

If you are afraid it will be bad, write a throwaway draft. Tell yourself this version is just for you and no one will read it. Perfection is the enemy of a first draft.

If you are overwhelmed, shrink the task. "Write an article" becomes "write the second bullet point I already know." One sentence is a legitimate start.

If you are tired or distracted, change the conditions. A short break, a glass of water, a clean desk, or a timer for a few minutes of focused work often clears it.

Change your method. Type on a keyboard if you usually handwrite, or speak your thoughts into a voice memo and transcribe them. A different channel can unlock you.

Make the goal to produce words, not to be good. Winning is writing a number of words, no matter how rough.

Common mistakes

Waiting for the perfect idea. Re-reading and editing the same paragraph over and over. Trying to fix all of the piece at once. Working when exhausted and confusing that with being blocked.

Practical tips

  • Write a timer's worth before you are allowed to judge it.
  • Use a rough "brain dump" file with no rules.
  • Keep a list of ideas so you always have a starting point.
  • Change your environment when you are stuck in the same chair.
