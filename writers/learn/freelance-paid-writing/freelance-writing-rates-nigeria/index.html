Freelance writing rates in Nigeria — the two-currency market

The same Lagos freelance market pays ₦0.5 a word for mill work and $300 an article for international clients. The tiers in naira and dollars, the rails, and how to change tiers.

Intermediate Assumes you can already draft and revise a piece.

Researched & written by BRYME Editorial Desk · 3 min read · Updated 2026-09-06

← All Freelance & paid writing guides

The Nigerian writing market is really two markets sharing one city, and the exchange rate between them is enormous. The same week can contain a ₦2,000 product-description batch for a local buyer and a $300 feature for an American fintech — roughly the same work, different universes. This guide maps both universes honestly, because pretending there is one "Nigerian rate" is how writers get underpriced.

The tiers, in naira and dollars

Synthesised from current Nigerian market guides and platform data (2026); observed ranges, not an official survey:

TierLocal (naira)International (dollars)
Entry₦0.5–1.5/word · ₦5,000–15,000 per 500 words$15–30 per 500 words
Established₦3–5/word · ₦10,000–25,000 per 1,000 words$30–60 per 1,000 words
Specialist₦30,000–80,000 per article for well-funded local firms$50–300+ per article
Retainer / expert₦150,000–400,000/month (Nigerian corporates)₦300,000–800,000+/month (international clients)

The pattern to internalise: naira rates describe the local market; dollar rates describe the international one — and the work is often identical. Entry-level per-word figures in naira translate to fractions of a US cent; the same writer's first direct international client multiplies the effective rate tenfold without a skill change.

The local market, honestly

Nigerian corporates — especially fintech, which pays ₦150,000–₦500,000/month at staff level and similar freelance rates — are a real, growing buyer set with money behind them. Local work pays in the currency you live in, arrives through instant transfers, and builds a portfolio fast. It is worth having.

But its ceiling is real too, and the small-business end (₦60,000–₦150,000/month expectations) cannot sustain a full-time specialist. The correct local strategy is selectivity: corporate retainers yes, "exposure" and ₦2/word bulk no — the [two-markets problem](/learn/freelance-paid-writing/african-writers-international-markets/) applies at full force at home.

The international market, practically

International clients pay against their market's card — this is the standing rule of this series and the core of our [Nigerian writers' market guide](/learn/freelance-paid-writing/nigerian-writers-us-uk-markets/): US buyers pay [US rates](/learn/freelance-paid-writing/freelance-writing-rates-us/). The practical path in runs through portfolios and direct outreach more than bidding platforms, where you compete on price against the whole world instead of on value against nobody.

Two Nigeria-specific facts belong in every international-rate decision:

  1. The W-8BEN fact: with no US–Nigeria tax treaty, US-source royalties (KDP, some platforms) suffer the full 30% withholding — but client service income invoiced directly is not subject to that. The guide above has the details and the form mechanics.
  2. The 2026 home-front tax law now expects worldwide income declared and self-assessed — price with that reality in your head, not beneath it. Figures live in the tax section of the same guide, verified against the new act.

The rails: USD-receiving accounts (Payoneer and peers) for platforms and foreign clients, domiciliary accounts to hold dollars, conversion as a choice — all documented in the [payment platforms comparison](/learn/freelance-paid-writing/payment-platforms-for-writers/). When the invoice is ready, the [invoice generator](/tools/invoice-generator/) speaks naira natively.

Changing tiers

Nobody moves from ₦1/word to $0.15/word inside one platform. The tier jump is a change of work: a [specialised niche](/learn/freelance-paid-writing/high-paying-writing-niches/) with consequence, three portfolio pieces that prove it, a rate card with published minimums, and pitches to buyers in the paying tier — the [raising-rates playbook](/learn/freelance-paid-writing/how-to-raise-your-freelance-rates/) applied across the currency divide. Check every new quote against the [rate calculator](/tools/freelance-rate-calculator/) before sending; per-word and project pricing split at specialist level, and the [pricing models comparison](/learn/freelance-paid-writing/per-word-day-rate-or-project-fee/) shows which fits the job.

What to do this month

  1. Locate your honest tier in the table — in both currencies.
  2. If you have no international client, make the first direct pitch this month; the [US pitching guide](/learn/writing-for-publication/how-to-pitch-us-editors/) is the editorial route and the niches guide is the commercial one.
  3. Write the rate card. Publish the minimums. End the negotiation before it starts.
  4. Set up the rail before the acceptance, not after — and keep the ledger the 2026 tax law assumes you keep.
  5. Price the next quote from the buyer's market, run it through the calculator, and invoice like a business — because as of 2026, the law treats you as one.

Tier figures synthesised from published Nigerian market guides and platform data as of 7 September 2026; no official survey exists, so treat these as observed market behaviour, not statistics. Rates vary by client, niche and experience.
