WRITERS / SUBMISSION GUIDE

Noema submissions: how to pitch, what to send and what pay is known

Official sources checked 9 October 2026. BRYME is an independent guide, not Noema’s submission portal.

Send Noema a full draft or a pitch of at least 500 words at edit@noemamag.com. Its official contact page says a full draft gives you the best chance. The linked writing guidelines suggest 2,500–4,000 words for a finished essay and require an AI statement with every pitch and draft. Neither page publishes a standard payment rate.

What to include in your Noema pitch

  1. Lead with a concise thesis. State the argument, not just a broad subject you want to explore.
  2. Meet the 500-word minimum if pitching instead of sending a draft. The usual short magazine-pitch template is not sufficient here.
  3. Give the expected finished word count. The official essay sweet spot is 2,500–4,000 words; it is guidance, not a guarantee that every accepted format uses that length.
  4. Link to a few published articles. The guidelines ask for previous work.
  5. Include an AI statement saying whether AI was used and, if so, how, where and why.

Noema’s contact page also invites reported features, interviews, videos and short fiction. Send editorial work to the editorial inbox above; original-image pitches go to art@noemamag.com. These are different submission routes.

What kind of essay fits?

Noema’s writing guidelines favour a clear argument developed through concrete examples for readers beyond academic specialisms. They ask writers to explain unfamiliar terms, use short organising subheadings and avoid long paragraphs that are difficult to read on mobile. When addressing a problem, they want substantial attention to proposed solutions rather than diagnosis alone.

BRYME’s practical check: before drafting, write down the claim you intend to defend, the evidence that could support it and the strongest objection. If your pitch only announces “an essay about AI” or “an essay about democracy,” it still needs a specific argument. This is our preparation advice, not an additional Noema rule.

Does Noema pay writers—and how much?

The official guidelines discuss payment through a 25% kill fee when Noema decides not to publish a piece written promptly and in good faith. They do not disclose a standard per-word or per-piece commission fee. Therefore BRYME cannot verify a blanket “$1 per word” rate from these sources.

Ask for the agreed fee, payment trigger, editing scope, expenses and rights in writing. A kill fee is not the fee for an accepted article, and its conditions matter. Full rights terms and a standard response-time commitment were not established from the two sources checked.

Disclosure is required; permission is not automatic

Noema prefers original work created without AI. It asks for upfront disclosure of any AI used to develop a pitch or draft and considers permitted use case by case. Disclosure alone is not blanket permission. Its guidance warns that unauthorised or undisclosed use can end the relationship and forfeit a previously discussed kill fee.

Common submission questions

Can I send a finished draft?

Yes. The official contact page specifically prefers a full draft over a pitch.

Is there a deadline or country restriction?

The checked guidance does not specify a closing date or country list. That is not a guarantee of eligibility for every writer or a permanently open window; recheck the official page before sending.

Can I submit simultaneously?

The checked pages do not settle simultaneous submissions. Ask the editor rather than assuming exclusivity or permission. No fixed follow-up timetable is claimed here.

Has BRYME submitted to Noema?

BRYME’s existing editorial record lists a rejected submission. That is not evidence of acceptance or payment, and it does not establish Noema’s response time for other writers.

Choose the right next step

Read our pitch-writing guide for structure, adapting it to Noema’s longer minimum. For different editorial needs, compare Literary Hub’s literary-culture pitches and The Drift’s essay guidelines. These are distinct publications, not interchangeable places to send the same generic pitch.

Sources: the official contact page and its linked writing guidelines, checked 9 October 2026. This update does not claim an editor interview or a new firsthand submission. Report a correction.
