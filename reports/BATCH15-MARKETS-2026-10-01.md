## 1. The three markets, with the sentences the figures came from

### Colorado Review — `colorado-review` (US)

| field | value | sentence |
|---|---|---|
| pay | $300 story/essay, $100 poet, $100 cover photography | "Colorado Review pays its writers and artists: $300 for short stories and nonfiction; $100 for poetry; and $100 for cover photography." |
| pay (again, by genre) | same | "We pay our poets a flat fee of $100 and we pay $300 for short stories and essays." |
| writer's cost | $3 online, book reviews free | "The fee for online submissions is $3. … There is no fee to submit." (book reviews) |
| window | fiction + poetry 1 Aug – 31 Mar; nonfiction year-round | "Fiction & poetry manuscripts are accepted from August 1 to March 31." / "Nonfiction manuscripts are accepted year-round." |
| length | 15–25 manuscript pages; poetry ≤7 poems, ≤20 pages | "generally, however, Colorado Review prefers short stories and essays that are somewhere between 15 and 25 manuscript pages" |
| eligibility | worldwide | "Authors do NOT need to be residents of Colorado or the United States." |
| simultaneous | accepted, notify immediately | "Simultaneous submissions are accepted; writers must notify us immediately if the work is accepted elsewhere." |
| rights | FNASR, revert on publication | "Colorado Review purchases First North American Serial Rights; all rights revert to the author upon publication in CR." |
| AI policy | not stated | no AI language anywhere on the page |

**The trap.** The page's "Deadlines" block lists *Spring — Reservation: November 15, Copy Due:
December 15* beside *Whole Page … Cost: $150* and *Half Page … Cost: $75*. Those are
**advertising** bookings and **advertiser** costs. Read carelessly they become a submission
deadline and a payment figure. Neither is recorded. Payment timing is likewise never
stated, so the record says so rather than guessing "on publication".

### The Fairy Tale Magazine — `fairy-tale-magazine` (International)

| field | value | sentence |
|---|---|---|
| pay | $25 USD per piece | "We pay $25USD for each poem and prose piece we publish." / "We pay $25 USD per piece (whether prose or poetry)." |
| window | 15–21 August, midnight to midnight EST | "Submission window: August 15-21 (midnight to midnight EST)" |
| length | stories 900–2,000 words; poems ≤500 words | "Short Stories (between 900-2,000 words) … Poetry (500 word limit) … (1 piece per submission period)" |
| fee | fall call described as no-fee | "Fall no-fee call for submissions" |
| eligibility | worldwide | "We are happy to read and publish work from any country, and are open to work with a wide range of backgrounds and experiences." |
| simultaneous | accepted, with WITHDRAW notice | "Simultaneous submissions are welcome. Please email … with WITHDRAW in the subject line to let us know if your work is accepted elsewhere." |
| AI policy | prohibited | "do not use AI. AI-generated submissions are equal to plagiarized work." |
| rights | first + electronic/digital, in perpetuity for promotion; revert after publication | "First and all electronic and digital rights are being sought…" |
| age | 18+ | "You must be 18 years old or older to submit work" |

**The trap.** The same page runs a Prose & Poetry Contest: "First place winners in both
categories will be awarded $100 USD", with "Contest fees go directly to prizes". The $100 is
**prize money and the fees are contest costs** — neither is the rate for published work, and
neither is recorded as one. The rate stays $25 USD.

**On the window.** The page states the window as "August 15-21" with no year, and anchors it
with "Chosen Authors notified on or around Sept 22, 2026" for the issue that "Debuts here on
Nov 1st, 2026". The record therefore dates the window 2026-08-15 → 2026-08-21 and marks the
market **closed** as of the check date. No 2027 window is invented, and the record asserts
nothing about whether the window recurs.
