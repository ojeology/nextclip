## 2. Reference Article (House Standard)

The completed article **"Water Damage Insurance Coverage: What's Actually Covered (and What Gets Denied) in 2026"** is the template for structure and quality going forward. It belongs under **Home & Property**. Every new article in any of the three active niches should match its structure:

- 60-second answer up top
- Clear comparison table(s)
- A decision flowchart or step-by-step process where relevant
- A "what if it goes wrong" section
- Regional differences (US/UK/CA at minimum, since we target Tier 1)
- An interactive tool or quiz embedded in the piece
- FAQ section with schema markup
- Internal linking plan
- **No fabricated credentials.** Byline is the real author. Do not invent a "reviewed by [licensed professional]" line unless an actual licensed person reviewed it. This is a non-negotiable house rule — violating it risks both Google penalties and real liability.
- **No unsourced statistics.** Every stat needs a citable source (industry body, government data, original research) before publishing. Flag unsourced numbers clearly if the draft isn't finished, the way the water damage draft does.

## 4. Standards and Conduct (How Agents Should Operate)

- **Say exactly what you know.** If a fact isn't verified, say so or don't publish it — don't smooth it over.
- **No fabricated expertise.** No invented reviewer names, credentials, or "15 years of experience" placeholders left in final copy.
- **Cite real sources** for every statistic, especially in Home & Property (insurance, real estate) and Writers/Freelance (tax, accounting) — both are YMYL-adjacent.
- **Tools before traffic.** Interactive tools should be functional before the article promoting them goes live, not after.
- **Consistency over speed.** One well-sourced, complete article beats three thin ones. Match the water damage article's depth as the floor, not the ceiling.
- **Flag, don't guess.** If a dev or writing agent hits something outside its confidence (a legal claim, a tax rule, a regional insurance difference), flag it for human review rather than filling it in with a plausible-sounding guess.
- **Corrections stay open.** If something published turns out to be wrong, it gets corrected in the open per the site's existing corrections policy — not quietly edited.
