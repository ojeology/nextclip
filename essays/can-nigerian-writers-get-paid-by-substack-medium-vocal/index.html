Can a Nigerian writer actually get paid by Substack, Medium or Vocal?

The writing is the easy part. Getting the money into a Nigerian account is where most of these platforms quietly stop working.

By Ibrahim Sodiq · 2026-09-06

← All essays

Every list of "platforms that pay writers" is written from somewhere Stripe works. Read one from Lagos and you will spend a month writing before discovering the part nobody mentioned: the payout button does not work for you.

So here is the version written from this side of it.

The one fact that decides everything

Substack, Medium and Vocal all pay through Stripe. Stripe does not pay out to Nigerian bank accounts.

Nigeria sits in what Stripe calls its Extended Network, through its subsidiary Paystack. That means a business can accept payments from Nigerian customers via Stripe. It does not mean a Nigerian can receive a Stripe payout. Those are different products, and the confusion between them wastes an enormous amount of people's time.

That single constraint explains almost every frustration Nigerian writers have with these platforms.

But the three platforms are not equally blocked

This is the part that gets flattened in most advice, and the distinction is worth real money.

Stripe has two account types, and they have different country coverage:

  • Stripe Standard — the full account. Roughly 48 countries. Nigeria is not one.
  • Stripe Express — a lighter, platform-managed payout account. Wider coverage, and platforms can enable it per country.

Substack and Medium have historically used Stripe Standard. Vocal Media uses Stripe Express.

That is why you will find Nigerian writers who genuinely cannot connect Substack, and Nigerian writers who are being paid by Vocal. They are not lying about each other. They are on different rails.

What each platform actually looks like

Vocal Media — the most accessible

Vocal publishes its numbers openly, which is more than most:

Free creatorVocal+ ($9.99/mo)
Rate per 1,000 reads$3.80$6.00
Minimum withdrawal$35$20
Withdrawal fee7%2.9%

Do the arithmetic before you get excited. At $3.80 per 1,000 reads, $35 requires roughly 9,200 reads. If your pieces average 100 reads, that is about 92 articles before your first withdrawal.

Vocal is owned by Creatd, Inc., a publicly traded US company, and it does pay — there are verified withdrawal screenshots in creator communities. But there are also credible, repeated complaints on Trustpilot about account suspensions with accrued balances withheld. Treat it as real income with real counterparty risk, and withdraw at the threshold rather than letting a balance build.

Medium — possible, with a caveat I want to be honest about

Medium's official payout terms are clear and worth knowing:

  • Minimum payout: $10 USD — much lower than Vocal's $35
  • Payouts initiated by the 10th of each month, for the previous month
  • Then a further 5–7 business days to reach your bank
  • Requires a connected Stripe account and verified taxpayer information
  • Balances under $10 roll over

Medium also requires you to be a paying member ($5/month) to join the Partner Program. So you are paying $60 a year for the right to earn, and typical earnings for most writers land somewhere between $10 and $100 a month. Run that sum honestly before you commit.

Now the caveat. Several Nigerian creators have published detailed accounts of connecting Medium payouts via Stripe Express onboarding, using a Nigerian passport and local bank details, completing a W-8BEN rather than a US tax form, and receiving Naira. Medium did expand Partner Program eligibility to a large number of additional countries.

I could not verify that route on Medium's own documentation. Their published payout page says "Stripe" without specifying account type or listing countries. So: treat it as reported by other writers, not as confirmed by Medium. The only way to know is to open the enrollment flow and see what it offers you. That costs you nothing but time.

Substack — the hardest, and the most worth it if you solve it

Substack is the platform most people actually want, because the model is the best one:

  • Readers pay you directly, not the platform
  • Substack takes 10%, Stripe takes about 2.9% + $0.30
  • You keep roughly 90% and own an exportable email list
  • Nearly 100,000 publishers are earning money on Substack globally, almost 30,000 of them outside the US

And it is the one where the Stripe wall is highest, because paid subscriptions require a full Stripe account.

But note what is not blocked: publishing is free, and building an audience is free. Nothing stops a Nigerian writer from running a Substack, growing a list, and monetising it another way — a Paystack or Flutterwave link, a Buy Me a Coffee button, selling a product, or converting readers into clients. The email list is yours and exportable. That asset is worth more than three months of Vocal reads.

The workarounds, ranked by how much I would trust them

1. Stripe Express, where a platform offers it. Free, legitimate, uses your real identity and your own bank. If the flow accepts you, this is the answer. Start here.

2. A virtual USD account — Payoneer or Grey. Widely used by Nigerian freelancers, and you connect it as the payout destination. Watch the conversion spread and fees; they can quietly eat a meaningful share of small payouts.

3. A US or UK company with a real bank account. Works, and is what larger operators do. It costs a few hundred dollars to set up plus ongoing filing and tax obligations. Only sensible once the income justifies it — not for a $35 Vocal withdrawal.

4. Using someone else's Stripe account. Widely suggested. Don't. The tax documents are issued in their name, the funds are legally theirs, and you have no recourse if the relationship sours. It is a bad trade for money this size.

What I would actually do

If you are starting from zero in Nigeria:

Do not begin with the platform that pays per read. Ninety-two articles to earn $35 is not a business, it is a hobby with paperwork.

Begin with commissions. A single UK or Canadian magazine commission pays more than months of Vocal reads — Pellicle pays £375 for a feature, Briarpatch CAD$350 for investigative work, Geist up to CAD$650. Those pay by bank transfer or PayPal on an invoice you send, with no Stripe involved at all. BRYME lists [138 verified publications](/writing/) with the rate stated on each.

Run a Substack alongside it, free. Build the list. Solve the payment rail later, when there is something worth collecting.

Treat platform income as supplementary. It is genuinely supplementary — for almost everyone, including writers in countries where Stripe works perfectly.

The uncomfortable summary

The barrier for Nigerian writers is not talent, and it is not access to markets. Publications across the UK, Canada and Australia accept international submissions and pay properly.

The barrier is a payment rail — a business decision made in San Francisco about which countries are worth supporting. It is worth knowing precisely where that wall sits, so you stop pushing on it and walk around instead.
