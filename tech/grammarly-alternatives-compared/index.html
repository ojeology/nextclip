Grammarly alternatives: free and paid, honestly compared

By the BRYME Tech desk · published 2026-09-09

In one line: Grammarly's free tier is deliberately thin. Depending on what you actually need, the free alternatives now cover more ground — and the paid gaps have narrowed.

Grammarly won the grammar-checker name recognition war, and its free tier is built like a funnel: spelling and basic grammar included; tone, clarity rewrites, plagiarism and the fuller rewording engine held back for the subscription. That's a legitimate business model — but it means "Grammarly" and "free Grammarly" are different products, and plenty of writers never need the paid one. Here's the honest field, checked September 2026.

LanguageTool: the best free default

LanguageTool is the free alternative that most people actually want: core grammar and spelling with no meaningful word-count ceiling, 30+ languages (Grammarly is English-centric), browser extensions that work across Gmail, Docs and most web editors, and an open-source core you can inspect. Style and "picky" rules sit behind its own premium tier, but the free layer is a complete product, not a teaser — for multilingual writers it isn't just the value pick, it's the only pick.

Hemingway Editor: the readability pass

Hemingway (free, web and desktop) doesn't do grammar at all — it scores readability and names the disease: passive voice, adverb fog, sentences that need a machete. Grammarly polishes sentences; Hemingway tells you which ones to delete. It pairs with any checker rather than competing, and the free web version covers everything most drafts need.

ProWritingAid and QuillBot: the specialists

ProWritingAid's free tier (~500 words per check) goes deeper on style analysis than Grammarly free — overused words, sentence variety, pacing — aimed at authors and long-form writers who'll trade a word cap for a real critique. QuillBot is the paraphraser: free-tier rewording with tighter limits, useful for untangling your own clumsy paragraphs and for nothing else. Grammarly Premium's domain — tone detection, full-sentence rewrites, plagiarism — remains the most integrated single package if you publish professionally all day; the honest gap is that the free ecosystem now covers maybe 80% of that for nothing.

The decision, by need

Multilingual or free-forever: LanguageTool. Clearer, tighter prose: Hemingway alongside any checker. Deep style critique on long drafts: ProWritingAid (free tier, paid for novels). Professional publishing pipeline with tone and plagiarism in one pane: that's the case for paying for something — Grammarly Premium or ProWritingAid Premium, whichever's interface you'll actually keep open. And the desk's own trick, free: run a draft through LanguageTool and Hemingway — two minutes, different diseases, better than either premium tool at only its own job (the wider free-software canon is on this desk).

Free tiers checked September 2026 — word caps and feature lines shift with pricing seasons; the product pages are the authority on the day.
