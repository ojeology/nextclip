AI assistants compared: the 2026 matrix (free tiers, prices, caps)

By the BRYME Tech desk · published 2026-09-09

In one line: What's actually free across ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity, Copilot and more, what the $20 tier buys, and a needs-based chooser. Checked 12 Sep 2026.

BRYME Tech desk · published 12 September 2026 · market table checked 12 September 2026 — AI products change monthly; re-verify before you pay

The 60-second answer. Every major AI assistant has a genuinely usable free tier in 2026 — what the ±$20/month tiers actually buy you is relief from caps, not secret features. The honest map (checked 12 Sep 2026): Claude for writing and code ($20/mo, $17 annual), Perplexity for research with visible sources ($20/mo, 5 free Pro searches/day), Copilot if you live in Microsoft 365 (free in Windows; Pro $20), Mistral Le Chat when EU data residency matters ($14.99), Grok free with an X account, DeepSeek and Meta AI free outright — and the cheapest paid step in the industry is Gemini’s AI Plus at $4.99/mo. ChatGPT remains the broadest generalist (free, ad-supported in the US; Plus $20).

Use the chooser to rank them by your needs, then read the full matrix — and the caps table, because caps are what you’re really buying.

Which assistant for which job

  • Writing and code: Claude’s editorial quality and long-document handling keep winning comparisons; ChatGPT is the broader all-rounder if you also want images and data analysis in one place. — the three-way breakdown lives in ChatGPT vs Claude vs Gemini
  • Research where sources matter: Perplexity — the citations are the product. For getting your site into those answers, that’s a different game (getting cited by AI search).
  • Google-centric work: Gemini — and its $4.99 AI Plus is the sensible first paid experiment for anyone.
  • Microsoft 365 shops: Copilot — but check the licence requirement before assuming the $20 buys everything.
  • Privacy-sensitive / EU: Mistral Le Chat’s EU residency is the differentiator.
  • Zero budget: ChatGPT free, Gemini free, DeepSeek, Meta AI — genuinely usable, capped (where free tiers really hold up).

Caps: what you’re actually buying at $20

The honest read of 2026’s pricing: free = capable but rationed; $20 = the caps stop hurting; $100–$200 = more caps gone (ChatGPT Pro $200, Claude Max $100/$200, Perplexity Max $200). Power tiers mostly buy higher limits — pay for one only after you’ve hit the $20 tier’s ceiling repeatedly, not because a feature list whispered.

  • ChatGPT: caps reset roughly every 3 hours — heavy mornings can burn through them.
  • Claude: per-5-hour windows — plan long sessions, not bursts.
  • Perplexity: the free 5 Pro searches/day is generous for occasional research; daily researchers hit it by noon.

When to actually pay

  • You’ve hit free caps repeatedly (a week of real work, not one busy Sunday).
  • The paid capability is load-bearing: long documents on Claude, sourced research on Perplexity, Workspace integration on Gemini.
  • Annual billing: typically ~15% under monthly if the choice is already made ($17 effective Claude annual, $200/yr Perplexity).
  • Not yet: if you can’t name the task that hit the cap, stay free another month — useful vs hype applies to subscriptions too.

FAQ

Which AI assistant is completely free?
All of the major ones have usable free tiers: ChatGPT (ad-supported in the US), Gemini, Claude (daily caps), Perplexity (5 Pro searches/day), Copilot in Windows, Grok via X, DeepSeek and Meta AI. Free means capped, not crippled.

Which AI is best for writing and coding?
On the checked-date comparisons, Claude leads for writing quality and long documents, with ChatGPT the broader generalist. Both are $20/mo paid (Claude $17 annual) with usable free tiers.

What’s the cheapest paid AI plan?
Google’s Gemini AI Plus at $4.99/mo is the cheapest sensible paid step in the industry as of 12 Sep 2026; Perplexity’s student plan ($4.99 with verification) matches it for students.

Is the $20/month AI tier worth it?
If you’ve hit the free caps repeatedly in real work — yes, it buys cap relief where the work happens. If you can’t name the task that ran out, stay free another month.

Which AI shows its sources?
Perplexity is built around inline citations; that’s its core differentiator at $20/mo (or $200/yr).
