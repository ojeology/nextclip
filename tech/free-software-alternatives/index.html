Free alternatives to expensive software that people don't know exist

By the BRYME Tech desk · published 2026-09-09

In one line: The desk's own canon — photo, audio, video, office, passwords and messaging — every one opened and used, not scraped from a listicle.

There's a tier of free software that quietly outclasses the subscriptions people assume they need — not "free trials", not crippled demos: finished, maintained, sometimes open-source tools that professionals genuinely use. This desk has reviewed and compared them first-hand across a whole shelf; this page is the single map of the canon, category by category.

Photos: you may not need that subscription

Photopea (the full first-hand comparison is here) is a browser-based editor that opens Photoshop's own file formats and covers most of what most people ever did in Photoshop — for free. And the ground shifted again when Affinity went free (what that actually includes), putting a professional-grade creative suite in everyone's hands. For quick edits and collabs, our Pixlr-vs-Canva and Polotno comparisons sort the lightweights.

Office: LibreOffice, and the free web suites

LibreOffice is the open-source office suite that reads and writes Word, Excel and PowerPoint formats — free, offline, no account, no telemetry. Combined with Google Docs and Microsoft's free web Office, the honest position is that paying for office software is now a choice about features and ecosystem, not access (which suite fits which workflow is its own comparison).

Video and audio: the professional free tier is real

DaVinci Resolve — Blackmagic's professional editor and colour grader — ships a genuinely usable free version that's not a watermark demo. OBS Studio, open-source, is the streaming and screen-recording standard that paid competitors reskin. Audacity remains the audio editor equivalent of a hammer: unfashionable, effective, everywhere.

Passwords and messaging: the security canon

Bitwarden (reviewed honestly here) free tier covers unlimited passwords on unlimited devices — the "free password manager can't be trusted" take never survived contact with it (manager vs browser-built-in, compared). Signal (the privacy comparison) is the messaging app whose encryption protocol the commercial apps adopted.

Workspaces and AI: the free floors are higher than advertised

Notion's free plan (what it includes and withholds) runs a personal workspace indefinitely, and the AI assistants' free tiers have become useful daily tools (where each free tier actually ends). And when a "free" comparison itself is trying to sell you something, that's the tell — we've caught listicle-shaped marketing on this desk before.

The rule that keeps this honest

Free software isn't a charity; it's three business models — open source (community or foundation-funded), freemium (the free tier is the advertisement), and platform loss-leaders (Google wants you in the ecosystem). Knowing which one you're using tells you what changes when it doesn't: open source survives its company; freemium tightens; loss-leaders pivot. Choose accordingly for the tools your work depends on — and check this shelf first, because every recommendation on it was opened, used and argued with before it earned a link.
