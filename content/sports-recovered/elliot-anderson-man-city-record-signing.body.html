Elliot Anderson, Explained: Man City’s £135m Man

Rodri left for Barcelona; City answered with a reported £135m for Elliot Anderson. What he brings, what the fee means, and why Friday at Palace matters.

BRYME Editorial2026-08-255 min read

Why City paid a reported £135m

When Rodri left for Barcelona this summer, Manchester City lost the single most important structural midfielder in world football. Elliot Anderson — signed from Nottingham Forest for a reported fee around £135m — is the man tasked with replacing the irreplaceable.

What City actually bought

Anderson’s game is built for Pep-style control: he receives under pressure, carries the ball through lines rather than around them, and covers ground on both sides of the ball. At Forest he was the engine of their midfield; at City the ask is different — tempo, positioning, and the discipline to be the floor of the team rather than its firework.

The fee in context

The summer’s midfield market went nuclear: Morgan Rogers to Chelsea for a reported £117m, Sandro Tonali to Spurs around £108m, Mateus Fernandes close to £99m. Anderson’s deal tops them all — which means the scrutiny will too. City also reportedly continue to shape the room around him, with Lille’s Ayyoub Bouaddi discussed as the next midfield addition.

Early evidence

Matchweek 1 was a promising start rather than a statement: City left it late against Bournemouth, winning 2–1 without ever fully controlling the tempo the way Rodri’s teams used to. The real examination begins Friday night at Crystal Palace — a hostile ground, a deep block, and the first proper look at whether the new midfield can impose a rhythm away from home.
