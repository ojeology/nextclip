Literary Hub submissions: how to pitch Lit Hub

Official pitch guidance checked 9 October 2026. BRYME is independent of Literary Hub.

Email a 2–3 paragraph pitch, or a finished essay with a brief description, to info@lithub.com. Literary Hub wants stories about literary life and culture: criticism, craft, reported literary issues and personal essays connected to wider questions about writing. Its pitch page does not state a payment rate.

Read Literary Hub’s official How To Pitch page before sending. Despite its publication of fiction and poetry elsewhere on the site, this pitch route does not accept original fiction or poetry. Interview pitches are also excluded in the current guidance.

What does Lit Hub want?

The editors describe an interest in how books and their authors connect to the wider culture. That includes criticism written for a general audience, essays on craft and reported pieces about literary issues. A personal experience can work when it opens into a larger literary question rather than remaining only a private anecdote.

The official page also invites stories about upcoming nonfiction books when the writer contributes analysis or reporting on the broader issues around them. Merely summarising a book is not the same as proposing that additional perspective.

BRYME’s preparation advice: identify the literary subject, the wider question and what you can contribute that a publisher’s description cannot. Read recent work to judge the audience and tone. Do not imitate another writer’s wording or treat a topical book release as a complete angle by itself.

A checklist for the 2–3 paragraph pitch

  1. Name the proposed story. Make its central idea clear without a long introduction about yourself.
  2. Explain why it matters now. The editors explicitly ask why the story is interesting and timely.
  3. Say what you bring. Identify your analysis, perspective or reporting, rather than promising only enthusiasm for the subject.
  4. Give a sense of style and tone. The pitch is also a sample of how you write.
  5. Include previous-work links if you have them. The official wording makes this conditional; it does not say every applicant must already have clips.

A finished essay can be sent instead, with a brief description in the email. The pitch page does not publish a finished-essay word limit, so do not confuse the 2–3 paragraph pitch instruction with the length of the eventual piece.

What not to send through this route

  • Original poetry or fiction.
  • Interview pitches: the checked page says these are not being accepted at this time.
  • Pieces written with generative AI.

Those are the publication’s stated exclusions. BRYME’s advice is to draft the submission in your own words and not use a generated sample as a ready-to-send pitch.

Payment, response time and rights

No rate is published on the checked pitch page. This is not proof that the publication does not pay. It means a specific dollar figure cannot be verified from this source. Confirm the fee and payment terms before accepting a commission.

Lit Hub says it reads everything it receives, but it does not give a reply deadline or promise a response to every submission on that page. “We read everything” should not be converted into a guaranteed reply. The checked guidance also does not establish rights terms, simultaneous-submission rules or a country-eligibility list.

Ask about any term that would change your decision to pitch. In particular, clarify exclusivity and rights before placing the same finished essay elsewhere. There is no official two-week rejection rule in the source checked.

Before you press send

Make sure your email goes to the address on the official pitch page, that your draft is accessible and that your short description explains the idea rather than only introducing the attachment. BRYME has researched this opportunity but its existing record does not claim a successful submission or confirmed payment.

For general structure, use our pitch-writing guide. Compare Noema’s longer argument-led pitch requirements or The Drift’s nonfiction pitch process only when the work fits those publications.
