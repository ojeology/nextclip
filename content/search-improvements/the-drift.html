The Drift submissions: pay rates, pitches and section inboxes

Official submission guidance checked 9 October 2026. BRYME is an independent guide.

The Drift lists $2,000 for essays, $500–$1,000 for short stories, $150 for poems and $25 for Mentions. Nonfiction begins with one 2–4 paragraph pitch at a time to pitches@thedriftmag.com. Do not send a completed nonfiction draft: its editors say they cannot read draft nonfiction submissions.

Read the official submission instructions and rates. Fiction, poetry and Mentions have separate inboxes and preparation requirements; sending everything to the essay-pitch address is not the correct approach.

How to pitch an essay to The Drift

Send a 2–4 paragraph pitch, one at a time. The Drift says it develops essays over many months and drafts and wants to start with the proposal, not a completed manuscript. It describes its interests as sharp, unexpected interventions in culture, literature and politics, including socially engaged criticism.

BRYME’s preparation advice: state the assumption your piece challenges, the argument you intend to make and the evidence or reporting that would sustain it. A large theme alone is not yet an essay proposal. Read the magazine’s list of subjects and approaches it does not want before investing time in a pitch.

The editors say they read and respond to every pitch, but warn that it can take time. That is a response commitment without a fixed deadline—not evidence that silence after two weeks means rejection.

Fiction, poetry and Mentions: different instructions

Short fiction

Send the work to the fiction inbox as a Word document or PDF named lastname_firstname. The official fiction instructions state no word limit. Do not generalise that statement into “no length rules for every section.”

Poetry

Attach up to six poems in one Word document or PDF named lastname_firstname. The Drift asks poets to submit no more than twice per year. This limit refers to poetry submissions, not the number of poems it will publish.

Mentions

The magazine describes Mentions as 150-word reviews. Its submission instructions ask for a topic in the subject line and, in the email body, your name, relevant clips and a draft of 1–4 sentences. A clear, narrow angle, humour and an effective final line are part of its stated preferences. Use the Mentions inbox rather than sending these as essay pitches.

What The Drift does not want

The official page excludes personal essays and anything written by AI. It also rejects straightforward longform reviews narrowly focused on one book or film, alongside a longer list of overused approaches and subjects. Check that full list: the brief summary here cannot replace it.

This updates an earlier BRYME record that marked the AI policy as unstated. The official source now provides a clear restriction. A generated pitch or essay is not an appropriate workaround.

What still needs agreement?

The checked page does not settle the full contract terms, payment schedule, rights or simultaneous-submission policy. Nor does a lack of a stated country restriction establish payment availability in every country. Clarify practical terms with the editor before committing to a commission.

Prepare the right submission

Use our pitch guide for general organisation, then follow The Drift’s section-specific rules. For a different fit, compare Literary Hub’s literary-culture essays and Noema’s argument-led longform guidance. They have different requirements; do not send an identical template to all three.
