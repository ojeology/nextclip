A polished chart is not evidence that the firm behind it is authorised, that an order will fill at the shown price or that a withdrawal will work. Compare the legal provider, the account contract and the software separately. This is a worksheet, not a ranking: BRYME has not paid for, tested or endorsed a broker in this guide.

1. Confirm who provides the account

Find the legal entity in the agreement and check it independently with the appropriate regulator before entering personal or payment details. Compare permissions for the actual product and your country, not merely a logo or a global group name. Use our US/UK/CA/AU verification steps; a firm's presence on a list does not promise safety or returns. When a platform is a software vendor rather than the broker, identify the broker as well.

2. Price your own use case

Choose one instrument, likely position size, holding period and account currency. Record the bid-ask spread at the times you would realistically transact, commission both ways, any data or platform subscription, FX conversion, financing or margin interest, inactivity or withdrawal fees and potential tax reporting exports. A 'zero commission' banner does not make the spread free. Compare the round-trip fee worksheet and ask whether the published rate is a minimum, an average or a promotional quote.

3. Test the controls, not a screenshot

  • Check whether the app offers the order types you need and explains what triggers a stop. Read the rules for after-hours orders, time-in-force, cancellation and partial fills; see execution mechanics.
  • Confirm whether a quote is live or delayed and how the chart's price relates to an executable bid and ask.
  • Try a demo for navigation and record keeping, but do not treat demo fills as proof of live performance. Look for transaction confirmations and a downloadable record of fills and charges.
  • Check that account login supports strong authentication and that withdrawal instructions are documented. Never share one-time codes or install remote-access software on demand.
  • Ask support a concrete question about a fee or rejected order. A quick answer to a sales query is not a substitute for written contract terms.

4. Reject unanswerable comparisons

Do not score providers by a promised return, an influencer's referral bonus or an unverified 'best' badge. A platform can be easy to use yet unsuitable for the product you want; an authorised entity can still have high costs or poor execution. If you cannot find the current agreement and a regulator record matching the exact account entity, leave the result 'not checked', not 'passed'. The SEC's brokerage account bulletin lists account costs to ask about, while its online investing guide advises verifying whether an order or cancellation actually completed.
