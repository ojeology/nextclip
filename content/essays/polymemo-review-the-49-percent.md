Somebody hands you a genuinely new idea. Readers who want to read your article in Turkish pay for the translation themselves, and in exchange they earn a cut of everyone who reads that Turkish version afterwards. You write once. The audience translates you into the world.

That is Polymemo, and I have not seen the model anywhere else. Its press release has been syndicated across half a dozen trade wires with the same three sentences and the same headline number: **authors keep 70%**.

I read the terms of service instead. The 70% is real. It is also not what you get.

## What Polymemo is

Polymemo is a multilingual content platform run by **Polymemo Co., Ltd.**, registered at 3-33 Komukainishimachi, Saiwai-ku, Kawasaki, Kanagawa, Japan. Its terms were established on **22 April 2026**. iOS and Android apps followed in May. The Android app was last updated on 29 August 2026.

Everything runs on points. **One point equals one US cent**, fixed in the terms.

The platform is about four and a half months old.

## Why writers are paying attention

Three reasons, and they are legitimate.

**The translation model is actually novel.** On every other platform, going multilingual is a cost you carry. Here it is a cost your readers carry, because they want to read you.

**Negative pricing exists.** Authors can set a viewing fee anywhere from 1 to 300 points — or from **−1 to −300 points**, meaning you pay readers to read you. As a promotional mechanism that is a genuinely interesting inversion.

**The company is not hiding.** Named legal entity, physical address, published phone number, 18 articles of detailed terms, Japanese law and Tokyo District Court jurisdiction. That is more than most platforms in this space offer.

## How it works

1. You post. It costs **25 points (~$0.25)**. Your title is auto-translated into roughly 200 languages. Your body text is not.
2. Readers browsing in their own language find your title. To read the body, someone has to pay for the translation.
3. That reader becomes a **translation investor**. They pay characters × 0.002 points — about **10 points ($0.10)** for a 5,000-character article.
4. Once translated, everyone who reads it in that language pays your viewing fee.
5. Revenue splits: **author 70%, translation investor 20%, platform 10%.**

### A worked example

You post a 5,000-character essay and set the viewing fee at 100 points ($1.00).

- Posting costs you 25 points.
- A reader in Poland pays 10 points to translate it.
- Fifty Polish readers then read it, paying 100 points each — 5,000 points total.
- Your 70% share: **3,500 points**, face value $35.
- The translation investor's 20%: 1,000 points, on a 10-point outlay. Good return, if the readers arrive.

So far this looks fine. Now the part nobody writes about.

## The 49%

**Article 7 of the terms of service** governs cashing out. Three clauses matter:

> Minimum redemption amount: 5,000 points
> Redemption rate: 70% (5,000 points = US$35.00)
> Redemption is conducted via the PayPal Payouts API

Read that again against the platform's own definition of a point. Five thousand points are worth **$50** at 1pt = $0.01. Redemption returns **$35**.

That is a **second 30% cut**, taken at the door on the way out, after the platform has already taken 10% on the way in.

Compound them:

| Reader pays 100 points ($1.00) | |
|---|---|
| Platform takes 10% | −10 pt |
| Translation investor takes 20% | −20 pt |
| You are credited 70% | 70 pt · face value $0.70 |
| You redeem at 70% | **$0.49** |

**About 49 cents of every reader dollar reaches you as cash.** Where no translation investor is involved you keep more of the points, but the 30% redemption haircut still applies to all of it.

To reach the 5,000-point minimum, readers must spend roughly **7,143 points ($71.43)** on your work. You receive **$35.00**.

Polymemo is not lying about this. Every figure is published in its own binding terms, findable in about ninety seconds. But no press release states it, no trade write-up mentions it, and a writer reading the coverage would reasonably conclude they receive 70 cents on the dollar. They receive about 49.

## Fees and costs, in full

Nothing here is hidden by Polymemo — it is simply never collected in one place.

| Like | 2 pt (1 pt back to the creator) |
| Direct message | 2 pt per message |
| Group chat | 1 pt per message |
| **AI chat** | **10 pt per message** ($0.10) |
| Translation investment | characters × 0.002 pt |
| Premium | $9.99/month or $99.99/year |
| Team plan | $49.99/month, 5 members |
| Business | $199.99/month, 20 members |
| Enterprise | $999.99/month, 100 members |

You get **50 points free** at registration — two posts — and 10 points per referral.

**Two things worth flagging.** Purchased and free points are **non-refundable** and can never be cashed out; only points you earn from activity are redeemable. And **negative pricing — the headline feature in every press release — requires Premium**, at $9.99/month.

## Translation and international reach

Translation runs on the **Google Translation API** plus AI translation. Article 11.2 of the terms is an explicit disclaimer: results are "for reference only" and are not guaranteed to be accurate or fit for purpose.

The language count is inconsistent across the platform's own surfaces — the Google Play description says **244 languages**, the website says **200+**, the terms say "approximately 200." A small thing, but it is the kind of imprecision worth noticing.

The important limitation: **"200+ languages" is a capability, not distribution.** Only your *title* is auto-translated. Nobody reads your article in Japanese until a Japanese reader pays to translate it. If nobody does, your piece exists in exactly one language.

## How payments work, and who can actually get paid

Money in and money out both run entirely through **PayPal**. Points are bought via PayPal; redemption uses the **PayPal Payouts API**, processing in 2–5 business days.

This is where a global readership needs to pay attention, because the answer differs sharply by country.

**If you are in the US, UK, Canada, Australia or most of the EU:** PayPal Payouts should reach you without difficulty. The 49% arithmetic is your main concern, not access.

**If you are elsewhere:** check before you write anything. PayPal Payouts is a mass-payment product with its own country eligibility, distinct from ordinary consumer PayPal. **Polymemo publishes no payout country list.** The Google Play description claims "PayPal in 200+ countries lets you buy and withdraw points from anywhere" — that is a platform claim, and it papers over the difference between buying and withdrawing.

**Nigeria specifically:** registering and publishing look fine — nothing in the terms restricts by country. Cashing out is doubtful. Current sources indicate Nigerian personal PayPal accounts remain send-only; other sources claim a 2026 partnership has changed this. The evidence conflicts, and Payouts is a different product again. A Nigerian writer should verify personally before accumulating any balance. The same caution applies to India, Pakistan, Bangladesh and much of sub-Saharan Africa.

## Realistic earning expectations

Here is the honest position: **nobody knows, because there is no evidence anyone has been paid.**

I searched for independent accounts of Polymemo earnings — Reddit, YouTube, blogs, forums, payout screenshots. I found none. Every substantive description of Polymemo online traces back to the same company press release.

That is not an accusation. The platform is four months old with a 5,000-point redemption floor. It is entirely possible nobody has reached the threshold yet. But it means the earning proposition is **completely untested**, and you would be among the first to test it.

What we can say from the structure:

- You need readers to spend **$71.43** on your work to withdraw **$35**.
- Readers must pay to read you, on a platform with no demonstrated paying readership.
- The tool for overcoming that — negative pricing — costs $9.99/month.
- Google Play shows **50,000+ downloads and no star rating at all**, which means too few ratings to display one. Downloads are not readers, and a 50K-install app with no visible rating suggests low engagement.

## Advantages

- A genuinely original economic model, not a Medium clone
- Transparent, specific, numerically explicit terms — better than most competitors
- **You keep your copyright.** The licence to Polymemo is explicitly non-exclusive (Article 10)
- **No exclusivity requirement** — publish the same work elsewhere
- Points never expire while your account is open
- Real company, real address, real jurisdiction, actively maintained apps

## Disadvantages

- The effective cash rate is ~49%, not 70%
- 5,000-point minimum before you can withdraw anything
- You pay to publish
- Negative pricing is paywalled behind Premium
- Purchased and free points can never be cashed out
- AI chat at 10 points a message is expensive to use casually
- Machine translation with an accuracy disclaimer, published under your name in languages you cannot check

## Risks to understand

**No proven payout record.** For an income platform this is the entire question, and it is unanswered.

**Audience risk.** Readers must pay before reading you. On a young platform that is a steep barrier.

**Your points vanish if you leave.** Article 13.4: on account deletion, unused points expire.

**Redemption is discretionary.** Article 7.6 lets the company hold or cancel a redemption request on reasonable grounds. Standard anti-fraud language — but untested.

**No AI-content policy exists.** Article 12's prohibited-actions list contains no clause about AI-generated writing at all. For an AI-heavy platform in 2026, that gap is conspicuous, and it may change without much notice.

**Jurisdiction.** Any dispute goes to the Tokyo District Court under Japanese law. Over $35, that is not a practical remedy for anyone.

**Terms can change unilaterally.** Article 17: revisions take effect on posting, and continued use is deemed acceptance.

## Is it legitimate?

**Yes, on the evidence available — and that is a narrower statement than it sounds.**

What is verified: a real registered Japanese company with a published address and phone number; live apps on both stores; 18 articles of detailed terms under a named jurisdiction; authors retain copyright; the fee and revenue-share structure is disclosed precisely.

Publishing your own unflattering redemption rate in your terms is not what a fraud does. A scam obscures the exit; Polymemo documents it.

What is **not** verified: that anyone has been paid. That there is a readership. That your country can receive a payout. Those are the questions that decide whether it works for you, and none of them can be answered today.

**"Legitimate company" and "reliable income" are different claims.** Polymemo has earned the first. It has not yet had the chance to earn the second.

## Is it worth trying?

**As an experiment, if you enjoy being early: yes, cautiously.** It costs $0.25 a post and you start with 50 free points. Publish two or three pieces, see whether anyone translates them, and treat any earnings as a bonus.

**As an income plan: no.** Not yet. A single commission from an established magazine — Pellicle pays £375 for a feature, Geist up to CAD$650 — will beat months of Polymemo unless the platform grows enormously. BRYME lists [138 verified paying publications](/writing/) with the rate stated on each.

**Rules if you do try it:**

- Do not buy points hoping to prime the pump. Purchased points can never be cashed out.
- Do not accumulate a large balance before testing a small withdrawal — if you can reach the threshold, cash out and find out whether it works.
- Never make it your only home for a piece. There is no exclusivity requirement, so publish elsewhere too.
- Check whether PayPal Payouts reaches your country **before** you write anything.

## FAQ

**Is Polymemo free?** Free to join, with 50 points at signup. Publishing costs 25 points a post, so it is not free to use.

**How much do writers actually make?** Unknown. No independent earnings evidence exists.

**What is a translation investor?** A reader who pays your translation cost up front and then earns 20% of that version's viewing revenue. It is not a regulated investment and there is no principal protection.

**Do I keep my copyright?** Yes. Article 10.1. The licence you grant is non-exclusive.

**Can I publish the same piece elsewhere?** Yes — no exclusivity is required.

**Can I use AI to write on Polymemo?** The terms say nothing either way. There is no published AI policy.

**How long do withdrawals take?** 2–5 business days after a redemption request, subject to the 5,000-point minimum.

**Is Polymemo a scam?** No evidence suggests so. But "not a scam" is not the same as "will pay you."

## Final verdict

Polymemo is the most interesting idea in online publishing I have looked at this year, attached to economics that its own coverage has consistently misrepresented by omission.

The reader-funded translation model deserves to work. The company is real and unusually transparent. And the numbers, once you compound them, mean **roughly half of what a reader spends on your writing reaches you as money** — with no public evidence yet that anyone has completed that journey.

Try it as an experiment if the idea excites you. Do not plan around it. And read Article 7 before you write anything, because it is the only part of Polymemo that nobody else will tell you about.
