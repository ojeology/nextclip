Every other platform in this series decides whether your country is allowed to receive money. Substack and Medium route through Stripe. Polymemo routes through PayPal. Ream covers 46 countries.

Stacker News routes through bitcoin, and bitcoin does not have a country list.

That single fact makes it worth understanding even if you never use it — because it is the only working counter-example to a problem the rest of the industry treats as unsolvable.

## What Stacker News is

Stacker News is a link-sharing and discussion site, closer in shape to Hacker News than to Medium, where the votes are money. Instead of an upvote, readers send you **sats** — small units of bitcoin — over the Lightning Network. These payments are called **zaps**.

The subject matter skews heavily toward bitcoin, economics, technology and adjacent territory. It is not a general-interest writing platform and does not pretend to be.

## Why it is different

**A vote costs the voter something.** The platform's own reasoning, stated in its FAQ: *"Sending someone a like or an upvote incurs no cost to you, and therefore these metrics can easily be gamed by bots. Sending someone sats incurs a direct cost to you, which gives the recipient a meaningful reward and acts as a clear signal."*

That is the whole design. Money as a ranking signal, because money resists bots in a way clicks do not.

**You pay to post.** A small fee in sats per post and per comment, varying by community. Same logic — spam gets expensive.

**Nobody approves your country.** You attach a Lightning wallet and receive payments. There is no onboarding country list, no Stripe eligibility, no payout jurisdiction.

## How the money works

Stacker News publishes its take rate plainly, which very few platforms do. From the FAQ:

> **A 100-sat zap: 70 sats to you, 21 sats to the territory founder, 9 sats to the daily rewards pool.**

So the platform layer takes **30%**, and the FAQ explains why: *"We take 30% of zaps to protect against sybil attacks."* Making zaps costly is what makes them a usable quality signal.

Whether 30% is fair is a judgement call. That it is published, itemised and justified is more than most competitors manage.

**Four ways to earn:**

1. **Zaps** — peer-to-peer, requires an attached Lightning wallet
2. **Daily rewards** — the site redistributes its revenue to the day's best contributors
3. **Referrals**
4. **Founding a territory** — founders take 70% of posting and boost fees in their territory, plus 21% of zaps within it

## The catch you must understand first

**If you have not attached a Lightning wallet, you do not earn bitcoin. You earn Cowboy Credits.**

Cowboy Credits (CCs) are the platform's onboarding mechanism. They spend on-site 1:1 for posts, comments, zaps and boosts — but per the FAQ, they **cannot be withdrawn**.

This is disclosed, and the intent is benign: it lets newcomers participate before setting up a wallet. But the practical effect is that **a writer who never attaches a wallet is accumulating store credit, not income.**

If you try Stacker News, attach a wallet first. Everything before that is unpaid.

## What writers actually earn

**Unmeasured — and the most honest available assessment is discouraging.**

I could not find reliable earnings data. The most candid account I located came from a working writer on the platform itself, describing zap income as:

> "peanuts/cherries on top, compared to the major revenue flow of writing"

— which for them came from editors commissioning their work.

Another commenter on the same thread noted earning $5 over roughly ten years of conventional blogging, for context on how low the bar is elsewhere.

**Set expectations accordingly.** Stacker News is a small community. Zaps are micropayments — tens or hundreds of sats, often worth cents. Nobody has published evidence of a writer earning a living, and nothing about the design suggests they would.

## Who it actually suits

**Good fit:** you already write about bitcoin, economics, technology, privacy or adjacent subjects. You want a technically literate audience that argues back. You are outside the countries Stripe and PayPal serve and want to see what permissionless payment feels like.

**Poor fit:** you write fiction, lifestyle, general interest or anything outside the community's subjects. You need predictable income. You are uncomfortable holding bitcoin.

## Advantages

- **No country gatekeeping.** The only platform here where this is true
- Take rate published and justified — 70/21/9
- No bank, no Stripe, no PayPal, no KYC gate described for basic use
- Payments settle in seconds
- Pay-to-post keeps quality unusually high and spam low
- Small, engaged, genuinely argumentative audience
- Four distinct earning routes, not just one

## Disadvantages

- **Earnings are tiny and unmeasured**
- Narrow subject range — most writing does not fit
- Cowboy Credits cannot be withdrawn
- Requires a Lightning wallet, which is real friction for non-technical writers
- Bitcoin price volatility applies to anything you hold
- 30% platform take
- Posts lose visibility after about a day
- Local regulatory treatment of bitcoin is your problem, not theirs

## Risks

**Volatility.** Sats are bitcoin. Convert promptly if you need a stable value.

**Regulation.** Receiving bitcoin has different tax and legal treatment in every country. Nothing here is advice — check your own position.

**Self-custody.** A Lightning wallet is your responsibility. There is no support desk to restore lost funds.

**Concentration.** A small community means a small ceiling. If the community shrinks, so does everything.

## Is it legitimate?

**Yes, with the earnings question left open.**

Verified: the platform operates publicly, publishes its fee structure numerically, explains its reasoning, and the founder restates the split in dated public comments. Payments are on a public network — you can see them settle. There is no custody of your funds beyond your balance, no payout approval process, and no country eligibility to fail.

What is unverified is whether meaningful money is available. The available evidence says small.

⚠️ **One practical warning:** do not confuse **stacker.news** with **stacker.com**, an unrelated American content-syndication business that appears in the same searches.

## Is it worth trying?

**If you write in its subjects: yes, as a supplement.** It costs almost nothing, payment is immediate, and the feedback is unusually direct — people paying to respond to you is a stronger signal than any like count.

**As income: no.** Treat zaps as cherries on top, in the platform's own writer's phrase.

**If you are locked out of Stripe and PayPal: try it for the education alone.** Understanding how a permissionless payment rail works is worth more than the sats. The wall that blocks writers in most of the world from Substack, Medium, Ream and Polymemo does not exist here — and knowing that a functioning alternative exists changes what you accept from everyone else.

## FAQ

**Do I need bitcoin to start?** No. You can earn Cowboy Credits from zaps and use them for fees. But CCs cannot be withdrawn.

**How much does Stacker News take?** 30% of every zap — 21% to the territory founder, 9% to the daily rewards pool.

**Does it cost money to post?** Yes, a small fee in sats, varying by territory.

**Can I get paid from any country?** Effectively yes, on the payment rail. Your local legal and tax treatment of bitcoin is a separate question.

**How much can I earn?** Very little on the available evidence. No reliable data exists.

**Can I write about anything?** Technically yes. Practically, the audience is bitcoin, tech and economics.

## Final verdict

Stacker News is a small, honest, technically interesting platform that will not pay your rent and does not claim it will.

Its real significance is as a proof of concept. Every other platform in this series has a country list, and writers in most of the world are on the wrong side of it. Stacker News shows what it looks like when the payment layer simply does not ask where you live.

For the right writer it is a genuine supplement. For everyone else it is the clearest available demonstration that the payout wall is a business decision, not a law of nature.
