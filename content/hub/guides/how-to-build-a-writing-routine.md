Talent gets you started; a routine gets you published. Writing a little every day beats writing a lot once in a while, because the habit is what compounds. The trick is to make it small enough to keep.

## What a routine is

A routine is a fixed time, place, and starting move that signals "now I write." The goal is not to write great work every day — it is to write regularly enough that the great work has a chance to happen.

## How to build one

**Start embarrassingly small.** Ten minutes, or one sentence. A routine you keep is worth more than a two-hour plan you abandon. You can always build from small.

**Pick a time and anchor it.** Attach writing to an existing habit — after your morning coffee, right before bed, after lunch. Anchoring to something you already do makes it automatic.

**Set a consistent place.** A desk, a corner, a café. Your brain learns: this spot, this habit, I write.

**Have a clear starting move.** Deciding the whole piece is too big. Decide the first line, or the section you will work on. "I will write one sentence about the part I understand" is a real start.

**Write for the timer, not a goal.** A writing timer keeps you from negotiating with yourself. Ten minutes of just writing is a win.

**Forgive missed days.** One missed day is not a broken habit. The habit is made by returning, not by being perfect. Miss twice? Just start again.

**End with a note for next time.** Leave the next sentence or a thought so starting again is easy. "Park on the edge" makes tomorrow's session effortless.

## How to keep it going

**Track it loosely.** A simple tally or a streak, but do not let a break feel like failure.

**Make it the same small thing.** Do not scale the habit up before it is steady. Consistency first, then volume.

**Remove friction.** Have your document open, your notes ready, your spot clear. Fewer obstacles, more writing.

## Common mistakes

Planning a two-hour daily block and quitting at day three. Waiting for inspiration. Making the routine about quality instead of showing up. Punishing a missed day so hard you quit. Changing the routine every week.

## Practical tips

- Ten minutes daily beats two hours weekly.
- Anchor writing to a habit you already keep.
- On hard days, write one sentence — the streak is the point.
