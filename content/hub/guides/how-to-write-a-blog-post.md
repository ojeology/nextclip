A blog post is not an essay. Readers skim, click on a headline, scan the headings, and decide in seconds whether it is worth their time. Write for that reader.

## What it is

A blog post is a comparatively short piece of non-fiction published online, usually on a specific topic, written to be readable and scannable.

## Why it is used

It shares knowledge, builds an audience, and ranks in search, with very little cost or gatekeeping. For many writers it is the first place they publish and build a portfolio.

## Who uses it

Bloggers, companies, specialists, and freelancers. Almost anyone who wants to be found by people looking for information.

## Typical structure

1. **Headline** — specific and promising, not clever for its own sake.
2. **Introduction** — the promise: what this post does for the reader.
3. **Body** — short sections with clear headings, each with one job.
4. **Conclusion** — what to do next, or a summary.
5. **Optional extras** — a table of contents, bullet lists, and a call to action.

## How to write it

**Know the single thing this post does.** "How to write an introduction that keeps readers" beats "writing tips."

**Pick a headline that states the outcome.** Readers click when they see what they will get. Include "how to," a number, or a clear benefit.

**Open with the promise.** In the first few lines, tell the reader what they will learn and that it is worth their time.

**Use headings to carry the structure.** A reader who only reads your headings should roughly get the gist.

**Write short paragraphs and use lists.** Walls of text lose online readers. Bullets and short paragraphs help them skim.

**Be specific and useful.** Concrete examples, steps, and numbers outperform vague generalities.

**Close with a next step.** A takeaway, a question, a summary, or a clear call to action.

## Common mistakes

Writing a headline that over-promises. Burying the point in a long introduction. One giant block of text. Being so generic that anyone could have written it. Ending with nothing but tailwind.

## Checklist

- [ ] My headline states a specific outcome.
- [ ] The introduction promises something the post delivers.
- [ ] I used headings so the reader can skim.
- [ ] I wrote short paragraphs and used lists.
- [ ] I was specific and original, not generic.
- [ ] I ended with a clear next step.
