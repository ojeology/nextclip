Financial writing pays more than any other copywriting specialism in the UK survey data, and it stays that way year after year for a reason most writers never test: the barrier is not talent, it is a willingness to learn rules that are genuinely tedious.

## What it pays

From market analyses built on the **ProCopywriters survey of 474 UK freelance copywriters**:

| Specialism | Day rate |
|---|---|
| **Financial services and fintech** | **£600–£850** |
| Technology and software | £450–£750 |
| Overall UK average, all copywriting | £480 |
| Charity and non-profit | £250–£375 |

From the **EFA 2026 Rate Chart** (1,100+ US respondents), the nearest comparable categories run 32¢–87.5¢ a word for business and marketing writing, with technical at 87.5¢–$1.08.

A financial specialist at £600–£850 sits at or above the **top decile** of UK freelancers generally, which sits around £708 a day. That is not seniority. That is subject.

## Why it pays that much

**Regulation.** Financial promotions are legally constrained. In the UK the requirement that communications be **fair, clear and not misleading** is a regulatory obligation with consequences, not a style preference. A writer who understands that is not interchangeable with a cheaper one.

**Compliance sign-off is the real deliverable.** In most sectors the client approves the copy. In financial services, a compliance team does. Copy that passes first time saves days of revision cycles, and firms know exactly what those days cost.

**The subject genuinely repels most writers.** Pension drawdown, indemnity limits, capital adequacy, KIIDs. That is precisely why supply is thin.

**The decisions are large.** A mortgage, an annuity, a pension transfer. Copy that clarifies a high-value decision has measurable commercial worth.

## What the work actually is

**Product and policy copy** — explaining what something covers, in language that survives both compliance and the customer.

**Customer communications** — renewal letters, statements, regulatory notifications. Unglamorous, endless, reliably paid.

**Educational content** — the "what is an ISA" material. Usually the entry point, because it is the least regulated part of the estate.

**Fintech and app copy** — onboarding flows, in-product messaging, UX copy. Fastest-growing and closest to conventional tech work.

**B2B and intermediary material** — case studies, sector guides, adviser-facing content.

**Investor communications** — annual reports, shareholder letters. High-stakes, well paid, tightly controlled.

## How to break in without a finance background

**Learn one product properly.** Not "finance" — one product. Cash ISAs, or income protection, or SIPPs, or business current accounts. Read the provider documentation and the regulator's own consumer guidance. A focused week puts you ahead of most generalists.

**Understand the compliance frame before you pitch.** You do not need to be a compliance officer. You do need to know that promotions must be fair, clear and not misleading; that risk warnings are mandatory rather than decorative; and that past-performance claims are tightly restricted. Demonstrating that awareness in a first conversation is disproportionately persuasive, because it signals you will not create work.

**Rewrite something genuinely bad.** Financial documents are famously impenetrable, which makes them ideal sample material. Take a real public product summary and rewrite it clearly, **keeping every material fact and risk warning intact**. That single sample proves the exact skill the sector buys.

**Target the mid-market first.** The largest institutions use agency rosters. Building societies, brokers, IFAs, challenger banks and fintechs hire freelancers directly and have real budgets.

**Read the regulator, not the blogs.** In the UK that means the FCA's own materials. Elsewhere, your national equivalent. It is dry and it is the moat.

## What to expect that you will not enjoy

**Slow revision cycles.** Sign-off passes through people whose job is to say no.
**Constrained creativity.** Much of it is careful and heavily reviewed.
**Real responsibility.** Misleading copy about a financial product can cause genuine harm, and that is the reason the rate exists.

If that trade is acceptable, it is the most reliable high day rate available to a writer.

## Price it against the specialism, not your CV

The £600–£850 band attaches to the subject. If you can do the work, you are pricing into that band, not into the junior one.

Work out your own floor with the [freelance rate calculator](/tools/freelance-rate-calculator/), then compare. Most writers discover their instinctive quote sits far below what the survey says the market pays — and in this specialism the gap is widest.
