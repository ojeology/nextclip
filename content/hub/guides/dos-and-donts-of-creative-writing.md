Creative writing — fiction, poetry, memoir — lives on detail, voice and specificity. These dos and don'ts cover the craft choices that make a piece read like real writing.

## Do

- **Do show, don't tell.** "She was scared" tells. "Her hands shook as she turned the lock" shows. Let the reader feel it through action.
- **Do use specific detail.** Concrete, specific images make fiction feel true. "A blue mug" beats "a cup"; "the smell of rain on hot pavement" beats "it felt humid."
- **Do build a real character.** Give characters a want, a flaw, and a voice. A character with a desire and a problem drives a story.
- **Do write dialogue with subtext.** People in real life rarely say exactly what they mean. Characters should too — what they say and what they mean can differ.
- **Do trust the reader.** Leave room for the reader to infer. Over-explaining kills a story's power.
- **Do revise.** Creative writing is rewriting. The magic is usually in the draft after the first one.

## Don't

- **Don't tell the reader the point.** A short story that states its theme ("and that is when I learned to forgive") loses its effect. Draw the lesson through the story.
- **Don't use clichés.** "It was raining cats and dogs," "her heart was racing." Avoid phrases you have heard a hundred times. Find fresh language.
- **Don't over-adjectivise.** A string of adjectives ("the dark, stormy, dangerous night") weakens the noun. Let one sharp detail do the work.
- **Don't dump exposition.** Do not give the reader a paragraph of backstory. Reveal it slowly, through action and detail.
- **Don't let plot run the whole show.** A story with action but no character or feeling is empty. The reader needs to care about someone.
- **Don't force a perfect, explained ending.** Sometimes an ending that leaves a question is stronger than one that ties everything up.

## The quick test

For any sentence, ask: is this showing, or telling? If it tells, can I show it instead? Showing is the difference between a report and a story.

## Checklist

- [ ] I showed feelings through detail and action.
- [ ] My detail is specific, not generic.
- [ ] My characters have a want, a flaw, and a voice.
- [ ] My dialogue has subtext.
- [ ] I trusted the reader and cut over-explaining.
- [ ] I revised beyond the first draft.
