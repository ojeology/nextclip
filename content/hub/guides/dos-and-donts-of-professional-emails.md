Email is where professionalism shows most, because it is where rushed writing happens most. These dos and don'ts keep your emails clear, courteous, and acted on.

## Do

- **Do write a specific subject line.** Name the topic and, where useful, the action you need ("Question about the Q3 report — deadline"). A reader should know what the email is about before opening it.
- **Do state the reason at the top.** The first sentence should say what this is about. Do not make the reader scroll to find the point.
- **Do make the ask clear and easy to answer.** Say exactly what you need and, if you can, give options so the reader can just pick.
- **Do use a professional greeting and close.** "Hi [Name]" and "Thanks" are enough at work. Match the formality to the relationship.
- **Do proofread before sending.** Typos and grammar slips read as carelessness, especially in a job or client context.
- **Do think before you hit reply-all.** Check who actually needs to see it.

## Don't

- **Don't use a vague subject line.** "Quick question," "Hey," and "Re: the thing" waste the reader's time and read as sloppy.
- **Don't bury the point.** If the ask is in the third paragraph, or implied, the reader has to work for it — and may not bother.
- **Don't pad with politeness.** "I hope you're well, hope the weekend was good, just wanted to..." adds noise. Be warm but get to the point.
- **Don't be aggressive or emotional in writing.** Email has no tone of voice. If you are upset, wait, or pick up the phone.
- **Don't write a wall of text with no ask.** Long, dense emails get skimmed or ignored.
- **Don't send when you are angry.** Write it, wait, re-read, then send.

## The quick test

Could the reader, in one glance at the subject line, know what this is about? Could they reply in one line? If not, tighten it.

## Checklist

- [ ] My subject line is specific.
- [ ] I state the reason in the first line.
- [ ] My ask is clear and easy to answer.
- [ ] I cut the padding.
- [ ] I used a professional tone and checked the reply scope.
- [ ] I proofread before sending.
