An essay is a focused piece of non-fiction that makes and supports a single point. It is the most common form taught and one of the most useful to learn, because the same structure underlies articles, reports, and many other types of writing.

## What it is

An essay develops one central claim or idea in a clear, ordered way. It has an introduction that states the point, a series of body paragraphs that support it, and a conclusion that ties it together.

## Why it is used

Essays test and demonstrate clear thinking: can you make a claim and defend it? They are required in academic settings and common in journalism, blogs, and personal writing (the personal essay).

## Who uses it

Students, scholars, journalists, bloggers, and anyone making an argument or telling a story in a structured way.

## Typical structure

1. **Introduction** — hook, context, and a clear thesis (your main point).
2. **Body paragraphs** — one supporting point each, in a logical order, with evidence and explanation.
3. **Conclusion** — restate the point and what it means, without new arguments.

## How to write it

**Brainstorm a specific, arguable thesis.** An essay needs a point that can be defended, not a fact or a vague topic.

**Outline your supporting points.** Choose two or three strong reasons and order them so the essay builds.

**Draft the body first.** Each paragraph: a topic sentence stating the point, evidence or examples, and an explanation of how it supports the thesis.

**Write the introduction.** Hook, short context, then your thesis.

**Write the conclusion.** Show what the essay established and why it matters.

**Revise and proofread.** Check the argument holds, then fix the language.

## Example

*Thesis:* Home-cooked meals are a better learning environment than takeaway for a beginner cook.

*Body paragraph:* First, cooking at home lets you adjust each dish as you go, which is how you actually learn. A takeaway order gives you a finished thing with no feedback. For a beginner, feedback is the lesson.

*Conclusion:* Learning to cook means getting to make mistakes at home, taste the result, and adjust. Takeaway gives you a meal; home cooking gives you the skill.

## Common mistakes

A vague thesis that cannot be defended. Body paragraphs that repeat the same point. Using the intro as a fence (a list of things you will also bash against that has no real thesis). Introducing new ideas in the conclusion. Padding to hit a word count.

## Checklist

- [ ] My thesis is specific and arguable.
- [ ] Each body paragraph supports one point.
- [ ] The paragraphs are in a logical, building order.
- [ ] I included evidence or examples for each point.
- [ ] The conclusion ties the essay together without new claims.
- [ ] I revised the structure before proofreading.
