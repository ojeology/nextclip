Your first paid writing gig is usually small. That is fine — the point is to get on the board, prove you can deliver, and start a portfolio of paid work. The path is repeatable.

## The starting path

**Write first, publish somewhere.** You need something to show. Write a couple of strong pieces and publish them where you can, so an editor or client has evidence.

**Pick a small, reachable target.** Aim at a modest market that publishes work like yours and that you can genuinely match. BRYME lists many markets with their pay, word counts, and eligibility — start with one that fits you.

**Pitch one specific idea.** Not a general capability, but one angle for one publication with a clear pay and length.

**Use the official channel.** Use the publication's own submission method, not a third party or an invented address.

**Deliver on time, complete and clean.** If accepted, meet the word count, follow the style, and proofread. A clean first delivery builds trust and often leads to more.

**Ask for the next one.** Once you have a paid credit, use it. Pitch again, ask for more work from the same client, and raise your rate on the next gig.

## How to find a target

Use BRYME's verified opportunity pages. Each one shows the type of writing, the pay, the word count, and who it is open to. Find a market that fits your kind of writing and your location, then read its official guideline before you pitch.

## Accepting a market reality

A publication that pays per piece or per word is not a job offer — it is one opportunity among many. Do not put all your hopes on one market; pitch several and treat each as a chance, not a guarantee.

## Common mistakes

Waiting for the "perfect" big client. Pitching a vague capability instead of a specific piece. Using an unverified or invented submission address. Giving up after one no. Accepting work you cannot deliver.

## Practical tips

- Build three polished samples and keep them linkable.
- Pitch three markets for one good idea (and say so if required).
- Take the first paid piece even if small; it is your proof.

## Checklist

- [ ] I wrote and published a couple of samples.
- [ ] I picked a reachable market that fits me.
- [ ] I pitched one specific idea officially.
- [ ] I read the guideline and knew the pay and word count.
- [ ] I delivered on time, complete and clean.
- [ ] I used my first paid credit to pitch again.
