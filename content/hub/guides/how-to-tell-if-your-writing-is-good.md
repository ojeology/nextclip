"I don't know whether my writing is good" is the most common writing problem there is. It is usually not a problem of talent — it is a problem of not knowing what "good" means well enough to check it.

## What it is

"Good" writing is writing that does its job: it is clear, it holds the reader's attention, and it achieves its purpose. You can test for each of these with specific checks rather than a vague feeling.

## Why it matters

Without criteria, you loop between "this is terrible" and "this is brilliant." With criteria, you know what to fix and can measure whether you fixed it.

## Step-by-step guide

**Check clarity.** Can a person reading it once understand it? Read it cold. Every sentence you have to re-read is a place to fix.

**Check the purpose.** What is this piece trying to do? For non-fiction, is the main point clear and supported? For a story, is there a change or payoff?

**Check the structure.** Is there a beginning, a build, and an end? Do the sections flow so the reader is never lost?

**Check the title and opening.** Would you keep reading if you saw this on a screen, cold, knowing nothing? This is often the weakest part.

**Check for wasted words and repetition.** Good writing is economical. Remove what adds nothing.

**Check the details and errors.** Are the facts, names, numbers and spellings right? Is there a typo?

**Get a specific reader's view.** Ask one person actually to say what confused them, not for a grade. "Was it clear? What did you miss?"

**Check against strong examples.** Read the piece alongside something by a writer you trust whose style is similar. Compare the qualities, not the content.

## Common mistakes

Equating "good" with clever or literary. Judging a first draft against a polished published piece. Asking for reassurance ("is this OK?") instead of a specific critique. Assuming quantity of writing equals quality. Being unable to tell because you never get real feedback.

## Practical tips

- Give the piece time to cool, then read it as a stranger would.
- Read aloud; your ear flags what your eye forgives.
- Ask for two specifics: what was unclear, and what did you skip.
- A piece is "good" when it does its job, not when it is flawless.
