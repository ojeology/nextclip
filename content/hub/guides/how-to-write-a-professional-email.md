A professional email's job is to be understood quickly and acted on easily. It is not about sounding impressive or formal; it is about clarity and respect for the reader's time.

## What it is

An email written in a work or professional context: to a colleague, a client, an employer, an editor, or a customer. It should be brief, clear, and easy to reply to.

## Why it matters

Email is first impressions in writing. A clear, courteous email builds trust and gets answers; a muddled, rude, or error-filled one loses the reader and the opportunity.

## How to write it

**Use a subject line that names the subject.** "Proposal for the Q3 campaign — draft attached" beats "Question" or "Hello."

**Open with a greeting and get to the point.** One line of context if useful, then state what the email is about. Do not bury the ask.

**State the request or purpose clearly.** If you need something, say exactly what and by when. If you are responding, answer the point directly.

**Keep it to one topic.** One email, one main subject. If you have several unrelated things, change the subject line or split them.

**Be polite and brief.** A friendly tone is fine; a long-formal one is unnecessary. Two or three short paragraphs is usually plenty.

**Show you have done your part.** Attach what you promised, reference the previous message, and include any details the reader needs.

**Close with a clear next step.** "Could you confirm by Friday?" or "Please let me know if you'd like me to adjust." Then sign off with your name and role.

**Proofread.** A typo in a professional email is a small but real signal. Read it once before sending.

## Common mistakes

A vague subject line. Burying the request in paragraphs of preamble. Writing a wall of text. Being abrupt or accusing when you are frustrated. Asking several things with no priority. Forgetting attachments you promised. Sending while angry.

## Practical tips

- Put the most important sentence earlier in the email.
- Use bullet points for lists so they are easy to scan.
- If an email is making you emotional, write it and wait, or send it to no one first.
- Keep emails short enough that the whole thing fits on one screen.

## Checklist

- [ ] My subject line names the subject.
- [ ] I get to the point quickly.
- [ ] I stated exactly what I need and by when.
- [ ] I kept it to one main topic.
- [ ] I was polite and brief.
- [ ] I closed with a clear next step and proofread before sending.
