A handful of grammar mistakes cause most of the errors in everyday writing. Fix these few and your writing will be noticeably cleaner. You do not need to master every rule.

## The mistakes to fix

**1. Subject–verb agreement.** The subject and verb must agree in number. "The list of items *is* long" — the subject is "list" (singular), so "is." The error comes from matching the verb to the noun nearest it ("items" is plural). Find the true subject.

**2. Sentence fragments.** A fragment is a group of words that is not a complete sentence. "Because it rained." Fix by joining it to the sentence it belongs to.

**3. Run-ons and comma splices.** Two complete sentences joined with no punctuation (run-on) or only a comma (comma splice). "The report was ready, it went to the client." Fix with a period, a semicolon, or a conjunction.

**4. Its vs it's.** "It's" = "it is"; "its" = possessive. Say "it is" to test.

**5. Your vs you're** and **their/there/they're**. "You're" = "you are"; "your" = possessive. "There" = place; "their" = possessive; "they're" = "they are."

**6. Its/it's and similar homophones** — aloud (out loud) vs allowed (permitted), affect (verb) vs effect (noun), then (time) vs than (comparison), to/too/two, whose/who's.

**7. Then vs than.** "Than" compares ("better than"); "then" is about time ("back then").

**8. Effect vs affect.** "Affect" is usually a verb ("to affect"); "effect" is usually a noun ("the effect").

**9. Misplaced modifiers.** A phrase that modifies the wrong thing. "Walking to the store, the rain fell" suggests the rain was walking. Put the modifier next to what it describes.

**10. Redundant double negatives.** "I did not go nowhere" → "I did not go anywhere" or "I went nowhere."

## How to improve

Read your work aloud — your ear catches many of these. Set your spellchecker to your English variety and turn on grammar checks. Keep a personal list of the errors you repeat and check for them specifically. The proofreading checklist and tools help you catch them before you submit.

## Common mistakes gone wrong

Trying to learn every rule and getting overwhelmed. Relying only on a spellchecker (it misses homophones and agreement). Fixing errors but not understanding why, so they come back. Being so afraid of mistakes you never write.

## Practical tips

- Fix agreement, fragments and run-ons first — they cause the most confusion.
- Use the "say it as its/its it is" test for the hardest homophones.
- Keep a one-line list of your three most common errors and check for them.
