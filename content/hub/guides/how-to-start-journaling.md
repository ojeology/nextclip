Journaling is writing for yourself. There is no audience, no grade, no market — and no way to do it wrong. It is one of the easiest and most valuable writing habits to begin.

## What it is

Journaling is the regular practice of writing about your thoughts, experiences, feelings, or ideas in an ongoing record. It is private writing: reflection, not performance.

## Why it matters

It clears the mind, helps you process experiences, and — for writers — makes you fluent. Writing regularly, even only for yourself, builds the muscle that every other kind of writing needs.

## When to use it

Daily or a few times a week, for a few minutes. It is a practice, not a chore to be perfect at.

## Step-by-step guide

**Keep it low-pressure.** It is for you. No one reads it, so there is no need to be impressive or even to make sense.

**Set a tiny goal so you actually do it.** Two sentences a day, or three minutes, is enough. Consistency beats volume.

**Choose a simple tool.** A notebook, a note app, or a document. Whatever you will actually use is the right one.

**Just begin with what is true today.** Write how you feel, what happened, what you are thinking about. Describe a moment.

## Prompts to get started

- What is taking up the most space in my head right now?
- What happened today that I should not forget?
- What am I grateful for, and why?
- What is one thing I keep avoiding?
- What did I learn this week?
- What would I tell a friend about this situation?
- What is the one thing I want to figure out?

**Try a form.** Free writing (whatever comes), gratitude (a few things you're grateful for), reflection (what did this mean?), or a travel log (capture what you see and feel in a new place).

**Re-read sometimes.** Your past entries are themselves instructive — and often useful material later.

## Common mistakes

Trying to make it perfect or publishable. Setting a goal too large to keep. Journaling only when you are stressed, so it becomes about problem-solving and stops. Comparing yours to anyone else's.

## Practical tips

- Put the journal where you will see it to make the habit easy.
- Timer for three minutes; stop when it rings.
- If you miss a day, just start again. No streak to protect.
- Write messy and honest; you can always clean up later if you ever choose to.

## Checklist

- [ ] I set a small, realistic goal.
- [ ] I chose a tool I will actually use.
- [ ] I wrote something true about my day.
- [ ] I used a prompt when I did not know what to write.
- [ ] I kept it private and low-pressure.
- [ ] I will go easy on myself if I miss a day.
