A memoir is true, but it is not your whole life in order. It is a true story with a shape — a theme, selected moments, and a point of view. The task is to take real events and make them mean something.

## What a memoir is

A memoir is a non-fiction account of a period or theme in your life, told with the craft of a story. Unlike an autobiography (which covers a whole life in order), a memoir focuses on one span or one theme and shapes it like a narrative.

## How to write it

**Find your theme, not your whole life.** Pick the thread that runs through your story: coming home, leaving, a loss, a change, a choice. The theme gives the memoir its shape and tells you what to include and what to leave out.

**Choose the moment that is really about the theme.** A memoir is built from scenes. Choose the key moments — the ones that reveal the theme — and write them as scenes with detail and dialogue.

**Be honest, not flawless.** A memoir is more powerful when you show your faults and doubts honestly. The reader connects to a real person, not a hero.

**Use a point of view.** Reference the narrator's understanding at the time versus now. A memoir has room for hindsight — what you thought then and what you know now.

**End somewhere, not nowhere.** A memoir needs a resolution or a shift. It does not have to be a happy ending, but the reader should feel something changed.

## How to structure it

1. **The theme and the entry point** — start in a moment that sets up the theme.
2. **Scenes** — the key moments, told concretely.
3. **Reflection and hindsight** — what it meant then, what it means now.
4. **A resolution or shift** — how things landed, and who you became.

## Common mistakes

Telling your whole life in order (that is an autobiography, and it reads like a list). Being too self-flattering. Listing events without scenes or theme. Not knowing what the memoir is about. Explaining the meaning instead of showing it.

## Practical tips

- Write your key memories as scenes first, then arrange them around the theme.
- Ask "what is this REALLY about?" — the honest answer is your theme.
- Read a good memoir to see how theme and scene work together.
