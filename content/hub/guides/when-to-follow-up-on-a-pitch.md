Silence after a pitch is not an answer, but it is information. Most publications never reply to pitches they do not want; some simply drown. The follow-up exists for the second case, and it works only when it is short, calm and rare.

**The timing rule.** Wait one to two weeks, then send one short follow-up. Daily news has faster clocks; features and magazines can honestly take a month, and the publication's own submission page overrides everything - if it says "reply within four weeks or not at all", believe it.

**What to send.** Three or four sentences, the original thread kept below, no new attachments. Something like: "Resurfacing this in case it was buried - still happy to write it if it is useful. If not, a no is completely fine." That last sentence does more work than it looks like: it makes the reply easy.

**How many times.** One follow-up, at a stretch two, spaced at least a week apart. A third nudge to a silent editor is not persistence; it is a reputation. Silence after a second follow-up is the answer.

**The signals to act on.** An editor who replies to say "not for us" with a reason is worth pitching again - a real no with feedback is a door. An editor who opens nothing, ever, for three pitches straight is a wall; spend the next idea elsewhere. And if the publication announced a shutdown, a restructure, or an editor leaving on social media, stop - the pitch is not buried, it is homeless. Re-read it, freshen the hook, and send it to the next name on your list the same week.

**The habit that beats luck.** Pitch in batches. Five live pitches at once make one silence a statistic instead of a heartbreak - and that is the real cure for the follow-up anxiety.
