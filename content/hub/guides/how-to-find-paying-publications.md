The best way to get paid is to pitch the right market. The hard part is not finding places that list "writing jobs" — it is finding legitimate ones that welcome you and actually pay.

## What to look for

**A named, real publication.** A credible market has a website, an editorial team, published writers, and a clear submission guideline.

**A stated pay.** Even a range ("we pay £50–£150 per piece") is a good sign. A market that never mentions pay is a flag.

**Clear eligibility.** Good guidelines say who they are open to and any region or country rules. "International submissions welcome" is not the same as "open to everyone" — read carefully.

**An official submission channel.** A form, an editorial email, or a guideline-linked system. Avoid third parties promising to pitch for a fee.

**Real examples of published work.** Browse recent pieces to see the style and the quality. This tells you what they want and whether you can match it.

## How to judge legitimacy

**Check the official guideline.** It should state the submission method, format, and any rules. A page that only says "we accept submissions" with no detail is a flag.

**Confirm the pay or its absence.** If pay is not stated, treat it as unknown, not as "unpaid" or "paid." Never assume.

**Never pay to be published.** A legitimate publication does not charge writers to be considered, and does not promise publication for a fee.

**Avoid gigs promising guaranteed acceptance or money.** No legitimate editor guarantees a payout before reading your work.

**Verify before you commit.** Confirm the client or publication, the pay, and how you will be paid. If you cannot verify the route, do not use it.

## Where to look

Start with BRYME's opportunity directory — each entry is researched and verified, with the official guideline linked and a last-checked date. From there, follow markets that publish the kind of writing you do, and read a few of their recent pieces.

## Common mistakes

Treating a "writing jobs" list as verified. Relying on a repost or a third-party aggregator. Assuming "no country listed" means "open worldwide." Paying a fee to be "considered." Pitching a market that does not fit your work.

## Practical tips

- Verify pay and route against the publication's own guideline.
- Keep a shortlist of markets that fit your style and location.
- Record the pay, word count, and eligibility of each market before you pitch.
