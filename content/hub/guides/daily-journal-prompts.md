The hardest part of journaling is often the first line. A prompt removes that obstacle. Use these as starting points, then write what is true for you — there is no wrong way.

## Daily prompts

- What is taking up the most space in my mind right now?
- What happened today that I do not want to forget?
- What was one good thing about today, and why?
- What am I currently worried about, and what is actually in my control?
- What did I learn today, or last week?
- What is one thing I keep putting off, and why?
- What would I tell a close friend about this situation?

## Reflection prompts

- What has changed in my life in the last year?
- What am I most proud of, and what did it take?
- What belief about myself might be too small?
- What do I want to be different six months from now?
- What is something I avoided that I am glad or sorry I did?
- What is a time I was wrong about something, and what it taught me?

## Gratitude prompts

- What are three things I am grateful for and why?
- Who made a difference today, and how?
- What is a small comfort I often overlook?
- What is something in my surroundings I am thankful for?
- What is a skill I am grateful to have?

## Travel and place prompts

- What do I notice first in this new place?
- What sounds, smells and sights stand out?
- How is this different from home, in a good way?
- What would I tell someone who visits here?

## Writing prompts

- What is the story I keep telling myself, and is it true?
- If I wrote my day as a short story, what would the turning point be?
- What is a phrase or line from today worth keeping?

## How to use the prompts

**Just pick one and write.** Do not overthink. Set a timer for a few minutes and write without stopping.

**Write free and messy.** No one reads it. Do not edit, do not judge. What you are building is the habit.

**Vary the type.** Mix reflection, gratitude and daily notes so it stays fresh.

**Stop when the timer rings.** A short, consistent entry beats a long, rare one.

## Common mistakes

Seeing it as a chore you must perfect. Editing while you write. Re-reading and judging every entry. Skipping days and quitting instead of restarting.

## Practical tips

- Keep the journal where you will see it.
- Set a tiny goal (a few lines or a few minutes).
- Missed a day? Just start again — no streak to protect.

## Checklist

- [ ] I picked a prompt and wrote without stopping.
- [ ] I wrote freely and messily.
- [ ] I set a short timer and stopped when it rang.
- [ ] I did not edit or judge my words.
- [ ] I will come back tomorrow.
