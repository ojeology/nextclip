Gratitude journaling is the easiest journaling habit to start — you just name a few things you are grateful for and why. The difference between a good entry and a forgettable one is specificity: why it matters, not just what it is.

## What a gratitude entry is

A short record of the things — often small — you are grateful for, along with why. It is not a list of achievements. It is a practice of noticing what is already good.

## How to write one

**Pick two or three things.** They can be tiny: a good coffee, a helpful message, the rain stopping. Trying to be grateful for "everything" is too vague to work.

**Say why each one matters.** This is the difference between a list and a practice. "I am grateful for the quiet morning — it gave me space to think before the noise started." The why is where the benefit lives.

**Be specific, not grand.** "I am grateful for my health" is vague. "I am grateful I could walk without pain today" is specific. Specificity makes it real.

**Notice small comforts.** The overlooked things — the warm sun, a working light, a kind word — are often the most grounding.

**Write in present or past, whatever feels true.** You can write "I am grateful" or "today I noticed." Keep it natural and short.

**Do not force positivity.** If you are having a hard day, you can be grateful for just one thing, or for a small act of resilience. Gratitude is not denial.

## Examples

**A short entry:**
> Today I am grateful for the quiet before everyone woke up, because it is the only time the house feels like mine. I am grateful my sister called on time — it was the first easy conversation we have had in weeks.

**A one-line entry:**
> The coffee was exactly right and I did not have to rush.

## When to write it

At the end of the day, or first thing in the morning. Consistency matters more than the moment. A few minutes, a few times a week, is a genuine practice.

## Common mistakes

Writing only big things so you run out of ideas. Listing without saying why. Forcing a positive spin on a bad day. Quitting after a week because it felt routine.

## Practical tips

- Be specific and attach the why.
- Two or three items is enough.
- Miss a few days? Just start again.

## Checklist

- [ ] I said why each matters.
- [ ] I noticed small comforts, not just big ones.
- [ ] I did not force positivity.
- [ ] I kept it short and natural.
