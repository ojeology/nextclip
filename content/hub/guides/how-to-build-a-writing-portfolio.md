You do not need dozens of clips to get published — you need a handful of strong, relevant ones presented well. A portfolio is proof you can do the kind of work a publication needs.

## What it is

A portfolio is a curated collection of your writing samples that shows an editor or client the kind of work you do. It is not everything you have written; it is the best few pieces that fit the opportunity.

## Why it matters

Editors and clients decide quickly based on evidence. A focused portfolio that matches their needs is far more persuasive than a long list of unrelated pieces.

## How to build it

**Start with quality over quantity.** Two or three sharp, relevant pieces beat twenty weak ones. Editors would rather see three good samples than a wall of mediocrity.

**Write in the style you want.** If you want essays, write essays. If you want features, write features. If you want copy, write copy. Your portfolio teaches a client what you can do.

**Publish where you can.** A personal blog, a Medium account, or a free platform counts, provided the work is genuinely yours and well done. Read the guidelines first — some markets will not consider previously self-published work, but a portfolio is still valuable.

**Match samples to the opportunity.** When you pitch, send the clips most like the piece you want to write. Three relevant samples beat a random five.

**Present them as links.** Live links are easier for an editor to open than files to download. Keep them tidy and organised by type or topic.

**Keep it current.** A portfolio is a living thing. Update it as you publish, and remove weak or outdated pieces.

**Add a short bio.** One or two lines: who you are, what you write, and any published credits. Keep it brief and factual.

## What to include

Three to six strong pieces that show range within your niche, plus a short bio and clear contact details. If you are starting fresh, publish two or three pieces on your own site first so you have something to show.

## Common mistakes

Including everything. Leading with unrelated work. A portfolio with dead links. No bio or contact. Overthinking it instead of publishing something.

## Practical tips

- Pick a niche and show depth in it.
- Keep one strong sample per type of work you want to sell.
- Update monthly or as you publish.

## Checklist

- [ ] I have two or three strong, relevant samples.
- [ ] I wrote in the style I actually want to sell.
- [ ] My samples are published and linkable.
- [ ] I match samples to each opportunity.
- [ ] My links all work.
- [ ] I have a short bio and contact details.
