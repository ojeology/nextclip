A report is written to help someone decide. These dos and don'ts are what make a report land — and what make one get skimmed and shelved.

## Do

- **Do lead with the answer.** Put the key finding and recommendation in an executive summary at the top. A busy reader decides up front.
- **Do separate facts from recommendations.** Report what you found as fact, then recommend what to do. Keeping them distinct is what makes a report credible.
- **Do use clear headings and plain language.** A report is scanned, not read, by the people who need it. Headings, tables and short sections help.
- **Do evidence what you claim.** Say where your data comes from, and be precise. Numbers and sources build trust.
- **Do end with a clear next step.** Give a recommendation and say what you need to act on it, and by when.
- **Do keep it as short as the job needs.** A report that is too long hides its own point.

## Don't

- **Don't bury the finding.** If the conclusion is at the bottom, a busy reader never finds it. Lead with it.
- **Don't blend opinion into the findings.** Facts and recommendations are different. Keep them separate and clear.
- **Don't use jargon or a lot of passive voice.** "It was determined that..." and buzzwords obscure meaning. Say who did what.
- **Don't pad with context.** Do not fill pages with background the reader already knows. Every line should serve the decision.
- **Don't write a report with no recommendation.** Finding a problem without suggesting a fix is incomplete.
- **Don't ignore the reader.** A report for a busy manager is different from one for a technical team. Write for who reads it.

## The quick test

If someone read only your executive summary and recommendation, would they understand the report and know what to do? If yes, the report is structured well.

## Checklist

- [ ] My summary leads with the answer.
- [ ] I keep facts and recommendations separate.
- [ ] I used plain language and clear headings.
- [ ] I evidenced my claims with sources and numbers.
- [ ] I ended with a clear next step.
- [ ] I kept it as short as the job needs.
