Australia is unusual: it has a professional body that publishes actual recommended rates, updated annually and pegged to a union agreement. That makes the gap between what writers *should* be paid and what publications *do* pay unusually easy to measure.

It is a big gap.

## What the ASA recommends

The **Australian Society of Authors** publishes recommended minimum rates, benchmarked against the **MEAA** (Media, Entertainment and Arts Alliance) enterprise agreement for journalists and updated each financial year. All rates exclude GST.

**Freelance writing, 2026 (after the January 3.75% MEAA uplift):**

| | Rate |
|---|---|
| Per day | **A$1,197.82** |
| Per half day | A$798.18 |
| Per hour | **A$300.02** |
| 1,000 words or less | A$1,258.16 |
| Per word thereafter | **A$1.12** |

**Poetry, FY 2026/27** (after a 3.3% WPI increase applied July 2026):

| | Rate |
|---|---|
| Journals — per poem | **A$67–A$145** |
| Commissioned for print, publication/reading only | A$333 |
| Commissioned with reflection, prompt and recordings | A$555 |
| Corporate commission | A$1,111 |

The ASA is explicit that these apply to **short-term, one-off freelance projects** and are estimates rather than a description of market conditions — some editors pay more, many pay significantly less.

## What Australian publications actually pay

BRYME has verified **10 Australian publications** against their own guidelines. Among those publishing a figure, the **median is A$190 per piece**, ranging from A$40 to A$1,000.

| Publication | Rate |
|---|---|
| **Kill Your Darlings** | Minimum **A$1,000** — members only |
| **Westerly** | A$250 poem · A$300 sequence · A$500 prose · A$620 comics |
| **Overland** | A$580 print · A$180 online, **plus 12% super** |
| **Griffith Review** | A$0.75/word print · A$200 poetry · A$500 online |
| **Aurealis** | **A$0.02–0.06 a word**, rising to 6c for 2027 |

## The gap, stated plainly

> **The ASA recommends A$1.12 a word. Aurealis pays two to six cents.**

That is not a rounding error. It is a factor of roughly twenty to fifty.

Both are honest numbers. They describe different things:

**The ASA rate card describes commissioned professional work** — a magazine, government body, arts organisation or company hiring a writer for a defined job. It is what the MEAA has negotiated for staff journalists, translated to freelance terms.

**Literary journals are grant-funded and pay what their funding allows.** Aurealis is a small speculative-fiction magazine. Westerly runs out of a university. Their rates are not a negotiating position, they are a budget.

**One market survey sits between the two.** Rachel's List, which surveys Australian creative freelancers, reports an average day rate of around **A$350–500** and print story rates near **80c a word** — well below the ASA recommendation, well above the literary journals.

## What this should change

**Quote the ASA rate for commercial and commissioned work.** It exists precisely so you have something to point at, it is pegged to a union agreement, and it is updated annually. Naming it in a negotiation is normal practice, not cheek.

**Do not try to negotiate a literary journal.** A$20 or A$40 for a poem is a grant allocation. The lever is choosing better-funded markets — Westerly at A$250 a poem, Kill Your Darlings at A$1,000 minimum for fiction — not haggling with an editor who has no discretion.

**Notice the superannuation line.** Overland states its online rate as A$180 **plus 12% super**. Under Australian rules, some freelance writing engagements attract superannuation, and it is genuinely additional money. Ask whether a fee includes it.

**And GST.** All ASA rates exclude it. If you are registered, add it. If you are not sure whether you should be registered, that is a question for an accountant, not an article.

## The two markets in one line

**A day of commissioned commercial work at the ASA rate is worth roughly six poems at Westerly, or fifty at the bottom of the Aurealis range.**

That is not an argument against literary publication — a Griffith Review or Overland byline does things no day rate does. It is an argument for knowing which one you are doing before you agree to it.
