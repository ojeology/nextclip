Copywriting is writing with one job: to get the reader to do something — buy, sign up, click, or enquire. It is persuasion, but honest persuasion. The best copy says what the product does and makes that feel worth acting on.

## What it is

Copywriting is any writing designed to sell or convert: ad copy, landing page copy, email subject lines, call-to-action buttons, product pages and social ads. It differs from editorial writing because it carries a clear, measurable goal.

## How to write it

**Know the single action you want.** One piece of copy has one goal. Decide it before writing — "buy," "subscribe," "book a call" — and make every line move toward it.

**Write for the reader first, not the product.** The reader does not care what your product is; they care what it does for them. Open on their problem, their need, or their goal.

**Lead with a benefit, not a feature.** A feature is what it is; a benefit is what it gives them. "Refillable cartridge" is a feature; "never run out mid-sentence" is the benefit. Use the benefit to make them care, then the feature as proof.

**Use clear, plain language.** Copy is read quickly. Short sentences, everyday words, no hype that would make a reader suspicious.

**Address the objection.** A smart reader is sceptical. Name the hesitation and answer it — "you may be worried it is hard to use; it is not."

**Make the call to action specific and low-friction.** "Get started free" or "Book a call" is clearer and easier than "Submit" or "Learn more."

**Be honest.** Over-claiming erodes trust and fails long term. Copy that overpromises wins the click but loses the customer.

## The formula

**Problem → Promise → Proof → Call to action.** Name the problem the reader has, promise the outcome, give evidence to support it, then tell them precisely what to do next.

## Common mistakes

Talking about the product instead of the reader. Listing features with no benefit. Hype and vague superlatives ("world-class, best-in-class"). No clear call to action. Copy that reads like an essay rather than a direct appeal.

## Practical tips

- Write the call to action before the copy — if you know the goal, the rest is easier.
- Cut every word that does not help the reader act.
- Read it as a sceptic; would it convince you?

## Checklist

- [ ] I defined the single action I want.
- [ ] I wrote for the reader's problem first.
- [ ] I led with benefits, not just features.
- [ ] I used clear, plain, honest language.
- [ ] I addressed the main objection.
- [ ] I ended with a specific, low-friction call to action.
