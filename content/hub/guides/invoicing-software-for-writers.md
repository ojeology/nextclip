Before the comparison, the finding that shaped it.

While checking prices for this guide, five sources quoted the FreshBooks Lite plan at **$11.50, $15, $17, $21, $23 and $6.30 a month**. All six figures were published in 2026. None of them is a lie.

That is because invoicing software pricing is **regionally personalised and promotionally rotated**. FreshBooks lists different prices in the UK, Australia, Canada, Ireland, Singapore, South Africa and New Zealand, and runs introductory discounts that make the headline number temporary. The price you see depends on where you load the page from and what week it is.

**So the single most useful thing in any software comparison is the date and the source, not the number.** Everything below was read off the vendor's own pricing page on **6 September 2026**, from a single location. Check your own before you commit.

## What BRYME did and did not do here

BRYME has **not** run these tools day to day as a paying customer. This is a pricing and feature comparison built from vendors' own published documentation, not a hands-on review, and it does not pretend otherwise.

What it does add is the part writers actually need and general small-business reviews never cover: what happens when your client is a literary magazine rather than a company.

## The three that matter for writers

### Wave — the free one that stays free

Read from Wave's own pricing page, 6 September 2026:

| | Starter | Pro |
|---|---|---|
| **Monthly cost** | **$0** | $19/month, or $190/year |
| Invoices | Unlimited | Unlimited |
| Card payments | 2.9% + $0.60 | 2.9% + $0 on the first 10 transactions a month |
| Amex | 3.4% + $0.60 | 3.4% + $0 |
| Bank import | — | Automatic |
| Receipt capture | — | Unlimited |

Wave's free tier includes **unlimited invoices, estimates, bills and bookkeeping records**. For most freelance writers that is the whole job. Wave also lists a Canadian price ($25/month Pro) and a Wave Advisors bookkeeping service from $149/month, which is not aimed at you.

**The catch to understand:** the payment processing fee is where Wave makes its money on the free plan. On a £375 feature paid by card, 2.9% + $0.60 is about £11.50. If your clients pay by bank transfer — and magazines almost always do — you never touch that fee, and Wave is genuinely free.

### Zoho Invoice — free, and built for invoicing only

Zoho Invoice is a **free** invoicing product, distinct from Zoho Books, its paid accounting suite. Its published feature set covers quotes, recurring invoices, sales receipts, payment reminders, expenses, time tracking and a customer portal, with apps on iOS, Android, Mac and Windows.

For a writer who wants to send an invoice and chase it, and does not need bookkeeping, this is the leanest option on the list.

### FreshBooks — the one whose price you have to look up yourself

FreshBooks is the most recommended tool in this category and the hardest to quote. Beyond the price variance above, the structural thing to know is the **billable-client cap**: the cheapest tier is limited to a small number of billable clients, with the next tier up allowing considerably more. Sources agree on the shape of that ladder even where they disagree on the price.

**Why this matters more to writers than to most freelancers.** A designer might have five retainer clients all year. A writer pitching magazines can easily invoice eight different publications in a quarter, each for one piece. Client-count pricing punishes exactly that pattern. Read the cap before the price.

## The part no general comparison covers

Invoicing a magazine is not invoicing a company, and the software rarely helps.

**Many magazines invoice you, not the other way round.** Plenty of the publications BRYME tracks issue their own contributor payment form or self-billing document. Your beautiful invoice template is irrelevant; what matters is returning their form quickly and correctly.

**Payment terms are often "on publication", not "in 30 days".** That is not a term most invoicing tools can model. A piece accepted in September and published in February is paid in February. Your software will mark it overdue for five months. Turn the automatic late-payment reminders **off** for publication clients — an automated nudge to an editor who has done nothing wrong is a fast way to not be commissioned again.

**Kill fees need their own line.** If a commissioned piece is dropped, many publications pay a percentage. Invoice it as a distinct item naming the piece and the agreed kill-fee percentage, or it will look like an error.

**Currency is usually the real cost, not the software.** A UK magazine paying £150 into a non-UK account will lose more to conversion than any subscription costs. That is a banking problem, not an invoicing one — and for writers outside the US, UK, EU and a few others, it is the binding constraint.

## What I would actually use

**If your clients are magazines paying by bank transfer: Wave's free tier.** You will never hit the processing fee, unlimited invoices covers you, and there is nothing to cancel.

**If you want the simplest possible tool and no bookkeeping: Zoho Invoice**, also free.

**If you already use an accountant who asks for a specific platform:** use theirs. The cost of fighting your accountant exceeds any subscription here.

**Before paying for anything**, work out what you actually earn per month with the [freelance rate calculator](/tools/freelance-rate-calculator/). A $19/month subscription is roughly one poem's fee at typical literary rates. Make sure the tool is earning that back in time saved.

- [ ] I checked the billable-client cap, not just the monthly cost

- [ ] I have turned off automatic late reminders for publication clients
- [ ] The subscription costs less per month than the time it saves me is worth
