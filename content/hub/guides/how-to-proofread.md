Proofreading is not the same as editing. Editing improves what the piece says; proofreading catches the small errors and inconsistencies. It is the last step and it should be done with fresh eyes.

## What it is

Proofreading is a final pass that finds and fixes surface errors: spelling, grammar, punctuation, typos, formatting, and consistency. It does not restructure or rewrite.

## Why it matters

Small errors distract readers and damage credibility. A misspelled word is easy to miss yourself but damaging in a submission, an application, or a published piece.

## Step-by-step guide

**Proofread on a printed page or a different device.** It is hard to proofread on the screen where you wrote. Printing, or at least changing font and zoom, forces you to look differently.

**Read slowly, or read aloud.** Your ear catches things your eye skips. Read the last sentence of one paragraph while the next is fresh.

**Work from the bottom of the document up** (or from the end, backward paragraphs). Reading backward breaks the flow and stops your brain from "auto-correcting" what you meant to write.

**Do one kind of error at a time.** Pass once for spelling, once for punctuation, once for consistency, once for formatting. Concentrating on one thing is far more effective.

**Check the specific things that commonly go wrong.** Homophones (their/there/they're, its/it's, your/you're), missing words, doubled words ("the the"), and inconsistent tense.

**Check names and facts.** Spellings of names, numbers, dates, URLs, and titles you cannot guess.

**Leave it for a while before the final pass.** Fresh eyes catch what tired eyes miss.

## Common mistakes

Proofreading straight after writing when you're still reading what you meant to say. Editing and proofreading at the same time. Relying only on a spellchecker (it misses homophones and context). Skipping the final pass because "it's good enough."

## Practical tips

- Use the word counter and stylized tools to find repeated words or double gaps.
- Check one page at a time and put a mark where you stop.
- Confusing "which/that" or "its/it's" is worth adding to your personal check list.

## Checklist

- [ ] I proofread on a fresh surface or with a different font.
- [ ] I read aloud or backward to break flow.
- [ ] I did one error type per pass.
- [ ] I checked homophones and doubled words.
- [ ] I verified names, numbers, dates and URLs.
- [ ] I took a break before the final pass.
