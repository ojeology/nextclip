Clarity is the single most valuable quality in most writing. A reader should not have to re-read a sentence to understand it. The good news: clarity is mostly a set of editing moves you can apply mechanically.

## What unclarity looks like

Long sentences with nested clauses. Jargon the reader may not know. The real subject buried late in a sentence. Passive voice that hides who is doing what. Nutmegs of abstraction where a concrete word would do.

## How to edit for it

**Find the true subject and verb.** In each sentence, ask: who is doing what? Put the subject near the start and the verb near it. "The decision to delay was made by the board" → "The board decided to delay."

**Split long sentences.** If a sentence has more than one main idea or runs over ~25 words, split it. Use the sentence-length checker to find them.

**Cut the abstract, add the concrete.** Replace "provide assistance" with "help," "make a decision" with "decide." Prefer the simpler word a reader will not stumble on.

**Replace jargon only when you know the audience.** If your reader is an expert, keep the terms. If not, define them once or use plain words. The sentence-length tool and readability score show you the result.

**Name the doer.** If a sentence is passive and the doer matters, make it the subject. "The report was sent" → "The team sent the report."

**Cut hedges and padding.** "I think that," "in my opinion," "it is important to note that" usually add nothing. Remove them.

**Read it aloud.** If you stumble, a reader will too. Rewrite what you cannot say smoothly.

## How to check your work

Run the readability score and sentence-length tool. A high score and few long sentences suggest clarity. Then read the whole thing cold and mark anywhere you had to go back.

## Common mistakes

Trying to sound smart by using big words. Keeping a long sentence because it "reads fine" to you (you wrote it — you know the meaning). Cutting so much the meaning is lost. Editing for clarity but not checking the result.

## Practical tips

- Edit clarity before you edit for style.
- If you have to re-read, fix it — that is the test.
- Shorter is nearly always clearer.

## Checklist

- [ ] I made the subject and verb the subject of each sentence.
- [ ] I split sentences over 25 words.
- [ ] I replaced abstract words with concrete ones.
- [ ] I named the doer and cut needless passive.
- [ ] I removed hedges and padding.
- [ ] I read it aloud and fixed what I stumbled on.
