The hardest part of writing is sometimes knowing what to write. But ideas are not a rare gift — they are a thing you can generate on purpose, capture, and choose among.

## How to generate ideas

**Follow your curiosity.** Write down what you have been wondering about, what confused you, or what you would love to understand. Be curious, not clever.

**Ask the reader's question.** What is the one thing someone wants to know or solve? "How do I write a strong email?" is an idea. So is "Why do my paragraphs feel long?"

**Use prompts and combinations.** Combine two unrelated things ("remote work" + "African writers") to create a fresh angle. Change a known formula: take a common topic and do it for a specific audience.

**Notice the obvious problem.** What is the thing everyone does wrong, or the question everyone has after a beginner takes on a task? That gap is an idea.

**Keep a running list.** Ideas disappear if you do not write them down. Keep a note file and add to it constantly.

## How to choose an idea

When you have several, pick the one you can best answer. Ask: Do I know enough? Is it specific, not vague? Does a real audience want it? Am I genuinely interested? An idea you can write well and care about beats a brilliant idea you know nothing about.

## How to sharpen an idea

**Turn it into a question.** "How to write a strong introduction" is general. "How do I write an introduction that keeps a reader past the first line?" is a real question you can answer.

**Narrow the audience.** "How to write" for everyone is too broad. "How to write a cover letter for a first job" is a specific, useful idea.

**Add an angle or a limit.** A specific take ("for busy beginners") or a specific outcome makes an idea sharper and easier to write well.

## Common mistakes

Waiting for a "great" idea instead of working with a decent one. Choosing an idea you know nothing about. Picking a topic too broad to do well. Keeping no list, so ideas are lost. Full stops: chasing a perfect idea and never starting.

## Practical tips

- Keep an idea list and review it weekly.
- Choose the idea you are most ready to write now.
- Narrow a broad idea into a specific, answerable question.

## Checklist

- [ ] I generated ideas by following curiosity and reading questions.
- [ ] I kept a running list.
- [ ] I chose an idea I know something about.
- [ ] I narrowed it into a specific question.
- [ ] I am ready to outline and write it.
