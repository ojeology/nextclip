Pitching is a skill, and most editors see hundreds of pitches a month. These dos and don'ts are what separates a pitch an editor reads from one they delete.

## Do

- **Do pitch a specific idea, not a topic.** "An essay about family" is a topic. "An essay about how my mother apologised with food, never words" is an idea with an angle.
- **Do match the publication.** Read a few pieces first. Pitch something that fits their section, length and voice, and tell them why.
- **Do prove you can deliver.** Say what you have already done: the interviews, the sources, the reporting. This is the strongest line in any pitch.
- **Do offer a clear outline.** A few bullets showing the shape of the piece makes it easy for the editor to say yes.
- **Do be brief and polite.** Editors are busy. A short, professional pitch that gets to the point is respected.
- **Do follow the submission rules.** Many magazines want a query, some want a resume, some take pitches through a specific form. Read the guidelines.

## Don't

- **Don't pitch the publication's own article.** "I was inspired by your piece on X, here is my take on the same X" is a hard pass.
- **Don't pitch three unrelated ideas at once.** Pick one strong idea; multiple tangents read as unfocused.
- **Don't attach a full draft when they asked for a pitch.** Follow the format they requested.
- **Don't be rude about past rejections.** Never argue or complain about a "no." It burns bridges.
- **Don't overpromise timelines or exclusivity you cannot deliver.** Be honest about what you can do.
- **Don't pitch without a hook or a stake.** Why now, and why this editor? Give them a reason to care.

## The quick test

Read your pitch and ask: would an editor, after reading the first two lines, know exactly what this piece is and why they should care? If not, sharpen the first two lines.

## Checklist

- [ ] I pitch a specific idea with an angle.
- [ ] I read the publication and matched it.
- [ ] I showed proof I can deliver.
- [ ] I included a clear outline.
- [ ] I followed the submission guidelines.
- [ ] I was brief, polite and specific.
