Editing your own work is the hardest editing there is — you are too close to it. But it is a skill you can apply systematically, and doing it in the right order makes it far less overwhelming.

## The order matters

Edit big to small: **structure → sections → paragraphs → sentences → words → proofread.** Fixing commas in a piece with the wrong structure is wasted effort. Start with what the piece *says*, then how it reads.

## How to start

**Let it cool.** Step away for a while, or at least switch tasks. Distance turns "my precious words" into "a draft I am improving."

**Read it in one go.** Read the whole thing through without stopping to fix anything. Just take notes on where you got confused or wanted to stop.

**Ask the big questions first.** Does this piece do what it set out to do? Is the point clear? Is anything missing or in the wrong place?

## Then work through the levels

**Structure.** Move, cut and add sections. Is the order right? Does each section have a job? This is where pieces are really made.

**Paragraphs.** Does each paragraph have one idea and a clear topic sentence? Split or merge.

**Sentences.** Remove repetition, tighten wordy phrasing, vary length. Use the conciseness guide.

**Words.** Swap weak or repeated words for precise ones. Cut filler and intensifiers.

**Proofread last.** Typos, punctuation, spelling. Fresh eyes, or a different surface, or reading aloud.

## Use tools to help

Run the word and character counts to check you hit the target. Use the text cleaner to spot double spaces. Check your test cases and headings (descriptive headings improve a piece you will edit). The structure and checklist tools show you if the skeleton holds.

## Common mistakes

Editing while drafting. Polishing the first paragraph for an hour. Stopping at the first sentence-level fix. Editing proofreading issues before structure. Rewriting the whole piece from scratch when parts are fine. Being so critical you never finish.

## Practical tips

- Edit in separate passes, one level at a time.
- Read aloud to hear what is awkward.
- Ask a trusted reader what confused them — specific feedback beats praise.

## Checklist

- [ ] I let the piece cool before editing.
- [ ] I fixed structure and argument first.
- [ ] I checked each paragraph has one job.
- [ ] I tightened sentences and removed repetition.
- [ ] I swapped weak words for precise ones.
- [ ] I proofread last with fresh eyes.
