"Write about yourself" freezes a surprising number of writers. But a good bio or introduction is simple: a few lines that say who you are, what you do, and why it matters — without self-indulgence.

## What kind of "about yourself" are you writing?

- **A short bio** (for a website, author page, or publication). Two or three sentences for a byline.
- **A longer about page** (for a blog or portfolio). A few short paragraphs.
- **A personal statement** (for an application). A structured argument for why you fit.

Different formats, same principle: be specific, be honest, and give the reader a reason to care.

## How to write a short bio

**State what you do.** One line: "I am a freelance writer covering technology in Africa." Say the job, not just "I am passionate about writing."

**Add what makes you specific.** One detail that distinguishes you: a niche, a beat, a place, or a notable achievement. "I have covered tech for five years and reported from three countries."

**Say what the reader gets.** A bio is not just about you; it is a promise. "I write clear, practical guides on getting published," tells a reader what to expect.

**End with a way to connect.** A name, location, and contact or links.

## How to write a longer about page

**Open with the reader's question.** "If you are here to learn how to get published, you are in the right place." Put the reader first, then introduce yourself.

**Tell a short story, not a CV.** One relevant detail of your journey — how you started, what you learned — beats a list of everything you have done.

**Be honest about what you are.** Do not inflate. A realistic, specific writer is more trustworthy than a grand one.

**Keep it to a few paragraphs.** An about page is read by someone deciding whether to trust you. Short and clear wins.

## How to write a personal statement

For an application, write as an argument: who you are, a specific experience that shaped you, what it taught you, and why you want this opportunity and how you will use it. Use the personal-statement template for the structure.

## Common mistakes

"Passionate about writing" with nothing concrete. A vague list with no niche. Oversharing. Being so humble you say nothing. Writing about yourself as if you are in a job interview with no story.

## Practical tips

- Strip the adjectives; keep the specifics.
- Read it aloud; does it sound like you?
- Cut anything you would not say in a conversation.

## Checklist

- [ ] I state what I do clearly.
- [ ] I included one specific detail that distinguishes me.
- [ ] I told the reader what they get.
- [ ] I was honest, not inflated.
- [ ] I kept it short and gave a way to connect.
- [ ] It sounds like me.
