Writers improve by reading, but only if they read like writers. That means reading for how a piece is made, not just what it says. A reader notices the story; a writer notices the choices behind it.

## What "reading like a writer" means

It is reading with an eye on craft: how is this structured, why is this line here, how does the writer open and close, what tools are they using? You are not just enjoying the piece — you are studying its construction.

## How to do it

**Read once for pleasure, then once for craft.** The first pass, let yourself enjoy and be moved. The second pass, go slow and ask: how did they do that?

**Notice the opening.** How does the writer hook you? Is it a question, a detail, a scene, a claim? Study the first paragraph closely — it is the hardest part to write.

**Notice the structure.** Where are the sections, and what job does each one do? How does the piece move from point to point?

**Notice the sentences.** Are they short or long? When does the writer vary the rhythm? Where do they break a long sentence for effect?

**Notice the details.** What specific details or images does the writer choose, and why those? General writing is forgettable; specific detail is what you remember.

**Notice the ending.** How does the writer land it? There is a craft to a strong close — a payoff, a turn, an image that stays.

**Steal the technique, not the words.** Notice the method and make it yours. The goal is not to copy a writer but to add their moves to your toolbox.

## How to track what you learn

Keep a reading notebook: one line on what the piece did, and one on how it did it. Over time you build a personal catalogue of craft techniques. When you are stuck, a good move you noticed elsewhere can get you going.

## Common mistakes

Reading only for the story. Reading so fast you miss the craft. Trying to copy a writer's voice directly. Not reading in the genre you want to write. Reading without thinking about what you would do differently.

## Practical tips

- Reread a favourite piece and annotate what it does.
- Read one thing a week like a writer.
- Keep a craft notebook.

## Checklist
