Most rate guides give you one of two things: a survey of what freelancers *say they charge*, or a list of what magazines *say they pay*. Those are different numbers, and the gap between them is where writers get confused.

This guide puts both side by side. The survey data comes from the Editorial Freelancers Association. The market data comes from BRYME's own database of **75 US publications**, every rate read off the publication's own guidelines page.

## What writers charge clients — EFA 2026

The **EFA 2026 Rate Chart** is the most credible US benchmark available. It is based on a survey of over **1,100 members** — more than a third of the EFA's membership — run from November 2025 to mid-January 2026, reporting rates charged during 2025.

| Category | Per word | Per hour |
|---|---|---|
| Medical | $1.00–$1.50 | $90–$126 |
| Scientific | $1.00–$1.20 | $87.50–$113 |
| Technical | 87.5¢–$1.08 | $75–$126 |
| Journalism | 40¢–50¢ | $75–$125 |
| Business/marketing | 32¢–87.5¢ | $65–$75 |
| Articles and essays | 25¢–45¢ | $70–$100 |
| Blog posts | 25¢–45¢ | $74.50–$87.50 |

The EFA is careful to note it neither sets rates nor advises members what to charge, and that individual rates vary with turnaround, expertise, cost of living and experience.

**The headline: medical writing pays roughly four times what blog writing pays per word**, from the same pool of freelancers.

## What publications actually pay — BRYME's 75 US markets

Now the other side. Of 75 verified US publications:

- **43 publish a per-piece figure**
- **14 quote a per-word rate**
- **18 confirm they pay but publish no figure**

Among those with a per-piece figure:

| | Amount |
|---|---|
| Lowest | **$5** |
| 25th percentile | **$40** |
| **Median** | **$100** |
| 75th percentile | **$300** |
| Highest | **$2,500** |

**The median US publication in BRYME's database pays $100 per accepted piece.**

The per-word rates publications actually publish cluster far below EFA's client-work figures: 8¢, 10¢, 14¢ per word are typical for fiction markets; 40–50¢ and $1/word appear only at the top end of reported journalism.

### The top of the market

- **WIRED** — $2,500+ per feature
- **The Drift** — $2,000 essays, $500–$1,000 stories, $150 poems
- **Earth Island Journal** — $500 short, $750–$1,500 long
- **New Lines Magazine** — $600–$800
- **Longreads** — from 50¢ per word
- **Rest of World** — $1/word

## The gap, and what it means

Put the two datasets together and the picture is uncomfortable but useful:

> **EFA respondents charging clients 25–45¢ a word for articles are being paid better than the median publication pays.**

A 1,500-word piece at the EFA's low end for articles (25¢) is $375. The median BRYME-verified US publication pays $100 for a piece.

This is not a contradiction. They are two different markets:

**Client work** — content for a business, agency or brand. You set the rate, you invoice, the money is predictable. This is what the EFA survey measures.

**Publication work** — a magazine commissions or accepts your piece at *their* rate card. You do not set the price. This is what BRYME's database measures.

**The practical consequence: if you need income, client work pays more reliably. If you want a byline, credibility and clips, publication work buys those.** Most working writers do both, and the mistake is expecting one to behave like the other.

## What this should change about your pricing

**Stop quoting a per-word rate to clients if you are fast.** Per-word pays you for length. At $80/hour and 500 finished words an hour, 16¢/word is the same money — but if you get faster, per-word pays you less for the same result. See [per word, day rate, or project fee](/learn/freelance-paid-writing/per-word-day-rate-or-project-fee/).

**Specialise, because the survey says it is the single biggest lever.** Medical at $1.00–1.50/word against blog posts at 25–45¢ is a four-fold difference driven entirely by subject, not by skill. The [highest-paying niches guide](/learn/freelance-paid-writing/high-paying-writing-niches/) covers where those subjects are.

**Treat the $100 median as the floor to beat, not the target.** A quarter of BRYME's verified US markets pay $300 or more. They are listed, with the rate on every card.

**Do not accept "we pay competitive rates" without a number.** Eighteen of the 75 US publications BRYME tracks confirm they pay and publish no figure. That is normal, and it is also the point at which you ask.
