Citing sources is how you give credit and let readers verify your work. It is not a formality — it is the backbone of honest academic and factual writing.

## What it is

A citation tells the reader where a fact, idea, quote or statistic came from. It appears in the text (in-text citation) and in a full list at the end (references or bibliography). The exact format depends on the style you are using.

## Why it matters

It goes hand-in-hand with avoiding plagiarism, lets a reader check your claims, and shows the sources you built your argument on. Missing or wrong citations undermine credibility.

## The common styles

- **APA** (psychology, education, social sciences): in-text "(Smith, 2020)" and a "References" list.
- **MLA** (literature, humanities): in-text "(Smith 23)" and a "Works Cited" list.
- **Chicago** (history, many humanities): either author-date in text or footnotes, with a bibliography.
- **Harvard** (common in business and many UK institutions): author-date like APA, with a reference list.

Pick the style your field or publication requires and apply it consistently.

## How to cite in text

For a direct quote, use quotation marks and a citation with a page number if available. For a paraphrased idea (restated in your own words), cite the source but no page number is usually required. Always cite the idea's origin, not just direct quotes.

## What to include in a reference

For a book: author, title, publisher, year. For a journal article: author, title, journal, volume, pages, year. For a webpage: author or site, title, site name, date, URL. Exactly which parts and the order depend on your style — follow the guide for your field.

## How to build a reference list

List every source you actually cited, in alphabetical order by author. Do not list sources you did not use. Keep the style's exact punctuation and italics rules consistent.

## How to avoid common mistakes

Do not cite a source you did not read (secondary citation — if you read it quoted somewhere, say "as cited in"). Do not use a citation tool you have not checked. Do not drop citations you are unsure of. Use an identifier like a DOI or URL so the reader can find the source.

## Practical tips

- Record every source's details as you use it, not at the end.
- Keep a reference manager or a simple note file.
- Double-check the specifics of your style before submitting.

## Checklist

- [ ] I used the style my field or publication requires.
- [ ] I cited every direct quote and paraphrase.
- [ ] My in-text citations match my reference list.
- [ ] I only listed sources I actually used.
- [ ] I included enough to find each source (author, year, and DOI/URL/page).
- [ ] I applied the style consistently.
