Writers who track every payment spot the patterns that change businesses. Writers who do not are guessing — at what to charge, whether this month was good, and which client is quietly becoming half their income.

**What gets measured gets negotiated.** A writer who knows last quarter's average per piece, and per client, negotiates from data. One who does not negotiates from hope — and hope prices low.

**Log four things, nothing more.** Client, amount, date, status — paid, invoiced or pending. That is the whole ledger. It takes under a minute per payment and it produces the four numbers that matter: the year total, this month, the average per piece, and what is outstanding.

**Respect the pending column.** Invoiced money is earned money you cannot touch or spend. Watching that number is how you notice a client quietly going slow — thirty days becoming sixty — while there is still time to ask politely. The pending column is early-warning radar for cash flow.

**Watch client concentration.** When one client passes half your paid income, you no longer have a client; you have an employer with none of an employer's obligations. The fix is deliberate, not dramatic: keep pitching new markets while the relationship is good, and treat the concentration flag as a deadline, not a statistic.

**The monthly review is the actual habit.** Once a month, look at the four numbers for ten minutes. Is the average per piece trending up? Is any client's share creeping? Does the set-aside pot match what the tracker says you were paid? Ten honest minutes a month is the entire difference between a freelance business and a slot machine.

**Back it up.** A ledger that lives only in a browser is one cleared cache away from gone. Export to CSV now and then and keep the file with your tax records — your future self, assembling figures for a tax form or a loan application, will be grateful.
