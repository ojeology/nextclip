A product description's job is to help a shopper decide. It is not a list of specifications — it is a short, clear pitch that connects what a product does to something the buyer wants.

## What it is

A product description is the copy on a product page that explains what the product is, what it does, and why it is worth buying. It should be useful, honest, and easy to scan.

## Why it matters

Shoppers decide fast. A clear description that answers "is this for me and does it solve my problem?" converts. A vague or confusing one loses the sale.

## How to write it

**Name what it is and what it does in one line.** "A stainless water bottle that keeps drinks cold for 24 hours" beats "Premium hydration solution."

**Lead with the benefit, support with the feature.** A feature is what it is; a benefit is what it does for the buyer. "1-litre capacity" is a feature. "Carry enough water for the whole day, no refills" is the benefit. Pair them.

**Be specific.** Numbers and specifics build trust: "12-hour cold retention," "1,000 mAh," "fits standard cup holders." Vague adjectives ("amazing") do not persuade.

**Describe it to a person, not a catalogue.** Write for the shopper reading it. Say "you" and focus on what they will experience.

**Use scannable formatting.** Short paragraphs, bullet points for features, and a clear opening line. Most buyers skim.

**Be honest.** Do not overclaim or list features the product does not have. Honesty builds trust and avoids returns.

**End with a reason to act.** A line on why it is worth it now, or what sets it apart. Not a pressure tactic — a genuine differentiator.

## Typical structure

1. **One-line description** (what it is + what it does).
2. **Key benefit** and why it matters.
3. **Features** as a short bulleted list.
4. **Who it is for.**
5. **What makes it different.**

## Common mistakes

Listing specs with no benefit. Writing copy filled with adjectives but no facts. Being vague about what it actually does. Writing for everyone so it convinces no one. Overpromising. A description that is a wall of text.

## Practical tips

- Write the benefit first, then back it with a specific feature.
- Read it out loud; it should sound helpful, not salesy.
- Cut any word a buyer does not need.
