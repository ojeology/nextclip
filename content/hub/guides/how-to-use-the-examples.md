Every example in this section is a complete, finished piece followed by a breakdown of how it is built. They are here to be copied in structure, not in words. This guide explains the best way to use them.

## Why examples work

You can be told how to write an essay a hundred times and still not know what one looks like. Reading a finished piece shows you the shape — the opening, the order, the ending — in a way no rule list can. Once you have seen the shape, writing it yourself is far easier.

## How to read them like a writer

**Read the whole piece once, straight through.** Let it land as a finished thing before you pick it apart. Get a feel for it.

**Then read the breakdown.** Each example explains the structure: what the opening does, why each section is ordered that way, and what the ending accomplishes.

**Notice the shape, not the subject.** The example essay is about financial literacy, but the structure applies to any argument. Copy the structure; change the topic and the words to your own.

**Identify the move that makes it work.** Ask: what is the one thing this piece does that makes it good? Is it the strong thesis, the concrete detail, the call to action? That move is what you borrow.

## How to use an example in your own writing

1. **Pick the closest form.** If you are writing a blog post, read the blog post example; if you are arguing a point, read the essay example.
2. **Map its structure onto your subject.** Write out the skeleton — opening, sections, ending — and fill it with your own content.
3. **Write, then leave it and return.** The first draft is yours; the structure is a guide, not a cage.
4. **Edit for your own voice.** Do not copy the example's words. Adapt them until the piece sounds like you.

## The one rule

**Never copy the example's words — copy its structure.** An example is a model to imitate in shape, not a template to paste. Change the topic, the detail, and the voice to your own, and the structure becomes yours.

## Common mistakes

Copying the example's actual sentences. Reading the breakdown before the piece. Trying to fit your subject into an example's exact wording. Expecting the example to be the only way to write the form.
