The UK quotes writing rates in two units the rest of the world does not use much: **a day rate** for commercial work, and **per 1,000 words** for editorial. Getting those straight is most of the job.

Here is what both actually are, from survey data and from 21 UK publications BRYME has verified against their own guidelines.

## Commercial work — the day rate

The **ProCopywriters annual survey** is the clearest UK benchmark. Its 2025 edition, based on **474 freelance copywriters**, put the average day rate at **£480**, up £40 on the previous year. The 2026 survey reports a **median of £440** — the difference is the usual mean-versus-median effect, with a few very high rates lifting the average.

Broken down by experience and specialism, as reported in market analyses drawing on that survey:

| Segment | Day rate |
|---|---|
| Junior (0–2 years) | £250–£350 |
| Mid-level (3–7 years) | £400–£600 |
| Senior (8+ years) | £500–£800 |
| **Financial services and fintech** | **£600–£850** |
| Technology and software | £450–£750 |
| Specialist experts | £800–£2,000+ |
| Charity and non-profit | £250–£375 |

Hourly, experienced copywriters report **£50–£100**.

For scale: a wider analysis of UK freelance contracts put the overall market day rate at **£390**, with the top 10% at around **£708**.

**The detail worth noticing:** rates rose in a year when almost everyone expected AI to push them down. What fell was the price of generic writing. Strategy, positioning and accountability did not.

## Editorial work — per 1,000 words, and much lower

Now the other market. Of BRYME's **21 verified UK publications**:

- **13 publish a per-piece figure**
- **1 quotes a straight per-word rate**
- **7 confirm they pay but publish no figure**

Among those with a figure:

| | Amount |
|---|---|
| Lowest | **£20** |
| 25th percentile | **£20** |
| **Median** | **£35** |
| 75th percentile | **£150** |
| Highest | **£375** |

**The median UK publication in BRYME's database pays £35 per accepted piece.**

### The actual range

- **Pellicle** — £375 feature, £525 long read, £675 photo essay
- **Extra Teeth** — £200 print, £150 Substack
- **Wasafiri** — typically £150
- **Poetry Wales** — £150 per 3,000 words for features
- **Climate Home News** — from £0.35 a word
- **Gutter** — flat £50
- **Poetry London** — £35 per poem
- **The Fiction Desk** — £25 per 1,000 words
- **Shoreline of Infinity** — £20 per 1,000 words
- **The Rialto** — £20 per poem

## The two numbers side by side

> **£480 a day for commercial copywriting. £35 for a poem in a literary magazine.**

Both are current, both are accurate, and a writer who confuses them will make bad decisions for years.

The uncomfortable arithmetic: **one day of financial-services copywriting at £600 is worth roughly seventeen poems at the median UK literary rate.**

That is not an argument against literary publication. Bylines in Granta, Poetry London or The Rialto buy credibility, an agent's attention and a body of work — things a day rate never buys. It is an argument against expecting them to pay rent.

**The pattern most working UK writers land on:** commercial work funds the week, editorial work builds the career. Deciding which a given piece is *before* you take it prevents most of the resentment.

## Understanding the per-1,000 convention

UK editors quote per 1,000 words more often than per word. Converting is trivial but the psychology is not: **£25 per 1,000 words is 2.5p a word**, which sounds much worse and is the same money. Do the conversion before you agree, not after.

Also British: **submission fees are normal at some literary markets.** Of the UK publications BRYME tracks, several charge to submit — The Fiction Desk £5, Granta £3.50 with 200 free low-income places per window, The London Magazine £3.50 outside its free quota, Hinterland £3, Magma £3 for non-subscribers. Charging a reading fee is not a scam signal in UK literary publishing, but it is a real cost to weigh against a £25 rate.

## What to do with this

**Quote a day rate for commercial work, not per word.** It is the unit UK clients expect, it is what the survey measures, and it protects you from being paid less as you get faster.

**Do not negotiate literary rates.** They are fixed rate cards, usually funded by grants. The lever is choosing better-paying markets, not haggling — and BRYME lists [21 verified UK publications](/writing-opportunities/united-kingdom/) with the rate on each.

**Specialise if you want the day rate to move.** £600–£850 for financial services against £250–£375 for charity work is a subject difference, not a seniority one. See [finance and insurance copywriting](/learn/freelance-paid-writing/finance-and-insurance-copywriting/).

**Check your own floor first** with the [rate calculator](/tools/freelance-rate-calculator/). Most UK writers discover their instinctive quote sits well below £480, and that gap is the cheapest raise available.
