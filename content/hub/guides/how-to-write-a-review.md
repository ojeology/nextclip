A review tells a reader whether something is worth their time or money — and why. The best reviews are honest and specific, helping the reader decide for themselves rather than just saying it is "good" or "bad."

## What it is

A review is an evaluation of a product, book, film, album, place, or experience. It combines description (what it is) with judgment (how well it does what it sets out to do).

## Why it is used

To help a reader decide. A good review saves the reader time and money and gives them enough to make up their own mind.

## Who uses it

Critics, customers, bloggers, and everyday people. Reviews shape buying, reading, and watching decisions everywhere.

## Typical structure

1. **What is being reviewed** and what it is, in one or two lines.
2. **Who it is for** — the reader or audience it suits.
3. **What worked** — the strengths, with specifics.
4. **What did not work** — the weaknesses, with specifics.
5. **Overall verdict** — a fair summary and a rating.

## How to write it

**Actually experience it.** Review the thing for what it is, grounded in real use. Do not review a book you have not read.

**Describe before you judge.** Tell the reader what it is and how it works before you say whether it is good.

**Be specific with evidence.** "The pacing dragged in the second act" beats "It was slow." Specifics make a review credible and useful.

**Judge it against its intent.** A children's book is not a failure for being simple. Judge what it set out to do.

**Be honest, not just kind or harsh.** A good review is fair. Acknowledge what worked even if the verdict is negative, and vice versa.

**State who it is for.** The most useful line in a review is often "If you like X, this is for you; if you prefer Y, it is not."

**Give a clear verdict and rating.** End with a definite recommendation, so the reader knows what to do.

## Common mistakes

Giving away the whole plot or ending. Being vague ("it was good"). Reviewing the thing for what you wanted it to be, not what it is. Being cruel for effect. No real verdict — a review that never says "worth it or not."

## Practical tips

- Write the verdict last and let the evidence support it.
- Compare to similar items when it helps the reader.
- Keep it specific and concrete, not generic praise or complaint.

## Checklist

- [ ] I genuinely experienced the thing.
- [ ] I describe it before judging it.
- [ ] I gave specific evidence for strengths and weaknesses.
- [ ] I judged it against its own intent.
- [ ] I stated who it is for.
- [ ] I ended with a clear verdict.
