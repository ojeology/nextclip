What you actually get for each plan

Nigerian streaming in 2026 splits into three honest tiers: global giants (Netflix, Prime Video), the local heavyweight (Showmax), and free live TV. Prices below are estimates as of August 2026 — promos change monthly, so confirm on each official page before paying.

Choose by what you actually watch

FIRST, FOOTBALL: the Showmax mobile plan is built around the Premier League and undercuts every full sports package. StarTimes carries Bundesliga, Serie A, Ligue 1 and the World Cup — but not the EPL.

First, Nollywood and reality TV: Showmax again, with Netflix’s growing Naija originals as the second pick.

First, international series: Netflix + Prime Video together cover most of what Nigerians argue about online.

First, free: YouTube’s official channels, NTA, Channels TV and WAP TV cover a full week of viewing at zero cost — if you do not mind ads and being a week behind.

Northern and diaspora audiences

AREWA24 On Demand streams Hausa dramas and Kannywood films on an inexpensive subscription — a lane the global apps barely touch.

The BRYME rule of thumb

One football plan + one global plan is the sweet spot for most Nigerian households; everything else is optional. Before renewing anything, check what you actually watched last month — subscription creep is how ₦15,000 quietly leaves your account every month.

Desk notes: a framework that outlives the prices

Added September 2026 by the desk — the archive piece above stands as recovered; these notes go deeper.

Prices and catalogues rotate; the byline says so and means it. The durable part of this page is the evaluation framework, which works whatever the current numbers are. Four questions, in the order that matters for Nigerian households. One: total monthly cost against your data budget — and whether the app has a real download tier, because offline viewing changes the maths completely for mobile-first homes. Two: device footprint — phone-only apps and smart-TV apps are different products wearing the same name. Three: catalogue depth in what you actually watch, not library size; a hundred thousand titles you will never play beats nothing only slightly. Four: payment friction — card declines, renewal failures and naira repricing decide whether a subscription survives month two.

Then trial properly. Do not browse the library during a free tier — pick one title you genuinely want, and confirm it plays on your actual device before money moves. Browsing tells you what the service sells; one full playback tells you what it delivers where you live.

And the house rule, restated because it never changes: this desk writes about legal services only. No piracy links, no “unofficial app” winks, anywhere in the family.
