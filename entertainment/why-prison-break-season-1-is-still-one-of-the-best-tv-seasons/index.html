Editorial / Why Prison Break Season 1 Is Still One of the Best TV Seasons
TV Series

I didn't even want to watch Prison Break at first. Then I spent my free MTN data on it — and Michael Scofield's tattooed escape plan made it the one American series I still put above everything else.

By Ibrahim Sodiq · Editorial guide · Reading time: about 5 minutes

I didn't even want to watch Prison Break at first.

That probably sounds strange considering how highly I rate it now.

But I've always had this problem with heavily hyped movies and series. When everybody keeps telling me something is the greatest thing ever, I start expecting it to be disappointing.

Prison Break was one of those shows.

Then one day, I had some free MTN data and thought, basically, why not waste it?

So I downloaded Prison Break.

That decision completely changed my opinion.

Michael Scofield's Plan Was Crazy

The premise alone is enough to make you curious.

Michael Scofield deliberately gets himself sent to prison because his brother, Lincoln Burrows, is facing execution for a crime Michael believes he didn't commit.

But Michael doesn't simply walk into prison hoping to figure things out later.

He already has a plan.

And the craziest part?

The prison is tattooed across his body.

When I first saw that, I was thinking:

How the hell did this guy even plan all of this?

The tattoo isn't just there to make Michael look cool.

It's part of a much larger plan.

Every piece has a purpose, and slowly you begin to understand why he went to such extreme lengths before entering the prison.

That was the moment Prison Break stopped feeling like just another prison series to me.

The Prison Was Supposed to Be Impossible to Escape

Fox River isn't presented as somewhere you can simply walk out of.

It's a prison designed to keep people inside.

That's what makes Michael's plan so interesting.

He's not trying to escape from an empty building.

He's trying to escape from a place where guards, locked doors, schedules, surveillance and other prisoners are constantly working against him.

And then there are the unexpected problems.

Because Michael can plan almost everything, but he can't control everybody.

Everyone Has Their Own Game

One of the things I loved about Season 1 was the way the prison felt like its own world.

Michael has his plan.

But other prisoners have their own plans.

Some want freedom.

Some want revenge.

Some want money.

Some want to manipulate Michael.

Others simply want to survive.

So the escape isn't just one man's master plan.

It's a collection of different agendas constantly crashing into each other.

And that makes the story unpredictable.

You can understand what Michael is trying to do and still have no idea whether he's actually going to pull it off.

The Conspiracy Makes Everything Bigger

At first, you might think the story is simply about getting Lincoln out of prison.

Then you realize there is something much bigger happening.

The situation surrounding Lincoln's conviction leads into a conspiracy that makes Michael's escape plan even more complicated.

Suddenly, escaping isn't enough.

There are people outside the prison who want certain things to happen.

There are people inside the prison who have their own interests.

And Michael has to keep adapting.

That's what makes the series so gripping.

Season 1 Makes You Want the Next Episode

This is where Prison Break absolutely destroyed my expectations.

Once I started watching Season 1, I didn't want to stop.

There was always another problem.

Another obstacle.

Another piece of the plan that needed to be solved.

You finish one episode thinking you'll take a break.

Then you remember that Michael still has something to do.

So you watch another.

Then another.

And before you know it, it's late at night.

That's the kind of series Prison Break was for me.

It wasn't something I casually watched.

It pulled me in.

Why I Still Put It Above Breaking Bad

This is probably where people will disagree with me.

I know Breaking Bad is considered one of the greatest American television series ever made.

I've watched it.

I've genuinely tried to understand the hype.

But it never reached the level Prison Break reached for me.

That's not saying Breaking Bad is badly made.

It's simply about what grips me as a viewer.

Prison Break had me constantly asking:

How are they going to get out of this?

And that question kept me watching.

For me, no American series I've watched has beaten that feeling.

Don't Make the Mistake I Made

There's one thing I learned the hard way while watching Prison Break.

There is an additional movie that connects to the later story.

I didn't know that.

So when I finished one part of the series and jumped straight into the next section, I became completely confused.

For a while, I couldn't understand what had happened.

Then I found out that I had skipped an important piece of the story.

I went back and watched it.

Suddenly everything made sense.

So if you're watching Prison Break for the first time, don't randomly skip the additional story just because it isn't another normal season.

The extra movie matters to the overall experience.

Why Season 1 Still Works

Prison Break Season 1 has something that is surprisingly difficult to create.

It makes you believe that the plan might actually work while constantly giving you reasons to think it won't.

Michael is intelligent, but he's not in complete control.

The prison has rules.

The prisoners have their own agendas.

The conspiracy keeps expanding.

And every step toward freedom creates another problem.

That's why the tattoo remains one of the most memorable ideas in the series.

It wasn't just a tattoo.

It was the blueprint for a plan that had been built before Michael ever stepped through the prison gates.

And that's why, after all these years and all the hype surrounding other American series, my answer hasn't changed:

For me, Prison Break is still the one to beat.

Editorial / Why Prison Break Season 1 Had Me Watching All Night
TV Series / Opinion

A personal TV series opinion about Prison Break Season 1 — Michael Scofield's insane plan, the tattoos, and the show that made me stay awake all night.

Editorial guide · Reading time: about 3 minutes

For years, people told me to watch Prison Break.

I ignored them.

I didn't understand why everybody was so obsessed with Michael Scofield. At the time, I didn't even know who Michael Scofield was.

Then one day I decided to give the show a chance.

I told myself I would just watch a little.

That was a mistake.

Because once I started Season 1, I couldn't stop.

Michael Scofield Wasn't Trying to Escape Prison

The crazy part about Prison Break is that Michael Scofield didn't accidentally end up in prison.

He deliberately got himself arrested.

His brother Lincoln Burrows was facing execution, and Michael believed his brother had been wrongly convicted.

So Michael created an insane plan.

He would get himself inside the same prison.

Then he would break his brother out.

Think about how crazy that is.

Most people would do everything possible to stay out of prison.

Michael did the opposite.

He put himself there because it was the only way he could save his brother.

Fact note: Prison Break premiered on Fox in 2005 and ran for four seasons (2005–2009), with a revival season in 2017. Wentworth Miller plays Michael Scofield, who gets himself imprisoned at Fox River to break out his brother Lincoln (Dominic Purcell).

The Tattoos Were Insane

One of the things that completely hooked me was Michael's tattoo.

It wasn't just a normal tattoo.

It contained information connected to his escape plan and the prison itself.

Michael had essentially turned his own body into part of the blueprint.

That was the moment I realized this wasn't going to be a normal prison series.

This guy had been planning everything.

The routes.

The prison structure.

The people.

The risks.

The escape.

And the crazy thing was that even after all that planning, things kept going wrong.

I Was Watching All Night

Season 1 had me locked in.

I kept thinking:

"Okay, I'll watch one more episode."

Then another episode started.

Then another.

Before I knew it, I had spent most of the night watching Prison Break.

That's what I mean when I say a series has to hook me.

I don't need explosions every five minutes.

I need a reason to desperately know what happens next.

Prison Break gave me that.

Michael Scofield Became One of My Favourite Characters

What makes Michael interesting isn't simply that he's intelligent.

It's that he is willing to sacrifice himself for his brother.

He doesn't just say he loves his brother.

He creates an almost impossible plan and walks directly into prison to prove it.

That's what made the character memorable to me.

Why I Still Recommend Prison Break First

Whenever someone asks me which American series they should watch, Prison Break is still one of the first names I give them.

I've watched Breaking Bad.

I've watched Marvel.

I've watched plenty of other American series.

But Prison Break Season 1 created a feeling that very few shows have created for me.

It made me want to stay awake just to see what would happen next.

And that's why, for me, Prison Break remains special.

Not because everyone has to agree that it's the greatest series ever.

But because it was one of the first shows that made me completely forget I was watching television.

I was simply trying to find out whether Michael Scofield would actually pull it off.
