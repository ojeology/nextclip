Writing by country

What changes when you cross a border.

Most writing advice online is quietly American, or quietly British, and never says so. That is a problem if you are applying for a job in Lagos, submitting to a journal in Melbourne, or writing to a client in Toronto. This section collects the conventions that actually differ by country — the ones that make a reader think "this wasn't written for us" without being able to say why.

The rule: Match the destination, not your habit. Whatever variety you learned, the rule for any single piece of writing is the same: pick the convention of the place it is going, and apply it consistently from the first word to the last. A document that mixes -ise and -ize, or writes 03/04 without saying which month it means, costs the reader attention you needed for your argument.

Spelling

Dates and numbers

The single most dangerous convention gap in international writing, because both readings look correct.

United StatesUK & CommonwealthNotes and other regions
Numeric dates03/04/2026 means 4 March03/04/2026 means 3 AprilThere is no way for a reader to tell which you meant. Letters and formal address

Punctuation after a salutation, and how a letter closes, both differ — and both are visible at a glance.

United StatesUK & CommonwealthNotes and other regions

TitlesMr., Mrs., Ms., Dr. — with full stopsMr, Mrs, Ms, Dr — no full stopsThe UK omits the stop when the abbreviation ends with the word's last letter. The Commonwealth follows. Consistency matters more than the choice.

Salutation punctuation

US: Dear Ms Okafor: — colon in business letters

UK & Commonwealth: Dear Ms Okafor, — comma

The US colon is genuinely formal usage, not an error. A comma is safe almost everywhere; a colon in a UK or Commonwealth letter looks slightly foreign.

Titles

US: Mr., Mrs., Ms., Dr. — with full stops

UK & Commonwealth: Mr, Mrs, Ms, Dr — no full stops

The UK omits the stop when the abbreviation ends with the word's last letter. The Commonwealth follows. Consistency matters more than the choice.

Unknown recipient

US: Dear Hiring Manager, or Dear Sir or Madam

UK & Commonwealth: Dear Sir or Madam, closing with Yours faithfully

In UK convention the close depends on the opening: a named recipient takes Yours sincerely, an unnamed one takes Yours faithfully. This pairing is still taught and still noticed. Find the name if you possibly can — it beats both.

Closing

US: Sincerely, / Best regards, / Regards,

UK & Commonwealth: Yours sincerely, / Yours faithfully, / Kind regards,

Nigeria, Kenya, Ghana, India and Pakistan generally follow the UK forms, often somewhat more formally than the UK now does.

Formality level

US: Warmer and quicker to first names, even in a first approach

UK & Commonwealth: More reserved on first contact; first names follow the recipient's lead

Nigerian, Indian and Kenyan business correspondence tends to be more formal than either, and titles carry real weight. Erring formal costs you nothing; erring casual can cost you the reply.

Address block

US: Recipient address top left, then date

UK & Commonwealth: Sender address top right, recipient left, then date

Largely invisible in email, which is now most correspondence — but it still matters in a formal letter, a legal notice or a scanned attachment.

Punctuation and quotation

Quotation marks and the position of the full stop are the tells that reveal which side of the Atlantic you learned on.

Punctuation and quotation: US and UK conventions compared
United StatesUK & CommonwealthNotes and other regions

Punctuation and quotesInside: she said "maybe," and left.Outside unless part of the quotation: she said 'maybe', and left.The UK rule is logical — only punctuate inside if the punctuation belonged to the original. Quotation marks

US: Double first: "she said"

UK & Commonwealth: Single often preferred: 'she said'

UK usage is genuinely mixed and house styles vary. Doubles are safe everywhere. Whatever you pick, nest the other kind inside.

Punctuation and quotes

US: Inside: she said "maybe," and left.

UK & Commonwealth: Outside unless part of the quotation: she said 'maybe', and left.

The UK rule is logical — only punctuate inside if the punctuation belonged to the original. The US rule is typographic and applies regardless. This is the single clearest marker of which variety a text follows.

Serial (Oxford) comma

US: Standard in most US style guides: red, white, and blue

UK & Commonwealth: Usually omitted: red, white and blue

Named after Oxford University Press, which uses it, in a country that mostly does not. Use it whenever its absence creates ambiguity — that exception is universal.

Abbreviations

US: e.g., i.e., etc. — with stops and often a following comma

UK & Commonwealth: eg, ie, etc — stops increasingly dropped

Both are acceptable within their own conventions. Do not mix them in one document.

Collective nouns

US: Singular: the team is winning

UK & Commonwealth: Often plural: the team are winning

The UK treats a collective as plural when the members act individually. Both are correct in their own variety; neither is an error.

US: Résumé, one page, non-academic

UK & Commonwealth: CV, one to two pages

See the full comparison at /compare/cv-vs-resume/. Nigeria, Kenya, Ghana, India, South Africa, Australia and Ireland all say CV and mean the short document.

Photo

US: Never — employers may discard the application

UK & Commonwealth: Never

Also avoid in Canada, Ireland, Australia and New Zealand. Conventional and often expected in Germany, much of continental Europe, and many employers in Nigeria, India and the Middle East.

Date of birth, marital status, gender

US: Never

UK & Commonwealth: Never

Same across the anglophone West for anti-discrimination reasons. Still routinely requested on application forms in parts of West Africa, South Asia and the Gulf. Supply what the form asks and nothing more.

Referees

US: "References available on request" or omitted entirely

UK & Commonwealth: Two referees named, or available on request

In Nigeria, Kenya and India it is common to name referees with full contact details on the CV itself. Ask permission first, wherever you are.

Grades

US: GPA out of 4.0, included if strong

UK & Commonwealth: Degree classification: First, 2:1, 2:2

Nigeria and India use First Class, Second Class Upper/Lower, or a CGPA out of 5.0, 7.0 or 10.0. Always state the scale — "3.8" means nothing without knowing the maximum.

Personal statement

US: Application essay, prompt-driven, often several short ones

UK & Commonwealth: One UCAS statement, 4,000 characters, sent to every course

A US application essay can be a personal narrative; a UCAS statement must be about the subject. They are not interchangeable.

Directness

US: Get to the idea in the first sentence. No throat-clearing.

UK & Commonwealth: Slightly more preamble tolerated, but not much.

Writers trained in West African, South Asian or East Asian correspondence conventions are often taught to open with courtesy and build to the request. Western editors read the first sentence and decide. Lead with the idea; the courtesy can follow.

Self-presentation

US: State achievements plainly. Understatement reads as having none.

UK & Commonwealth: Plain statement, less superlative. Overselling reads as insecurity.

The gap between "do not boast" as a cultural value and "state your qualifications" as a professional requirement is real. Facts are not boasting: "I have published in three national newspapers" is a fact.

Formality

US: First names quickly. "Dear Sarah" after one exchange is normal.

UK & Commonwealth: Similar, slightly slower.

"Respected Sir/Madam" and "Kindly do the needful" are entirely standard in Indian and Nigerian professional English and are not errors — but to a US or UK editor they signal a template. Use the editor's name.

Local context

US: Explain non-US context; do not assume knowledge.

UK & Commonwealth: Same.

Your local knowledge is an asset, not a liability — it is often the reason you get commissioned. Explain the context in one clause rather than assuming it or apologising for it.

Payment and rights

US: Rates in US$, payment often 30 days after publication.

UK & Commonwealth: Rates in £, similar terms.

Ask which rights you are selling and how payment reaches you. International transfer fees can consume a meaningful share of a small fee — settle the method before you file, not after.

English variety

US: Submit in the publication's variety, consistently.

UK & Commonwealth: Same.

No serious publication rejects work for being written in another variety of English, but a copy-editor will convert it and inconsistency creates work. Set your spellchecker to the destination before your final pass.
