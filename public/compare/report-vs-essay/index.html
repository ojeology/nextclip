Which format do I need?

Report vs essay

Both are long, formal, and assessed. They are built on opposite principles.

Beginner Start here — assumes no prior knowledge.

Students hand in essays when reports were set, and the marks drop for reasons the feedback rarely makes clear. The difference is structural, not tonal. An essay is continuous prose that carries the reader through an argument, and its shape is invisible. A report is a navigable object with numbered sections that a reader can enter anywhere, and its shape is the point.

Essay

One argument, sustained from first line to last.

Typical length: Set by the brief; continuous prose throughout

Who reads it: Reads from the beginning to the end, in order

Voice: Formal but flowing. Paragraphs linked by transitions.

Shape: Introduction with a thesis, body paragraphs each advancing it, conclusion. No headings in most disciplines.

Use it when: Humanities, social sciences, philosophy, literature — anywhere the question begins "Discuss", "Evaluate" or "To what extent".

Report

Findings, organised so a reader can find any one of them.

Typical length: Set by the brief; broken into labelled sections

Who reads it: Skims the contents page and jumps to the section they need

Voice: Formal and impersonal. Short paragraphs. Bullets where bullets are clearer.

Shape: Title page, contents, executive summary, introduction, method, findings, discussion, conclusion, recommendations, references, appendices.

Use it when: Business, engineering, science, nursing, law, and any brief that says "Investigate", "Analyse the data" or "Recommend".

Decide quickly

Which one do you actually need?

  • The brief says discuss, argue, evaluate, or to what extent → essay.
  • The brief says investigate, analyse, assess, or recommend → report.
  • You collected or were given data → report.
  • You are working entirely from reading and reasoning → essay.
  • The reader needs to act on it afterwards → report.
  • Headings would break the flow → essay.
  • The reader might read only one section → report.

Common confusions

The questions that come up every time.

Can an essay have headings?

In the sciences and in most professional writing, yes and often should. In English, history and philosophy, headings usually read as a failure to build transitions. Follow your department's marking guide over any general rule.

Does a report need recommendations?

Only if the brief asks for them — but check carefully, because "what should the company do" is a request for recommendations even when the word never appears. A report that stops at findings when the reader needed a decision has done half the job.

Which is harder?

Reports are easier to structure and harder to write well, because the format lets you hide vagueness inside headings. Essays are harder to structure and easier to write well, because a weak argument has nowhere to hide.
