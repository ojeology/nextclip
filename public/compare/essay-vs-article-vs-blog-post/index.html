Which format do I need?

Essay vs article vs blog post

Three formats that look similar on the page and behave completely differently. How to tell which one you actually need to write.

Beginner Start here — assumes no prior knowledge.

These three get confused more than any other formats on BRYME. All three are prose, all three can be about the same subject, and a beginner told to "write something about climate change" genuinely cannot tell which is wanted. The difference is not length or topic. It is who decides what counts as finished: an essay is judged on your reasoning, an article is judged on your reporting, and a blog post is judged on whether it was useful to the reader who landed on it.

Essay

An argument you build and defend yourself.

Typical length: 500–5,000 words, set by whoever assigned it

Who reads it: A marker, an examiner, or a reader who has chosen to think alongside you

Voice: Considered. First person allowed in personal and literary essays, usually avoided in academic ones.

Shape: Thesis, then evidence in a deliberate order, then a conclusion that has actually moved somewhere.

Use it when: You are being assessed on how you think, or you want to work out a position in public.

Article

Information gathered from outside yourself and organised for a reader.

Typical length: 600–3,000 words, set by the publication

Who reads it: A publication's existing audience, arriving mid-stream

Voice: The publication's, not entirely yours. Read three of its pieces before you start.

Shape: Lead, context, the substance in descending order of importance, close.

Use it when: You have found something out and someone else's readers should know it.

Blog post

A useful answer to something a specific person is trying to do.

Typical length: 300–2,500 words, set by how long the answer takes

Who reads it: One person who searched a question and clicked your result

Voice: Yours, direct, addressing the reader as "you".

Shape: Answer near the top, then the working. Headings that a skimmer can navigate.

Use it when: You know how to do something and want to be findable by people who don't.

Decide quickly

Which one do you actually need?

  • Someone will grade it → essay.
  • Someone will publish it under their masthead → article.
  • You will publish it yourself → blog post.
  • The value is your reasoning → essay.
  • The value is your reporting → article.
  • The value is the reader finishing a task → blog post.
  • You have to convince a sceptic → essay.
  • You have to interest someone who wasn't looking → article.
  • You have to satisfy someone who was → blog post.

Common confusions

The questions that come up every time.

My lecturer said "write an article" — do they mean a journal article?

Almost certainly, and that is a fourth thing again: an academic paper with an abstract, a literature review, a method and citations. Ask. The word "article" outside journalism usually means "academic paper", and writing a magazine feature instead will cost you badly.

Can a blog post be an essay?

Yes, and the best personal blogs are exactly that. But then you are judged by essay standards — the argument has to hold — while still needing the blog's findability. Do not choose this because it seems easier.

Which one pays?

Articles, mostly. Publications pay for reporting because reporting costs time and access. Essays pay at literary magazines and a few outlets that commission opinion. Blog posts pay when they are yours and something else on the site earns.
