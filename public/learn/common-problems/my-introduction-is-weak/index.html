Common writing problems

My introduction is weak

Open strong with a specific hook, clear subject and a promise worth reading.

Researched and written by BRYME Editorial Desk.

← All Common writing problems guides

A weak introduction usually starts too general, too far back, or with no promise at all. The fix is to get specific fast and tell the reader what they are in for.

What makes an intro weak

Starting with "In today's world" or a dictionary definition. Beginning far back in history. A string of questions. Long setup before the point. No clear sense of what the piece will do.

How to fix it

Cut the general opener. Delete the first sentence or two of throat-clearing and start where the specificity begins.

Open on something concrete. A fact, a number, a scene, a name, or a moment beats an abstraction. "The average newsletter is deleted in three seconds" lands harder than "Newsletters are important."

State what the piece is about early. The reader should know within the first few lines. Do not make them guess.

Make a promise. Tell the reader what they will get: "This guide shows you the one checklist to run before you submit." Concrete promises earn the read.

Keep it short. Two or three sentences is often enough. The intro is a door, not a room.

Write it last, once you know what the piece became. It is far easier to open a piece you have already written.

Practical tips

  • Read the first sentence aloud; if it does not grab, cut it.
  • Give the reader one clear reason to continue.
  • Save your best, most specific line for the top.
