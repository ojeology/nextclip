Editing & proofreading

How to make writing more concise

Cut filler and tighten sentences so your writing says more in fewer, stronger words.

Researched and written by BRYME Editorial Desk.

← All Editing & proofreading guides

Concise writing is not about being short at all costs — it is about removing the words that carry no meaning so the ones that do carry more weight. Every sentence should earn its place.

What concision means

Removing filler, redundancy, weak verbs, and padding so the reader grasps your point with less effort. A concise piece is clear and direct, not thin.

How to cut filler

Remove redundant phrases. "In order to" → "to." "At this point in time" → "now." "Due to the fact that" → "because." "In the event that" → "if." "A large number of" → "many."

Cut intensifiers that do not intensify. "Very," "really," "quite," "absolutely," "truly." Usually the word they modify is enough — and "extremely important" is just "important" about a truly important thing.

Remove redundant pairs. "Each and every," "null and void," "basic fundamentals," "past history." One of the pair is enough.

Cut "there is/there are" openers. "There are several reasons why this matters" → "Several reasons make this matter." Lead with the real subject.

How to tighten sentences

Use strong, active verbs. Replace "made a decision to leave" with "decided to leave." Weak verb-plus-noun phrases are wordier than a single strong verb.

Remove "that" and "which" when not needed. "The report (that) I read." Often safe to drop.

Cut the last word or two. "I think that this is a good idea" → "This is a good idea." "I feel that" and "in my opinion" often add nothing.

Merge sentences. Two short sentences that make one point can often be combined.

Check each sentence for its job. If a sentence repeats what was already said, cut it.

Practical tips

  • After editing, ask of every sentence: does the reader need it?
  • Use the word-density and repetition tools to spot overused words.
  • Read aloud — if you can say the same thing in fewer words, do.
