Creative writing

How to write a short story

Find a story, build characters and conflict, and shape a short piece that hits a single effect.

Intermediate Assumes you can already draft and revise a piece.

Researched and written by BRYME Editorial Desk.

← All Creative writing guides

A short story is a compact fiction built for a single effect. Unlike a novel, it does not have room for many threads — it picks one moment, one change, one feeling and makes it land.

What it is

A short story is a piece of fiction short enough to read in one sitting, centred on one event or change that affects one or a few characters, with a clear point or emotional payoff.

Why it is used

It is the most common entry point for new fiction writers, and one of the most-requested forms in literary magazines and contests. It is a masterclass in doing a lot with a little.

Who uses it

Writers, students, literary magazines, contests, and anthologies.

How to write it

Find the moment, not the whole life. Pick a single scene, an argument, a decision, a discovery. Ask: what is the change, and what is at stake for the character?

Build a character with a want and a flaw. Give your main character something they want, something they are wrong about, and something that makes them feel real. The story is them trying — and the change that results.

**Establish a setting you can write.* A small, concrete place with a few sensory details beats a broad one.

Name the conflict. Something must push back against the character's want: another person, a situation, an inner fear, or circumstances.

Move from normal to change through a turning point. The story begins in an ordinary state, something happens, and the character is different by the end.

Write vivid, purposeful descriptions. One strong, concrete detail is better than a paragraph of general adjectives.

Let dialogue do double duty. Good dialogue advances the story, reveals character, and creates tension — it is not just people talking.

Keep it to one effect. Cut scenes that do not serve the central change, however nice they are.

Example shape

Maya wants to apologise to a friend she hurt (want). She is too proud to do it properly (flaw). At a party, she rehearses, then accidentally hears the friend talking about her (conflict). She lets her pride go and says the true thing (turning point). She leaves changed, no longer carrying it (change).

Common mistakes

Writing a novel's worth of plot into a short space. Explaining feelings instead of showing them. Backstory before the story. Scenes that go nowhere. An ending that does not change anything. Adding a "lesson" at the end that the story refuses to earn.

Practical tips

  • Write the ending you want, then make the start and middle lead to it.
  • Cut the first paragraph on a first revision; short stories often start too early.
  • Trust the reader; you do not have to say what every image means.
  • Read your favourite short story and notice its length, its turning point, and its last line.
