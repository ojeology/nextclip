Freelance & paid writing

What medical writing actually pays

Medical writing rates from the EFA, EMWA and AMWA surveys — per word, per hour, and per project, with what each figure covers.

Intermediate Assumes you can already draft and revise a piece.

Researched and written by BRYME Editorial Desk.

← All Freelance & paid writing guides

Medical writing is the highest-paying category in every freelance rate survey we could find. This guide gives the figures, names the source, and explains what the different numbers are actually measuring — because they are not measuring the same thing.

The headline figures

Editorial Freelancers Association, 2026 Rate Chart

Survey of over 1,100 EFA members, run November 2025 to mid-January 2026, reporting rates charged during 2025.

ServicePer wordPer hourPer page (250w)
Medical writing (work-for-hire)$1.00–$1.50$90–$113$250–$375
Medical ghostwriting$1.00–$1.50$100–$126$250–$375
Scientific writing$1.00–$1.20$87.50–$113$250–$300
Medical developmental editing5.3¢–6.8¢$75–$95$13.13–$16.88
Medical copyediting3.0¢–5.0¢$60–$75$7.50–$12.50

Two things to notice. First, writing pays roughly twenty times what copyediting pays per word in the same specialism — the gap between producing and polishing is enormous here. Second, ghostwriting carries a small premium over work-for-hire on the hourly rate.

EMWA Freelance Business Survey

Published in the European Medical Writers Association's peer-reviewed journal. Overall median hourly rate €82.20 across all services.

By service type:

Consultancy€109

By country: France €92.50 · Germany €82.50 · UK €79.00 · Italy €63.80 · Portugal €57.90 · India €23.00.

The survey also found the median rate climbs with experience, starting around €73 for those with under a year.

AMWA Compensation Survey

The American Medical Writers Association's compensation survey is a members-only report, so treat the following as publicly reported figures rather than something you can verify directly:

  • Employed regulatory writers averaged $166,457
  • Full-time freelancers focused on pharmaceutical regulatory writing averaged $268,847 gross
  • The average hourly rate for freelance regulatory writers was $131.65

Those are the highest figures in this guide, and they come with the biggest caveat. They describe experienced specialists in the most demanding corner of the field, not entry-level work.

Why the numbers disagree so much

You will see medical writing quoted anywhere from $25/hour to $300/hour. The spread is not noise — the surveys are measuring different populations:

Marketplace data is the floor. General freelance platforms report medians around $25–$40/hour. That reflects an open marketplace where clients filter on price.

Association survey data is the professional middle. The EFA at $90–$126/hour and EMWA at €82 median reflect people who have joined a professional body and price accordingly.

Specialist regulatory data is the ceiling. AMWA's $131.65 average for freelance regulatory writers reflects work that requires understanding submission requirements and carries audit consequences.

If you are comparing your own rate against a number you saw online, check which of those three populations it came from first.

How long the work actually takes

Rates per hour are useless without knowing the hours. The EMWA survey measured this directly:

DeliverableMean hours
Clinical study report111
Technical evaluation71
Standard scientific article57
Patient-facing material17.2
Translating a scientific article15.9

A clinical study report at €95/hour and 111 hours is roughly a €10,500 project. That is the real shape of regulatory work: few projects, large each, long timelines.

Patient material at 17.2 hours is the opposite, and it is the most common entry point for writers without a clinical background.

The sub-specialisms, ranked by pay

  1. Regulatory writing — clinical study reports, submission documents, protocols. Highest paid, highest barrier, most process-driven.
  2. Consultancy and training — €109 and €99.90/hour in the EMWA data. Usually reached after years in the field.
  3. Scientific publications — manuscripts for peer-reviewed journals. $1.00–$1.20/word in the EFA chart.
  4. Continuing medical education — materials for clinician training.
  5. Medical communications (MedComms) — agency-side promotional and educational work.
  6. Patient education and health journalism — the widest door, the lowest rates, and the best fit for a strong general writer.

What to charge if you are starting

Do not open with the AMWA number. A defensible approach:

  1. Work out what you need using the [freelance rate calculator](/tools/freelance-rate-calculator/).
  2. Check it against the EFA's medical band ($90–$126/hour).
  3. If you have no medical background, start at or slightly below the low end and move up as you build proof.
  4. Quote per project where you can, using the EMWA hour estimates above to size it — clients in this field are used to project fees and it protects you from your own speed.

An honest caveat

Medical writing is a real profession with real accountability. The rates are high because errors matter. If you are drawn here only by the numbers, start with patient-facing material or health journalism, where the stakes are lower and the learning is public — and build from there.
