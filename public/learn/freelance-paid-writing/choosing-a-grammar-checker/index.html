How to choose a grammar checker (and why "best" is the wrong question)

A grammar checker that does not know your publication's house style will confidently make your work wrong. What to test before you trust one.

Beginner Start here — assumes no prior knowledge.

Researched and written by BRYME Editorial Desk.

← All Freelance & paid writing guides

BRYME has not run these tools as a paying customer over months, so this is not a ranked "best" list. It is the set of tests to run yourself, and the reasons a grammar checker can actively damage professional work.

That framing is deliberate. The honest answer to "which grammar checker is best" is that it depends almost entirely on which variety of English your publication uses, and no reviewer can know that for you.

The failure mode nobody warns about

A grammar checker defaults to American English unless told otherwise. Set it wrong and it will:

  • change colour to color in a piece for a British magazine
  • move your commas inside quotation marks in a publication whose house style puts them outside
  • add a serial comma to a Guardian-style piece that omits it
  • flag whilst, amongst and learnt as errors when they are correct British usage

None of these is a grammar error. All of them are the checker imposing one house style on a piece written for another — and they are exactly what an editor notices first. See [British vs American English](/learn/grammar-language/british-vs-american-english/) and [punctuation and date conventions](/learn/grammar-language/punctuation-and-date-conventions-by-country/).

A grammar checker set to the wrong variety does not catch mistakes. It creates them, confidently.

The five tests to run before you trust one

Run these on a free trial. They take twenty minutes and tell you more than any review.

1. The variety test. Set it to British English. Paste a paragraph with colour, organise, travelled, whilst, 3 April 2026. Does it leave all of them alone? Many tools claim British support and still flag -ise.

2. The quotation test. Write `She said 'maybe', and left.` in British mode. Does it try to move the comma inside? If yes, it does not really understand the convention it claims to support.

3. The voice test. Paste 300 words of your own best writing. Count how many suggestions you would actually accept. If it is flagging every long sentence and every passive construction, it is optimising for blandness, not correctness.

4. The false-confidence test. Write a sentence that is grammatically fine but factually absurd. A checker should have nothing to say. If it rewrites it, it is guessing.

5. The privacy test. Read what the tool does with your text. If you are working under embargo, on an investigation, or under an NDA, a cloud checker that retains your drafts is a genuine professional problem, not a theoretical one.

The AI question, which is now the important one

Many grammar checkers now include AI rewriting alongside correction. That distinction matters enormously and the interface usually blurs it.

Because a growing number of publications ban AI-assisted work outright. Of the publications BRYME has verified, 41 prohibit AI, and the wording is often broad. The Fiction Desk's ban is contractual and explicitly includes grammar and punctuation correction tools. Island's guidelines say plainly: "Please don't send us anything you've written, rewritten or otherwise created using AI."

So before you use any tool with AI features, check the market's policy. A checker that quietly rewrites a sentence has, under some contracts, made your submission a breach. See [the AI ban essay](/essays/the-ai-ban-is-spreading/) for how far this has spread.

If in doubt, use a tool with AI features switched off, and keep the correction and rewriting functions clearly separate in your own workflow.

What actually matters, ranked

  1. Correct variety support — British, American, Canadian, Australian. Everything else is secondary.
  2. A clear line between correcting and rewriting, so you never accidentally do the second.
  3. Restraint. A tool that flags less but is right more is worth more than one that flags everything.
  4. Privacy terms you can live with.
  5. Working where you actually write, rather than requiring copy-paste.
  6. Price, last. The gap between free and paid tiers is usually smaller than the gap between correctly and incorrectly configured.

What no checker does

It does not know facts. It will pass a confidently wrong sentence. It does not know your publication. House style beats any default. It does not replace reading aloud. Nothing catches rhythm problems like your own voice. It does not make dull writing good. It makes wrong writing less wrong.

Free tools that do part of the job

BRYME's own [filler-word finder](/tools/filler-word-finder/), [repetition checker](/tools/word-repetition-checker/) and [word alternatives](/tools/word-alternatives/) run in your browser, send nothing anywhere, and have no AI rewriting to accidentally trigger. They will not replace a full checker, and for the specific jobs they do they are enough.
