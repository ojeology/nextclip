Example of a book review

A complete book review with the structure that helps a reader decide.

Beginner Start here — assumes no prior knowledge.

Researched and written by BRYME Editorial Desk.

← All Examples of writing guides

A good book review tells a reader whether a book is worth their time — without giving the story away. Below is a complete example, then the structure behind it.

The example book review

Title by Author, Publisher, 2026.

[Author]'s novel is a quiet book about a loud thing. It follows [main character], a [description], over the course of a year in which everything they believed about their family quietly falls apart. It is not a story of dramatic events; it is a story of the accumulation of small truths, and that is its strength and its risk.

The prose is its best asset. [Author] writes in sentences that feel considered and unhurried, and the novel's best moments are the ones where nothing much happens — a dinner, a walk, a conversation that turns. In these scenes, the book is genuinely moving, and you understand why the story is told the way it is.

The novel's weakness is its pacing. The middle section lingers on several episodes that make the same point, and — without spoiling anything — the final revelation lands with less force than it should because the book has already suggested it. A more confident editor might have cut a hundred pages and sharpened the whole thing.

Still, this is a novel that rewards patience. If you like deliberate, character-driven literary fiction, this will stay with you. If you prefer fast, plot-driven stories, it may not be for you.

[A verdict: "For readers of X and Y."]

Why this works

It tells you what the book is before judging it. The first paragraph states the premise and the book's approach, so the reader has context for the verdict.

It does not retell the plot. It describes the approach ("accumulation of small truths") without giving away the ending. The plot is a spoiler-free summary, not a synopsis.

It judges the book against its own ambition. The review praises the quiet style and then notes that same style has a cost (pacing). It evaluates the book on its own terms, not on what the reviewer wanted.

It gives specific evidence. "The middle section lingers on several episodes that make the same point" is a claim with a reason — not just "it dragged."

It ends by telling the reader who it is for. "If you like… this will stay with you; if you prefer… it may not be." That is the most useful line in a review.

The structure to copy

  1. State what the book is — premise and approach, in a paragraph.
  2. Comment on the strongest element — prose, pacing, character, etc.
  3. Note a weakness with evidence.
  4. Say who it is for — matching readers to the book.

Common mistakes

Summarising the plot. Spoiling the ending. Vague judgment ("it was good"). Judging a book for what you wanted instead of what it is. No clear recommendation.
