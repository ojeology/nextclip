Example of a professional email

A complete professional email — good and bad versions — with the reasoning explained.

Beginner Start here — assumes no prior knowledge.

Researched and written by BRYME Editorial Desk.

← All Examples of writing guides

Every professional email has one job: to be understood and acted on. Below is a complete, clear example, followed by a weak version and why the good one wins.

The example email (good)

Subject: Question about the Q3 report — deadline

Hi [Name],

I am working on the Q3 report and want to confirm the deadline before I plan the week. Could you let me know if [date] still stands, or if you would prefer it by [date]?

If you need a first draft to review before the final version, I can send one by [earlier date].

Thanks, [Your name]

What makes it work

The subject line is specific and scannable. It tells the reader exactly what the email is about and hints at the action (a deadline question). A busy person can triage it in seconds.

It names the reason at the top. The first line says what the email is about ("the Q3 report") and why it matters ("before I plan the week"). No throat-clearing.

It makes asking easy. It offers two options ("still stands, or you would prefer by…"), so the reader does not have to craft a reply — they just pick.

It is short and action-orientated. A few sentences, clear, and it moves the conversation forward.

It closes politely without a wall of thanks. One word ("Thanks") and the name. Enough, not too much.

The weak version (don't)

Subject: Quick question

Hey,

I hope you're doing well and had a good weekend. Just wanted to touch base re the thing regarding the report. Let me know. Also not urgent but I have a couple of other things to ask about the project when you get a chance, just whenever really!

Thanks, [Your name]

Why it fails

The subject tells the reader nothing. "Quick question" and "re the thing" force the reader to open and guess. It wastes the reader's time.

It buries the point. The actual question is hidden in vague phrasing ("the thing regarding the report"). The reader has to work out what the email is about.

It gives no clear action. "Let me know" and "just whenever really" offer no deadline, no options, and no reason to reply promptly. It asks the reader to figure out what to do.

It is padded. Wishes about the weekend and apologies ("not urgent") are noise. They do not help the reader act.

The template to copy

Subject: [Topic] — [the action you need]

Hi [Name],

I am [context: what you are doing that relates to this]. [State what you need, then make it easy to answer.]

[Optional: one extra helpful line.]

Thanks, [Your name]

Common mistakes

A vague subject line. Burying the point. No clear ask. Padding with politeness. Errors and typos — these read as carelessness. Replying all by mistake.
