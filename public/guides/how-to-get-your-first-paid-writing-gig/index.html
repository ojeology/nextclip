How to get your first paid writing gig

A starting path from writing samples to your first accepted, paid piece.

Researched and written by BRYME Editorial Desk.

In this guide
  1. Write first, publish somewhere
  2. Pick a small, reachable target
  3. Pitch one specific idea
  4. Send under the official channel
  5. Treat rejection as data

The first paid gig is usually small — the point is to get on the board.

Write first. You need something to show. Write a couple of strong pieces and publish them somewhere (a blog, a platform, or a publication that accepts your work).

Pick a small, reachable target. Aim at a modest market that publishes work like yours and that you can genuinely match. BRYME lists many such markets with their pay and word counts.

Pitch one specific idea. Not a general capability, but one angle for one publication.

Use the official channel. An unfamiliar or invented submission address is how opportunities go wrong.

Treat rejection as data. Editors are busy. A pass on one pitch is not a verdict on you as a writer. Note what the market wanted and keep going.
