Contact the desk

Contact BRYME.

Report a correction, a broken link, a closed market or a rights concern — and what to expect back.

How to reach BRYME

Email sodiqibrahim03@gmail.com. It reaches the editor directly — there is no support queue and no form that disappears into nothing.

Reporting an outdated listing

This is the most useful message you can send. Every publication page has a Report an outdated listing link that opens a message already filled in with the publication, the page URL and the date it was last verified.

Please include the official guideline URL if you have it. That turns a recheck from a search into a two-minute confirmation.

What BRYME can help with

  • A record that is out of date, wrong, or points at a dead link.
  • A publication that should be researched and added.
  • A rights or attribution concern.
  • A factual error in a guide.
  • A writing question that BRYME has no guide for yet.

What BRYME cannot do

BRYME is one person, not an agency. BRYME cannot submit your work for you, introduce you to an editor, ask a publication to reconsider a rejection, chase a payment on your behalf, or review your draft privately.

BRYME also cannot tell you whether a specific pitch will be accepted — no one can.

Response time

Corrections are prioritised over everything else. Other messages are read, and answered where there is something useful to say, but a reply is not guaranteed.

How To Reach The Writing Desk

How to reach the desk

The fastest route to the desk is the contact form or published email on this page; both reach the same working inbox. The desk reads every message and answers the ones that need answering, usually within a working week. Corrections and factual notes jump the queue: a message that says a specific page is wrong about a specific thing gets looked at first, because that is how the site gets better.

What to include in a correction

A useful correction names the page, the claim and the source that contradicts it. The desk checks corrections against primary sources — official reports, published rules, dated tariffs — before changing a page, and when the correction holds, the page is fixed with a dated note rather than a silent edit. The reader who sends a good correction is named in the note if they want to be.

What the desk cannot do

The desk cannot give individual professional advice — legal, medical, financial or engineering — and will not pretend otherwise in a reply. It cannot expedite third parties, resolve disputes with companies mentioned on the site, or guarantee a personal response to every message. Product support for items discussed on the site belongs to the manufacturer, and the desk has no channel into it.

Press, partnerships and pitches

Press enquiries and story pitches are welcome through the same route; the desk commissions from working writers and pays standard rates. Marketing approaches are read and almost always declined: the desk does not sell coverage, paid links or dressed-up placements, and a pitch that opens with an offer to pay for a link has already answered its own question. The honest collaboration proposal is one where the desk’s readers genuinely benefit.

This desk specifically

On this site, the writing desk covers craft, workflow and the tools working writers use, with guides written from newsroom and freelance practice rather than content-marketing theory and every tool page stating its own honest limits. The pages on this desk are written by the same editorial process that governs the whole site: sourced claims, dated figures and dated verdicts, applied here to writers-desk subject matter.

The desk-specific pages on this site — the guides, reviews and tools under the writers section — each carry their own method notes and review dates. Where a policy on this page and a practice on an individual page disagree, the individual page’s dated note is the specific record and this page is the general one.

The working list

  • Real inbox, real answers, usually within a working week
  • Corrections jump the queue and are checked against sources
  • No individual professional advice by reply
  • Press and pitches welcome; pay-to-play is not
  • Material corrections logged publicly with dates
  • Named in the correction note if the sender wishes

Background context for this page is cross-checked against the encyclopaedic record where a reference helps. The desk’s standard applies across the archive: named entities carry sources, measured figures carry dates and claims the desk cannot verify are marked rather than rounded. Policies are only as good as their practice, and the practice on this site is public: the pages carry their dates, the corrections carry their notes and the review cycle is a calendar rather than a mood. A reader who finds the practice and the policy disagreeing should write to the desk through the contact page — that message is the most useful one the desk receives, and it is answered.
