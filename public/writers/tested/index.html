Desk notes: what "tested" commits us to

BRYME Tested is the desk's hands-on shelf: tools and services actually used long enough to form an opinion, scored against stated criteria, with the testing date printed. The commitments that make the label mean something: no free gear with strings, no scores previewed to vendors, and a re-test whenever the product changes materially — the same verification discipline the opportunity desk applies to guidelines, applied to objects.

Read the shelf with the desk's standard scepticism tools: the criteria first (stated per review, so you can disagree with the weighting before the verdict), the date second (software rot is real), and the alternative column always — every tested pick names what it displaces. The commercial boundaries are on the disclosure page; when a tested item carries an affiliate link, the label travels with the link.

The testing method, spelled out

Every Tested verdict follows the same protocol, so readers can audit it: the tool or service is used for real work for at least two weeks; the criteria are stated before the verdict, not chosen after; the cost is stated in the currency it charges; the reference standards for the categories come from public sources — Poets & Writers for market context; and the date of the last re-test is printed, because software ages faster than prose. Where the desk has no competence to test a claim (privacy promises, for instance), the review says so instead of repeating marketing.

The shelf's relationship to the rest of the desk is deliberately boring: Tested reviews feed the tools section's recommendations, and any tool that stops earning its place gets re-tested out publicly. Vendors get the same treatment as publications on the opportunity desk — a dated read, a stated standard, and no preview of the verdict. The commercial boundaries around links are on the disclosure page, and they are short on purpose.

What the shelf will not do is equally part of the method: it will not review a category the desk cannot test properly (no secondhand software verdicts from other outlets' screenshots), will not fill space with me-and-my-family anecdotes standing in for criteria, and will not declare a winner where the honest result is "two good options for different people." Where a product changes its pricing or its behaviour materially, the verdict is re-tested rather than patched — the review you read is always the review of the product as it exists, not as it was. That is the whole contract, and it is the same one the opportunity desk makes with its listings: dated, criteria-led, re-checked, and correctable through the contact door when it is wrong.

What Tested Means

BRYME Tested marks opportunities the desk has personally used: submitted, accepted, published or paid, each with dates and the account kept separate from research. The standard is firsthand or nothing, and the label means exactly that.

  • The standard Firsthand experience or the label does not apply.
  • What gets tested Submissions, payments and the actual response behaviour.
  • The dates Every test carries the window it happened in.
  • Kept separate Tested records never blend into researched listings.
  • What failures teach Rejections and unresponsiveness are also recorded.
  • How to read the badge The badge is a claim with a date attached.
