About this tool

How to use the writing structure quiz.

What it does. Eight multiple-choice questions on the mechanics that hold writing together: the lede's real job, the inverted pyramid, the nut graf, three-act structure, show-don't-tell, skimmability, the through-line of meaning, and how professionals start a long piece.

How to use it. Pick an answer for each question - you get the correct answer and a one-line why immediately. Eight questions, about two minutes. Your score comes with a straight verdict, and Try again reruns the ones you missed.

Why it matters. Most writing problems are structure problems wearing a disguise. A piece that 'doesn't flow' usually has an unkept promise in its opening or moves that arrive in the wrong order. Name the mechanics and they become tools instead of luck.

Your data. The tool runs entirely in your browser - text you paste never leaves the page, and nothing is uploaded to a server.

Good to know

Where this fits

Quality tools are the last pass, not the first: they catch what familiarity has made you blind to once the real work is done. Used at the end they are a safety net; used at the start they become a way to avoid writing. Finish the draft first, then let the checks be ruthless on your behalf. The order matters less than the habit: same pass, same checks, every time, so nothing depends on how tired you are.

The Structure Quiz And What It Tests

The structure quiz runs a draft through the questions structural editors actually ask: where the piece is going, what each section does and where the reader needs a signpost the draft forgot to give.

The quiz presents the draft section by section with targeted questions — can the reader state this section’s job in a line, does the ending pay off the opening, where does the evidence sit relative to the claim it supports. The answers build a structural map of the piece with the weak joints marked.

What the tool measures is function, not form: a section that introduces a new idea mid-way through a string of evidence is flagged not for being an idea but for being in the wrong queue. The map makes visible what a linear read hides — the shape the reader actually travels.

The quiz cannot judge whether the structure suits the specific piece’s purpose as well as the writer can. Inverted pyramid, narrative, braided essay — the tool checks internal coherence against the structure the draft declares in its opening, and a deliberately unconventional piece may read as broken to it. The writer remains the arbiter of intent.

The working use is the middle draft: too late for the blank page, early enough to move sections. The map answers the question every writer hits at draft two — is this a structure problem or a prose problem — and knowing which one saves the wrong kind of polishing.

How the tool treats your text

  • Runs in the browser: the text never leaves the page unless the tool says otherwise
  • Free for individual use; bulk and API use by written permission
  • Every result states what was counted and how, beside the result
  • Honest limits published on the page, not buried in a footer
  • Method reviewed and dated on the desk’s editorial calendar

Background context cross-checked against the encyclopaedic record where a reference helps. The desk’s standard applies here as everywhere: the arithmetic is shown, the method is stated and the limits are named. The tool is one instrument on a working writer’s bench: it handles the mechanical layer so the writer’s attention stays on the argument, the evidence and the voice. Everything the tool counts, it counts the same way every time, which is more than any tired eye at the end of a deadline can promise. When the tool and the writer disagree, the writer wins — but the disagreement is worth having, because it means the choice was made rather than missed. The tool runs locally in the page, needs no account and forgets the text when the tab closes, which is the privacy posture a working draft deserves. Reviewed 2026-09-28.
