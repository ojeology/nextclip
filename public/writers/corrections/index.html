Corrections

Corrections.

BRYME's public record of material corrections — and how to report one.

Why this page exists

A resource that never admits an error is not more accurate than one that does — it is just less honest about it. Publications change their rules constantly, and BRYME will sometimes record something wrongly or fail to notice a change. When that happens it is fixed and logged here, with the date.

What counts as a material correction

A correction is logged when the change affects a decision a writer might make:

  • a pay rate that was wrong or has changed;
  • a market recorded as open when it had closed, or the reverse;
  • a broken, moved or wrong official guideline link;
  • an eligibility or rights term recorded incorrectly;
  • anything that misrepresents what BRYME has personally done.

Fixing a typo, tightening a sentence or improving a layout is not a correction and is not logged.

The log

No material corrections have been logged yet. Entries will appear here with the date, the page affected, what was wrong and what it now says. Records are corrected, not quietly deleted.

Report something

Every publication page carries a Report an outdated listing link that opens a pre-filled message to the desk. For anything else, use the contact page. Including the official guideline URL makes a fix much faster.

What a fix looks like

A real correction says what was wrong, what is right now, and when it changed — on the page that made the claim, not in a silent edit. Silent deletions are treated as a failure of the standard, not a way to meet it. If you ever spot a fix that looks silent, that report is as valuable as the first kind.

Corrections And The Corrections Log

The corrections policy

This page lists the desk’s corrections record and explains how corrections work. The policy is simple: when a page is wrong, the page is fixed and a dated note is added at the point of the error. Material errors — the kind that change what a reader would do — are additionally noted at the top of the page. The desk treats a correction as a product improvement, not a confession, and the archive below reflects that.

How a correction is verified

Corrections are checked against the best available primary source before a page changes: the competition’s official record for a sporting figure, the manufacturer’s documentation for a product claim, the published tariff for a cost figure, the study itself for a health claim. Where the sources conflict, the page is updated to say so rather than to pick the convenient one. The verification step is why some corrections take a day rather than a minute.

What is not a correction

A disagreement with a dated judgement is not a correction; the desk revises opinions on new evidence, not on volume. A change in the underlying facts — a price moved, a rule changed — is an update, noted with a new date rather than logged as an error. Style preferences are neither. Keeping these categories straight is what lets the corrections log stay a record of real fixes instead of a complaints box.

The standing commitment

The desk commits to four things: errors are fixed where they stand, notes are dated and visible, material errors are flagged at the page level and every correction that reaches the desk is logged here. The log is reviewed on the editorial calendar alongside the freshness sweep that re-checks dated pages. Readers who send corrections are doing the desk’s quality work and are treated accordingly.

This desk specifically

On this site, the writing desk covers craft, workflow and the tools working writers use, with guides written from newsroom and freelance practice rather than content-marketing theory and every tool page stating its own honest limits. The pages on this desk are written by the same editorial process that governs the whole site: sourced claims, dated figures and dated verdicts, applied here to writers-desk subject matter.

The desk-specific pages on this site — the guides, reviews and tools under the writers section — each carry their own method notes and review dates. Where a policy on this page and a practice on an individual page disagree, the individual page’s dated note is the specific record and this page is the general one.

The working list

  • Errors fixed at the point of error with a dated note
  • Material errors flagged at the top of the page
  • Corrections verified against primary sources
  • Disagreements and updates kept out of the error log
  • Log reviewed on the editorial calendar
  • Senders of good corrections credited on request

Background context for this page is cross-checked against the encyclopaedic record where a reference helps. The desk’s standard applies across the archive: named entities carry sources, measured figures carry dates and claims the desk cannot verify are marked rather than rounded. Policies are only as good as their practice, and the practice on this site is public: the pages carry their dates, the corrections carry their notes and the review cycle is a calendar rather than a mood. A reader who finds the practice and the policy disagreeing should write to the desk through the contact page — that message is the most useful one the desk receives, and it is answered.
