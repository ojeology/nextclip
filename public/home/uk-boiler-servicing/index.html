Boiler servicing: what UK homeowners skip, and what it costs later (UK)

BRYME Home & DIY desk · reviewed 2026-09-09 · general information, not professional advice

In one line: The annual service is cheap, it's the warranty's condition, and it's the CO safety check nobody thinks they need — until the winter it fails.

UK homes run on boilers the way US homes run on furnaces, and the British habit with them is the same as everywhere: ignore the thing until the first cold week of the year, when every heating engineer in the county is booked solid. The annual service is the habit that breaks that cycle — and in most cases it isn't optional at all.

Three reasons the annual service matters

First, warranty: the major manufacturers — Worcester Bosch, Vaillant, Ideal, Baxi, Glow-worm, Viessmann — condition their warranties on an annual service by a Gas Safe registered engineer, recorded in the benchmark service checklist. Miss a year and the cover can be void even for a fault unrelated to the service — with warranties now running five to twelve years, that's real money riding on a roughly hour-long visit. Second, safety: a boiler burns fuel and makes exhaust; the service checks the heat exchanger, flue and combustion, which is exactly the machinery that produces carbon monoxide when it fails — the alarm catches what slips through, the service stops most of it. Third, cost: an annual service is typically advertised around the £60–£125 mark (varies by region and provider — check current local prices), while an emergency winter callout with parts runs several times that, and a failed heat exchanger usually retires the boiler entirely.

A service is not a landlord's gas safety check

This is the most common confusion on the subject. Landlords are legally required (Gas Safety Regulations 1998) to have gas appliances checked annually and to give tenants the record — the CP12. That check is a safety inspection, not a service: booking a CP12 and assuming the boiler has been serviced is, as heating engineers put it, a common and expensive mistake. The two can be done in one visit by the same engineer, and for rental properties that's the sensible arrangement — but they are two jobs, and only one of them keeps the warranty alive. Tenants: you're entitled to the CP12 record, and to a landlord who takes a reported boiler seriously — the duties are tightening across the UK, and boilers are the model for it.

What a proper service includes

A real service — not a glance through the casing — includes: casing off, visual inspection of the flue route, combustion analysis with a flue gas analyser, checks on seals, pressures and the expansion vessel, safety-device testing, a condensate check, and the record updated in the benchmark checklist. Ask for the combustion reading: an engineer who measures and explains beats one who doesn't. Any Gas Safe registered engineer qualified on domestic boilers can do it — registration is the legal requirement for anyone working on gas in the UK (see the alarm-law guide for the enforcement side); you can verify any engineer's card on the Gas Safe Register site.

Timing and the honest bottom line

Book for late summer or early autumn: engineers are available, the weather isn't against you, and a fault found in September is an appointment, while the same fault in January is a cold house in a queue. If your boiler is over fifteen years old, service it by all means — but start an honest parallel conversation with yourself about replacement timing, because the same efficiency and safety logic that makes the annual service cheap makes an ancient boiler expensive.

Sources: UK manufacturer warranty conventions (Worcester Bosch, Vaillant, Ideal, Baxi and peers require annual Gas Safe servicing); Gas Safety (Installation and Use) Regulations 1998 (landlord annual gas safety check); Gas Safe Register.
