Editorial / Squid Game Season 1: Why It Became a Global Phenomenon
TV Series

Children's games, life-or-death stakes: why Squid Game Season 1 became a global phenomenon — and why it still stands out.

By Ibrahim Sodiq · Editorial guide · Reading time: about 3 minutes

At first, Squid Game doesn't feel like the kind of series that is about to become a worldwide phenomenon.

The beginning can feel slow. We meet people struggling with money, family problems and debts, and some of the characters can be difficult — or even frustrating — to watch.

Then the game begins.

And suddenly, everything changes.

Nobody Expected Children's Games to Become Deadly

That's probably one of the biggest reasons Squid Game worked so well.

The games themselves are familiar.

Red Light, Green Light.

Tug of War.

Marbles.

These are games that sound harmless. They're the kind of games people associate with childhood.

Then Squid Game takes that familiarity and turns it into something horrifying.

The first major shock comes when the players realize that losing isn't simply losing.

Losing means dying.

That moment completely changes how you look at every game afterward.

A simple children's game suddenly becomes a fight for survival.

Survival of the Fittest

Once the players understand the rules, Squid Game becomes much more than a competition.

It becomes survival of the fittest.

Everyone has a reason for being there. Debt, desperation and personal circumstances have pushed them into a situation where the prize money looks worth risking everything for.

But the money also changes people.

Friendships become complicated.

Trust becomes dangerous.

And every decision has consequences.

You start watching not only to find out who wins, but to see what people are willing to become in order to survive.

The Characters Make the Games More Difficult to Watch

Squid Game also gives you characters that you can love, hate or simply find frustrating.

Some characters are deliberately difficult to like.

There are selfish people, manipulative people, desperate people and characters who make decisions that leave you shouting at the screen.

But that's part of what makes the show uncomfortable.

These aren't perfect heroes entering a game.

They're ordinary people carrying their own problems into an extraordinary situation.

And when survival becomes the priority, those personalities become even more important.

The Games Keep Raising the Pressure

The biggest strength of Season 1 is its ability to make each game feel different.

You don't know exactly what is coming next.

You might think a game will be simple, only to realize that there is a brutal twist behind it.

The contestants don't have all the information.

Neither does the viewer.

That uncertainty creates the tension.

Even when you're watching something as simple as two people playing marbles, you know that the result could destroy someone.

That's what Squid Game does so well: it makes simple games feel terrifying.

Why People Couldn't Stop Talking About It

Squid Game wasn't successful simply because it had violence.

It had a concept that was incredibly easy to understand.

People are placed into games.

There is a huge prize.

They have to survive.

That's enough to make someone curious.

But underneath that simple idea are themes about money, inequality, desperation, greed, friendship and the value people place on human life.

That gave viewers something to talk about after each episode.

People weren't only asking:

"Who will survive?"

They were also asking:

"What would I do if I were there?"

And that's a much more powerful question.

Why Season 1 Still Stands Out

For me, Squid Game Season 1 works because of the constant feeling that everything could go wrong.

The early episodes may take their time, and some of the characters can definitely be frustrating, but once the games begin, the series becomes extremely difficult to ignore.

The killing.

The pressure.

The races.

The betrayals.

The uncertainty.

Everything keeps pushing the story forward.

And perhaps the most disturbing part is that the games themselves are so innocent.

Squid Game took things that were supposed to belong to childhood and turned them into a brutal fight for survival.

That's the idea that made the series unforgettable.

It wasn't just a game anymore. It was life or death.
