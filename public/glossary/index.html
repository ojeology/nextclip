Reference

Writing glossary.

31 writing terms explained in plain language, with examples. Use the search box to find a term.

Active voice
The subject performs the action: "The writer finished the report." Usually clearer and more direct than the passive voice.
Anecdote
A short, interesting story about a real person or event, used to open or illustrate a piece.
Abstract
A brief summary of a research paper that states the question, method, results and conclusion. Sometimes the only part many people read.
Adjective
A word that describes a noun: "a clear, concise report."
Adverb
A word that modifies a verb, adjective or other adverb, often ending in -ly: "she wrote carefully."
Argument
The central claim or point you are making and supporting with evidence.
Audience
The person or people you are writing for. Knowing your audience shapes tone, vocabulary and detail.
Body
The main part of a piece, between the introduction and the conclusion, where the point is developed.
Citation
The reference that tells a reader where a fact, idea or quote came from.
Clause
A group of words containing a subject and a verb. An independent clause can stand alone; a dependent clause cannot.
Conclusion
The closing section that ties the piece together and states what it means. It should not introduce new arguments.
Conflict
The obstacle pushing against a character's goal. It is the engine of a story.
Conciseness
Using the fewest words necessary to say something clearly. Cut filler and repetition.
Dialogue
The spoken words of characters in a story, shown in quotation marks and used to reveal character and advance the plot.
Draft
A version of a piece. The first draft is the rough initial version; later drafts are improved versions.
Editing
Improving the writing at the sentence and paragraph level: word choice, flow, repetition and clarity.
Hook
The opening line or lines that catch a reader's attention and make them want to continue.
Introduction
The opening section that states what the piece is about and gives the reader a reason to read on.
Metaphor
Describing one thing as another to make a comparison without using "like" or "as": "time is a thief."
Narration
The voice telling a story, and the perspective from which it is told (first, second or third person).
Paraphrase
Restating an idea from a source in your own words, still giving credit to the source.
Pitch
A short proposal sent to a publication or editor to sell an idea before you write it.
Plagiarism
Presenting someone else's words, ideas or work as your own without credit.
Plot
The sequence of events in a story and how they connect to create change.
Proofreading
The final pass that catches typos, punctuation errors and inconsistencies. It comes after editing.
Revision
The stage of improving the big picture of a piece — structure, argument and clarity — before sentence-level editing.
Tone
The attitude the writing conveys: formal, warm, urgent, playful. Tone changes per piece.
Topic sentence
The sentence that states the main idea of a paragraph.
Transition
A word or phrase that connects ideas and shows how they relate: "however", "as a result", "in practice".
Thesis
The central claim of an essay or paper that the rest of the work defends.
Voice
The writer's consistent, recognisable way of writing, independent of the tone of any one piece.
