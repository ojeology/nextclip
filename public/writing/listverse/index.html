What this publication wants

This is BRYME's summary of the publication's focus — not a copy of their website. Type of writing: Articles, Other. Official guideline: Listverse guidelines.

Topics, themes and styles they look for

  • A finished 10-item list with an introductory paragraph, 1,500–2,000 words, native-level English.
  • Quality online sources for each item. No Wikipedia or other editable pages. Format sources as in section 7 of the Author’s Guide.
  • Popular official themes: mysteries, secrets, facts/misconceptions, history with a twist, the darker side of people. Uniqueness is required — do not rework a list already on Listverse or elsewhere.
  • American spelling. Imperial measures with metric in parentheses except in scientific pieces.

Subjects they avoid

  • Writers outside US, UK, NZ, Ireland, Canada, Australia and South Africa (unless already published there).
  • Sports, technology, or self-help / advice (official guide: almost never published).
  • Personal or unverifiable material. Translations of existing foreign-language pages. Content farms / middle-men.
  • Images (their editors add images). Illegal content. Plagiarism.
  • Sending two or more lists in one day — they treat that as a sign you rushed.

Key requirements

  • Live in one of the seven named countries, or already be a published Listverse writer
  • PayPal
  • Finished 10-item list
  • Sources for every item

Who can submit

Official submit page: only considering submissions from the US, UK, NZ, Ireland, Canada, Australia and South Africa, unless you are an existing published Listverse writer. They do not accept lists from outside those countries. Nigeria does not qualify.

Rights

On purchase: ownership, copyright and all other rights including moral rights transfer to Listverse Limited. They may edit. If rejected, rights stay with you.

AI policy

Not stated in the guideline

How to write for them

Official requirement: always follow the publication's own guideline exactly.

BRYME's practical advice: write for the publication's audience, use a clear structure, keep paragraphs short, lead with a specific angle, and confirm the word count before submitting. These are practical suggestions — they are not the publication's official rules.

How to pitch

Where a pitch is required, this is the general pattern BRYME recommends — always adapt it to the publication's guideline.

  1. Research the publication and read several recent pieces.
  2. Choose a specific idea that fits their beat and audience.
  3. Write a concise subject line naming your idea.
  4. Introduce yourself in one or two lines (who you are, where you publish).
  5. Explain the proposed piece in a short paragraph — angle, structure, length.
  6. Say why it fits this publication.
  7. Include 2–3 relevant clips or links.
  8. Close professionally and thank them for their time.
  9. Send through the official channel only.

How to submit

Official submit-a-list form

Apply through the official channel below. BRYME only records submission destinations it has verified.

  • Official guideline: opens the publication guideline (new tab)
  • Submission: Official submit-a-list form
  • Submission email: submit@listverse.com

What the guideline asks you to prepare

  • Read https://listverse.com/authors/listverse-author-guide.pdf first.
  • Submit through https://listverse.com/submit-a-list/ and agree to the terms.
  • If you have not heard in 30 days, check spam. Official comms come from submit@listverse.com.
  • They do not negotiate rejected lists.
Safety note. Never pay to submit your work. A legitimate publication does not ask writers to pay a fee or provide identity documents to be considered. If you are asked to, do not proceed.

What happens after submission

Expected response: Aim to accept or reject within 30 days

If the publication does not state a response time, BRYME records that it is unknown. A follow-up is generally reasonable after a couple of weeks; if there is still no response, treat that as a likely non-acceptance rather than a rejection. Acceptance is normally communicated by the publication. If they accept, they will tell you exactly how to submit the final piece and, for most, how and when payment is made.
