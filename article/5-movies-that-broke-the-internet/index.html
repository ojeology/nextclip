5 Movies That Broke the Internet

Every few years, a movie doesn't just succeed — it crashes websites, floods timelines, and becomes the only thing anyone talks about. These are the films that broke the internet.

Avengers: Endgame (2019)

Ticket presales crashed theater websites worldwide in minutes. At release, it became the most-tweeted film ever, and 'I love you 3000' trended for months. The portals scene alone caused mass theater chaos — people screamed, cried, and recorded each other instead of the screen.

Spider-Man: No Way Home (2021)

The multiverse reveal broke fan sites and sent leaks into overdrive for a full year. When Tobey and Andrew finally appeared, cinemas erupted like a stadium crowd. It also broke pandemic-era box office records, proving the internet's favorite rumor could sell a billion dollars.

Squid Game (2021)

Netflix's servers strained as 1.65 billion hours were streamed in a month. The dalgona candy challenge flooded TikTok, green tracksuits became a global costume, and 'Red Light, Green Light' became the most recreated scene of the decade. A Korean show broke the internet worldwide.

Barbenheimer (2023)

Barbie and Oppenheimer released on the same day, and the internet turned it into a double-feature religion. The meme war produced merch, group costumes, and a combined $2.4 billion. No studio planned it — the internet made it happen.

Deadpool & Wolverine (2024)

The 'Bye Bye Bye' dance opened a thousand TikToks, cameo leaks broke fan sites for months, and the R-rated Marvel film became the highest-grossing R-rated movie ever. Ryan Reynolds and Hugh Jackman's banter owned every platform for a full summer.
