Movies Like Dune: Big-Scale Science-Fiction Epics to Watch Next

If Dune's scale, worldbuilding and political ambition are what you want more of, these are the sci-fi epics that come closest — with a note on what each one does differently.

Editorial guide · Reading time: about 2 minutes

Start with the story you loved

Dune works on several levels at once: a desert planet and its ecology, a royal power struggle, a chosen-one prophecy, and enormous scale. Different viewers latch onto different parts, so the best next watch depends on which one pulled you in.

For more of the same world: Dune: Part Two

The direct sequel continues Paul Atreides's story across the desert. If you want more of the sand, the Fremen and the political fallout of the first film, this is the obvious next stop before anything else.

For scale and design: Blade Runner 2049

Directed by Denis Villeneuve, the same director as the modern Dune films, Blade Runner 2049 shares that patient, enormous-screen visual language. It is more of a detective story set in a rain-soaked future city, but the sense of a vast, detailed world is very similar.

For the big questions: Interstellar

Interstellar trades Dune's sand for space and its politics for family and time. If you responded to the sense of cosmic scale and big ideas rather than the warring houses, this is the closest match in feel.

For a quieter cosmic mystery: Arrival

Arrival is smaller in spectacle but just as ambitious in idea. It asks how communication and time actually work, and it trusts the audience the way Dune does. A strong pick when you want the brain of Dune without the battles.

For problem-solving and survival: The Martian

The Martian is Dune's opposite in tone — optimistic, funny, and built around engineering problems. If what you loved was watching a stranded protagonist survive against a hostile world through skill and cleverness, this delivers that energy.

For pure tension: Gravity

Gravity is the most concentrated of the group: one character, one accident, very little margin for error. Choose it when you want the physical danger of space rather than Dune's sprawling political canvas.

How to choose

If you loved the world and politics, start with Dune: Part Two. If you loved the scale and visuals, go Blade Runner 2049. If you loved the big ideas, Interstellar or Arrival. If you loved survival against a hostile place, The Martian or Gravity. All of them are strong, and none is a copy of Dune.
