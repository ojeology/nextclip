Types of writing

All types of writing

A plain-language map of every common type of writing, so you can tell them apart and pick the right one.

Beginner Start here — assumes no prior knowledge.

Researched and written by BRYME Editorial Desk.

← All Types of writing guides

There are more types of writing than most people name, and knowing the difference matters — an essay and a report use the same skills but a different shape. This is a plain-language map of the common types, grouped by what they are for.

The four broad categories

Expository — writing that explains or informs: essays, articles, reports, textbooks. The aim is clarity. Narrative — writing that tells a story: short stories, novels, memoir, journalism. The aim is a compelling sequence. Persuasive — writing that convinces: argumentative essays, advertising, pitches, proposals. The aim is agreement. Descriptive — writing that paints a picture: poetry, travel writing, some journalism and fiction. The aim is vivid detail.

Most real writing blends these. A piece of journalism is narrative and expository; a political op-ed is persuasive and expository.

Academic & research

  • Essay — argues one point in a structured form. (See the essay guides.)
  • Research paper — original research, with a standard structure of abstract, method, results, discussion.
  • Thesis / dissertation — a long, original piece of research completing a degree.
  • Literature review — a summary and synthesis of what has been written on a topic.

Professional & work

  • Report — informs a decision, leading with the answer. (See the report guides.)
  • Business / cover letter — a short letter applying for a job or opportunity.
  • Professional email — a clear email that states its point and makes an easy ask.
  • Proposal — persuades someone to fund or approve a plan.
  • Memo — a short internal message to colleagues.
  • CV / resume — a structured summary of your experience and skills.

Creative & narrative

  • Short story — a complete narrative in a few pages. (See the story guides.)
  • Novel — a long work of fiction with a full plot and characters. (See how to develop characters.)
  • Poem — compressed, image-based writing using rhythm and sound. (See the poem guides.)
  • Screenplay / script — a story written in standard format for film, TV or stage. (See the screenplay guides.)
  • Memoir / autobiography / biography — a life, in your own words or another's. (See the personal essay guide.)

Online & content

  • Blog post — a scannable, useful article that solves a problem. (See the blog post guides.)
  • Newsletter — a short, regular email to a reader. (See the newsletter guides.)
  • Article / feature — a piece of journalism or editorial for a publication. (See the press release and query guides.)
  • Product description — copy that helps a customer decide to buy. (See the product description guides.)
  • Copywriting / advertising copy — persuasive copy selling a product or idea. (See the copywriting guide.)
  • Social media copy — short, shareable posts for a platform.

Practical & personal

  • Speech / presentation — written to be heard, with a clear structure and call to action. (See the speech guides.)
  • Review — an assessment of a book, film, product or service. (See the book review guides.)
  • Press release — an announcement written in news format. (See the press release guides.)
  • Journal / diary — personal, unstructured reflection. (See the journaling guides.)

How to pick the right type

Ask three questions: Who is my reader? (a manager, an examiner, a customer, a stranger). What do I want them to do? (understand, decide, feel, buy). What is the convention? (each form has a readership with expectations). The answers tell you whether you need an essay, a report, a post or a story.

Common mistake

Using the wrong form. Writing an essay when you needed a report, or a blog post when you needed a press release, because you only know one shape. Learning the types widens what you can write.
