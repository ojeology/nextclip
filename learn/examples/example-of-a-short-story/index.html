Example of a short story

A complete short story with a breakdown of the craft behind it.

Beginner Start here — assumes no prior knowledge.

Researched and written by BRYME Editorial Desk.

A short story does not need to be long to land. This complete example shows how a small moment, a single character, and a sharp ending can do the work of a whole narrative.

The example short story

The Key

Mamadou had lost the key twice, but it was the second time that mattered.

The first time, it was just a key. He found it in the pocket of the coat he wore to the market, laughed at himself, and slipped it back on its hook by the door. The key was to his own apartment, the one room he rented above the shop where he worked. It opened a door, and that was all.

The second time, he picked up his jacket on a hot evening and felt the familiar weight of keys. Except it was not there. He checked the hook, the table, the pocket again. Nothing. He had been to the market, the mosque, and the shop. One of those places, or none of them, had taken the key.

He stood outside his door holding the spare he had been meaning to make for months, and it was not the spare. It was the key to his mother's house, the one he kept on the same ring. He had carried the wrong key all along.

For a moment he did not know which door he wanted to open. The apartment was his — a bed, a chair, a window with a view of the street. But his mother's house was where he was from, where the cool walls breathed the smell of rain and rice, where his name still sounded like a child's. He had spent years trying to open the first door, and forgotten he already had the second.

He stood in the street as the evening light went copper, holding a key that would open a door he was not sure he wanted to go through anymore.

In the end he did not open either door that night. He sat on the steps, and for the first time in years, thought about what both keys were for.

What makes the story work

It is built on a single, ordinary object. A key is small and concrete. The whole story hangs on it, and that focus keeps the story tight — no sprawling plot, one image, one feeling.

The conflict is internal. Nothing is resolved by a chase or a fight. The drama is inside Mamadou: which door does he want? Internal conflict suits a short story, which has no room for a long plot.

It shows rather than tells. We are not told Mamadou is torn between his old life and his new one. We are shown two keys, two doors, and a man unsure which to open. The detail does the telling.

The detail is specific. "Copper light," "the smell of rain and rice," "the cool walls" — concrete, sensory detail is what makes the reader feel the difference between the two places.

The ending does not resolve, it opens. Mamadou does not choose. He sits and thinks about it. The ending asks a question rather than answering it, which is often stronger than a tidy finish.

The structure to copy

  1. Open on the object or moment that the story will turn on.
  2. Give the specific situation — one character, one place, a short time span.
  3. Raise a small conflict (here, the missing key) that triggers the real internal conflict.
  4. Earn the insight through detail and observation, not explanation.
  5. End on a question or a turn, not a resolved answer.

Common mistakes

Trying to cover a whole life in a few pages. Explaining the theme. Too many characters. A plot that needs a novel. An ending that explains the point instead of letting the reader feel it.
