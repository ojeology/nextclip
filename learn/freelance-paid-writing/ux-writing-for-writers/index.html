UX writing — what it pays and how to move into it

UK content writers average £345 a day. UX sits at £456–£550. The gap is not writing skill; it is proximity to product decisions.

Intermediate Assumes you can already draft and revise a piece.

Researched and written by BRYME Editorial Desk.

← All Freelance & paid writing guides

UX writing — increasingly called content design — is the writing inside a product: the button labels, error messages, empty states, onboarding flows and form hints. It is the highest-paid destination available to a writer who does not want to become a copywriter or a journalist.

What it pays

UK freelance day-rate benchmarks for 2026:

RoleAverage day rate
UX designer£550
Content strategist£407
Copywriter£421
Content writer£345
Brand strategist£495
Top decile, UX contracts~£675

Separate contract-market data puts freelance UX designer day rates around £456, rising to £582 for inside-IR35 engagements, where the higher headline rate compensates for employment-equivalent tax.

Salaried UK UX writer roles report a median around £49,000, with a range from roughly £28,000 to £87,000 across 390 reported salaries.

The headline comparison: a content writer averages £345 a day. UX sits £456–£550. That is a 30–60% premium for work that uses the same core skill.

Why the gap exists

It is not that UX writers write better sentences. It is what the writing is attached to.

The closer your work sits to research, systems, customer journeys and business decisions, the more clients pay.

That is the pattern across the whole freelance creative market — strategy at £520, brand strategy at £495, UX at £550, against graphic design at £353 and content writing at £345.

A blog post is a marketing asset. An onboarding flow is the thing that determines whether a user completes signup. One is measurable revenue, the other is measurable traffic, and budgets follow the first.

What the work actually involves

Microcopy. Buttons, labels, tooltips, confirmations. Ruthless economy — a button has three words.

Error and empty states. The moments where a product either helps or loses someone. Widely agreed to be the highest-value writing in a product.

Onboarding. Getting a new user to their first success. Directly tied to activation metrics.

Information architecture. Naming things, and deciding what sits where. This is the part most writers underestimate and where the seniority is.

Content design systems. Voice guidelines, terminology standards, reusable patterns. This is what turns you from a contractor into someone building infrastructure — and it is where rates move.

Working with designers and engineers, in Figma and in tickets, not in a document.

Moving into it from writing

Your existing skill transfers more than you think. Editing for clarity, cutting, knowing what a reader needs next — those are the job.

Learn to work in the design tool. You do not need to design, but you need to write inside Figma rather than sending a document. This is the most common practical barrier and it takes a weekend.

Rewrite a real product's copy. Pick an app with confusing onboarding or a bad error state. Rewrite it, screenshot before and after, and explain your reasoning. One of these is a better portfolio than twenty blog posts — the reasoning is what gets you hired.

Learn the vocabulary of product. Activation, retention, funnel, edge case, happy path. Not to sound like a product manager, but so you can join the conversation where the decisions happen.

Read your own product's support tickets. The gap between what a product says and what users understand is documented there, and pointing at it is the most persuasive thing you can do in an interview.

Study accessibility. Plain language, screen-reader behaviour, error announcement. It is a genuine specialism inside a specialism, and it is increasingly a legal requirement rather than a nicety.

Where it fits against the alternatives

RouteUK day rate
Financial services copywriting£600–£850
UX writing / content design£456–£550
Technology and software copywriting£450–£750
General copywriting£480 average
Content writing£345

[Financial services](/learn/freelance-paid-writing/how-to-become-a-financial-writer/) pays more. UX is more interesting to most writers, has a clearer skill ladder, and moves into salaried roles more easily if you want that option.

Two honest cautions

In-house is common. Much UX writing is staff work rather than freelance. If you want to stay freelance, aim at agencies, scale-ups and contract roles rather than product teams.

Check IR35 if you are UK-based and contracting. Inside-IR35 day rates look higher because they are taxed as employment. Compare take-home, not headline.
