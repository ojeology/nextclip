B2B content writing — how to specialise and raise your rate

EFA puts business and marketing writing at 32¢–87.5¢ a word against 25¢–45¢ for blog posts. The spread inside "B2B" is wider than the gap to any other niche.

Intermediate Assumes you can already draft and revise a piece.

Researched and written by BRYME Editorial Desk.

← All Freelance & paid writing guides

"B2B content writing" describes work paying anywhere from 5¢ a word to $5,000 a project. Treating it as one market is the reason so many writers are stuck at the bottom of it.

What the data says

From the EFA 2026 Rate Chart (survey of 1,100+ members):

CategoryPer wordPer hour
Business / marketing32¢–87.5¢$65–$125
Technical87.5¢–$1.08$75–$126
Articles and essays25¢–45¢$70–$100
Blog posts25¢–45¢$74.50–$87.50

And UK day rates from analyses built on the ProCopywriters survey of 474 freelancers: technology and software £450–£750, financial services £600–£850, against an overall copywriting average of £480.

Notice the range inside business/marketing itself: 32¢ to 87.5¢. That is a 2.7× spread within one category. The gap between the bottom and top of B2B is larger than the gap between B2B and most other niches.

Which means: moving up within B2B is a bigger lever than switching niche.

What separates the top from the bottom

The deliverable, almost entirely.

DeliverableWhy it pays what it pays
White papers and research reportsPriced as marketing assets. Often the best per-hour work available
Case studiesRequire interviewing customers and coordinating approvals. Most writers avoid the logistics, so supply is thin
Technical and integration contentRequires understanding the product. Smallest supply of competent writers
Sales enablement — battlecards, comparison pagesDirectly attached to closing revenue
Lifecycle and onboarding emailUndervalued relative to measurable impact
SEO blog contentMost abundant, worst paid, hardest hit by AI price compression

The ordering tracks two things: how hard the work is to supply, and how close it sits to revenue.

The three moves that raise a B2B rate

1. Move from volume to assets. Ten blog posts a month at $200 is $2,000 and endless. One white paper at $2,500 is more money and less work. The client is the same client — you are simply selling a different thing. Ask for it.

2. Take the work with logistics. Case studies require chasing a customer, running an interview, and getting legal approval. That friction is exactly why they pay well and why competitors avoid them. Volunteering for the annoying deliverable is the fastest rate increase available in B2B.

3. Go one layer more technical. Within any B2B sector there is a technical tier most writers cannot reach — API guides, compliance explainers, architecture overviews. That is where the 87.5¢–$1.08 band lives. See [technical writing rates](/learn/freelance-paid-writing/technical-writing-rates/).

How to specialise credibly

Pick a sector, not a format. "B2B writer" competes with everyone. "Writes for cybersecurity vendors" competes with almost nobody. Candidate sectors with real budgets: cybersecurity, fintech infrastructure, logistics, healthtech, manufacturing software, HR tech.

Learn the buyer, not just the product. B2B content is read by someone who has to justify a purchase internally. Content that helps them make that case to their CFO is worth more than content that explains a feature.

Read the analyst material. Gartner, Forrester and sector-specific analysts define the vocabulary your clients use. Reading it is free and it makes you legible to a marketing director immediately.

Publish one piece with real substance. An original take on a sector problem, with data. It is a portfolio piece and a pitch simultaneously.

Get one case study under your belt. It unlocks the category, and clients who need one usually need six.

Pricing

Project, not per word. B2B buyers think in deliverables and budgets. Per-word pricing punishes concision, which is most of the skill in sales enablement.

Anchor on the asset's value. A case study that supports a $50,000 deal is not a $300 job. Say what it is for when you quote it.

Retainers, with a defined scope. Predictable income for you, predictable spend for them. A stated 10–15% discount for a fixed monthly scope is a fair trade. An open-ended "a few pieces a month" is not.

Know your floor before negotiating — the [rate calculator](/tools/freelance-rate-calculator/) gives you the number below which the work costs you money.

What to stop doing

Stop quoting blog rates for asset work. If you price a white paper like four blog posts, you will be paid like four blog posts.

Stop competing on volume. The SEO-content tier is where AI compressed prices hardest, and no amount of speed wins that race.

Stop describing yourself as a B2B writer. Describe the sector.
