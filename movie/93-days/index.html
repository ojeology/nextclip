Quick summary

Steve Gukas’s 93 Days dramatises the first Ebola case treated in Lagos and the people who had to contain it. It is not an action movie. It is about protocols, fear, and the gap between a headline and a hospital corridor. BRYME keeps it in the catalogue because it is one of the clearer Nigerian films about a real public emergency.

What 93 Days is about

The film follows health workers and officials as they try to stop a single case becoming a city-wide outbreak. The tension is procedural: who goes in the room, who tells the truth, who panics.

It does not pretend Lagos is a set. The stakes are ordinary people going to work inside an extraordinary week. That is why it still belongs next to BRYME’s other Nigerian dramas, not next to outbreak schlock.

Why you might like it

You want Nigerian cinema that is about work and fear, not just crime.

It is useful if you are building a serious Nollywood list after October 1 or Eyimofe.

Do not come for jump-scares. Come for the human cost of getting the next decision right.
