Match overview

Gary O’Neil’s newly promoted Ipswich host Sunderland at Portman Road.

Recent form

Ipswich finished runners-up in the Championship; Sunderland entered after a positive first season back in the Premier League.

Head-to-head record

The overall record is close — 21 meetings, Sunderland 10, Ipswich 8, three drawn — but the split by venue is not close at all, and it is the most useful thing in this section.

At Portman Road, where this match is played, Ipswich have won six of eleven, Sunderland two, with three draws. At the Stadium of Light the pattern reverses hard: Sunderland eight wins from ten, Ipswich two, and not a single draw.

In other words, this fixture has been decided by home advantage to an unusual degree, and the home side here is the one the table says is weaker.

The two clubs have met four times in the Premier League, splitting them two apiece, but those were in 2001 and 2002. Every meeting since has been in the Championship or League One — which is its own comment on where both clubs have spent the last two decades.

Historical context

Ipswich have added several players and are still resolving their goalkeeper picture; Sunderland offer more continuity under Régis Le Bris.

The case for the underdog

The underdog label sits awkwardly here, and that is what makes it interesting.

Ipswich Town are the promoted side, up as Championship runners-up and back at this level after a single season away. Sunderland finished 7th on 54 points and qualified for the Europa League — but did so with a negative goal difference of −6, scoring only 42, the lowest of any side in the top half.

So the visitors are the higher-ranked team and the home side are the ones with something to prove, yet Sunderland's underlying numbers were the weakest of any European qualifier in the division. A promoted side at home against a team that overperformed its goal difference is a more even fixture than the table positions suggest.
