Arsenal Begin Their Title Defence: Can Anyone Stop Them?

Two games, two wins, no goals conceded. Arsenal have started their title defence perfectly — but Chelsea and Manchester City are level on points, and one of them visits the Emirates on Sunday.

BRYME Editorial2026-09-037 min read

Arsenal are champions of England, and they have started the defence of that title the way champions are supposed to: two matches, two wins, four goals scored, none conceded. They sit 3rd, 6 points from 2, 4-0.

The question in the headline is the one everybody asked in August. Three weeks into the season it already has a sharper edge, because the two clubs most likely to answer it have started just as well — and one of them arrives at the Emirates this weekend.

What the table actually says

Four clubs have won both of their opening games. The separation is not in the points column, it is in the goal columns.

Chelsea and Manchester City have the same six points. Chelsea have conceded five goals getting there, City two, Arsenal none. Over 38 games that difference is usually the title.

The number that matters

Arsenal have not conceded a Premier League goal this season. No side that has kept a clean sheet in both opening fixtures has ever been in a bad position in September.

The window: a spine, not a splash

Arsenal did not sign the most expensive player in England this summer. They signed the one their midfield had been missing for three years.

Bruno Guimarães at roughly €87.5m from Newcastle is the deal that defines the window. Piero Hincapié gives Arteta a ball-playing left-sided defender, Christos Tzolis a direct wide threat, and Illan Meslier arrives on a free as goalkeeping cover.

Out went Leandro Trossard, Jakub Kiwior, Christian Nørgaard, Karl Hein and — the one that will be tested all season — Gabriel Jesus to Barcelona for £8.6m.

So can anyone stop them?

Three sides have a genuine claim, and each has a different flaw.

Chelsea lead the table and have scored seven, but they have also conceded five in two games and sold Enzo Fernández to Manchester City for £125m on deadline day. Xabi Alonso is rebuilding a midfield mid-season.

Manchester City have Enzo Fernández, Elliot Anderson and Iliman Ndiaye, and a new manager in Enzo Maresca replacing Pep Guardiola. Six points from two, six scored, two conceded. On paper this is the most complete challenge.

Hull City are the anomaly — promoted, six points, no goals conceded, and a 2–0 win over Manchester United already banked. Nobody expects it to last. It is early enough that nobody can prove it will not.

BRYME view

The defence is the argument. Arsenal are the only side in the division yet to concede, and they added a €87.5m midfielder to protect it. City are the likeliest challengers because they have the deepest squad; Chelsea are the likeliest to drop points because they keep conceding. Sunday against Chelsea will tell us more than the previous two weeks combined.

What to watch next

Arsenal host Chelsea at the Emirates on Sunday 6 September, 16:30 UK. It is first versus third and, more usefully, the best defence in the league against one of the leakiest attacks in the top half. Our full predictions page has a scoreline for it and for every other fixture in Europe this weekend.

The fixture list is about to get harder

Arsenal's two wins came against Coventry (3–0 away) and Aston Villa (1–0 at home). Those are the sides currently 19th and 18th, with one goal scored between them all season. The clean sheets are real; the opposition has been the weakest available.

That is not a criticism of Arsenal so much as a warning against reading too much into two matches. Chelsea on Sunday is the first fixture that tells us something, and it is followed by a run in which Arteta's side face several of the teams currently above the bottom five.

The Gabriel Jesus question

Selling Gabriel Jesus to Barcelona for £8.6m was the window's quietest decision and may prove its most consequential. Arsenal have scored four goals in two games — fewer than Chelsea, Brighton, Manchester United or Manchester City — and did not sign a replacement centre-forward. Christos Tzolis at ~€40m is a wide player.

Bukayo Saka has two of those four goals. A title race in which Arsenal keep clean sheets but rely on a winger for goals is a narrower path than the table currently suggests.

How the last two title races were decided

The pattern in recent seasons has been consistent: the champion is rarely the highest scorer, but almost always has the best defensive record. Arsenal currently have that record by definition — nobody else has kept two clean sheets and conceded nothing. Manchester City are next on two conceded.

If that holds through October, Arsenal are in a strong position regardless of what the attack does. If it does not, the absence of a recognised striker becomes the story of their season.
