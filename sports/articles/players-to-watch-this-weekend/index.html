Players to Watch This Weekend Across Europe

Bruno Fernandes leads England with three in two. Raphinha has five in three. Donyell Malen has five in two for a Roma side yet to concede. The players in form, from the actual scoring charts.

Donyell Malen. Five goals in two matches, and Roma have not conceded a goal this season. If he scores against Atalanta, Roma are the best team in Italy on every available measure.

The names missing from these lists

Two absences are worth noting. The most expensive signings of the window — Enzo Fernández (£125m), Morgan Rogers (£117m), Elliot Anderson (~£116m) and Sandro Tonali (£100m) — appear nowhere in the Premier League scoring charts.

That is entirely normal for central midfielders in the opening fortnight, and it is also why goal charts are a poor way to judge a transfer window. Watch Fernández and Anderson this weekend for how often City's midfield recovers the ball in the opposition half, not for goals.

The second absence is a Tottenham player, because Tottenham have not scored.

Small samples, honestly labelled

The Bundesliga has played one round. Younes Ebnoutalib and Yuito Suzuki have three goals each from a single appearance, which is a hat-trick and nothing more — no player sustains three goals per game.

The same caution applies to Jack Hinshelwood's two goals in one appearance for Brighton. These are worth watching precisely because they are extreme: the interesting question is not whether the rate continues but whether the player keeps starting once it stops.

The most reliable indicator this early

Minutes. A player scoring at a high rate off the bench tells you less than a player who has started every match for a side winning games. On that measure the names to trust are Raphinha (three starts, five goals, league leaders Barcelona), Kylian Mbappé (three starts, four goals, Real Madrid unbeaten) and Bruno Fernandes (two starts, three goals).

All three play for sides who will dominate possession this weekend, which is the simplest reason to expect the numbers to keep climbing.

Our three picks, in order

Donyell Malen (Roma v Atalanta). Five goals in two matches for a side that has won 4–0 twice without conceding. The best form of any forward in Europe, against a defence good enough to make it a genuine contest.

Raphinha (Valencia v Barcelona). Five in three, and Valencia have conceded four while scoring one. The most favourable matchup on the board for a player already leading his league.

Bruno Fernandes (Everton v Manchester United). Three goals in two from midfield, away at an unbeaten side. If United are to win, he almost certainly decides it.
