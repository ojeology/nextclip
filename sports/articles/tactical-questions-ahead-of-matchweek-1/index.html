The Five Biggest Tactical Questions of the Season So Far

Chelsea are top and leaking goals. City have a £241m midfield and a thinner defence. Spurs have spent £185m and scored nothing. The five tactical problems that will define the autumn.

BRYME Editorial2026-09-037 min read

Two matchweeks have been played, which is exactly enough football to replace the questions we had in August with better ones. These are the five tactical problems that will define the autumn.

1. Can Chelsea keep winning while conceding this much?

Chelsea are 1st, 6 points from 2, 7-5. Both wins were 3–2 at Fulham and 4–3 against Brighton — seven scored, five conceded. Then they sold Enzo Fernández to Manchester City for £125m on deadline day without signing a replacement.

Xabi Alonso's Leverkusen sides were built on control. This Chelsea team is not controlled; it is simply outscoring people. Sunday at Arsenal, against the only defence yet to concede, is the test.

2. Does a two-man £241m midfield actually work?

Manchester City bought Enzo Fernández (£125m) and Elliot Anderson (~£116m) and let Bernardo Silva, John Stones, Manuel Akanji and Nathan Aké go. Maresca has replaced a single-pivot structure with two central midfielders and a thinner defence.

What to look for

Whether City’s full-backs still push as high. With three senior centre-backs gone, the cover behind an advanced full-back is not what it was.

3. Where do Tottenham's goals come from?

£100m for Sandro Tonali, £85m for Mateus Fernandes, and zero goals in two matches. Spurs are 20th, 0 points from 2, 0-5. De Zerbi bought midfield control and did not buy a striker; Mykhailo Mudryk arrived on loan as a winger.

This is the most solvable problem on the list and the one that cannot be solved until January.

4. Is Hull City's defence sustainable?

Hull are 4th, 6 points from 2, 3-0 with no goals conceded, including 2–0 against Manchester United. They spent £3m on Jack Butland and added three free transfers.

Low blocks work early in a season because opponents have not yet worked out how to break them and squads are fresh. The question is whether Sergej Jakirović has enough depth when injuries arrive in October.

5. Can Liverpool press without the players who did the pressing?

Andoni Iraola's teams press aggressively and defend in transition. He inherited a Liverpool squad that lost Mohamed Salah, Andy Robertson and Ibrahima Konaté on free transfers in a single summer.

Liverpool have drawn both matches 2–2 — 13th, 2 points from 2, 4-4 — and have been pegged back in each. Ronald Araújo's loan from Barcelona is the signing that has to settle fastest.

The weekend's answers

Three of these five get tested immediately: Chelsea at Arsenal, City against Coventry, and Hull against Aston Villa. Our predictions page has a scoreline and the supporting form for every one of them.

A sixth question: what happens to the promoted defences?

Hull City have conceded nothing and sit fourth. Ipswich have conceded six and sit eleventh. Coventry have conceded four and scored none.

Promoted sides almost always concede more as the season progresses, for a simple reason — opponents build a video file on them. A deep block that nobody has analysed is far more effective in August than the same block in January. Whether Sergej Jakirović can adapt Hull's shape once teams solve it is a better question than whether the clean sheets continue.

The pattern across Europe

The same tension appears in every top-five league right now. In Spain, Barcelona have scored twelve in three and Real Madrid ten, while Valencia have scored one. In Italy, Roma have won 4–0 twice without conceding, and Fiorentina have conceded seven without scoring. In France, Monaco have won both without conceding.

Early seasons exaggerate. Attacking sides that are sharp look unstoppable, and defensive sides that are organised look impenetrable, because neither has yet met an opponent who has worked them out. Nearly all of these extremes regress by October.

What we would watch in each match

At Arsenal v Chelsea, watch where Chelsea lose the ball. Five goals conceded in two wins suggests turnovers in midfield, and Arsenal are built to punish exactly that.

At Manchester City v Coventry, watch City's full-backs. If they push high against a side with no attacking threat, that is Maresca committing to his structure regardless of the missing centre-backs.

At Hull v Aston Villa, watch how long Hull's block holds. Villa have not scored all season and Hull have not conceded; one of those runs ends on Saturday.

The one we most want answered

Tottenham's. The other four questions are about whether something good continues; this one is about whether something broken can be fixed without a transfer market.

Roberto De Zerbi has £185m of midfield, no recognised centre-forward and four months until he can sign one. Everything he tries between now and January — a false nine, a winger through the middle, Mudryk central — will be a coaching solution to a recruitment problem, and those are the most interesting experiments in football to watch.
