The Players Who Could Explode This Season

Jack Hinshelwood has two goals in one appearance. Elliot Anderson cost £116m. Donyell Malen has five in two for Roma. Seven players set for a breakout season, and the evidence for each.

BRYME Editorial2026-09-036 min read

Every season a handful of players go from useful to essential. These are the seven best-placed to do it in 2026/27 — picked from players who either moved this summer or have already started scoring.

1. Jack Hinshelwood (Brighton)

Two goals in a single appearance, already among the Premier League's leading scorers. Brighton have scored seven in two matches and sit 9th, 3 points from 2, 7-4. Fabian Hürzeler had his contract extended through 2029 and rebuilt the squad around players of exactly this profile.

2. Rayan Cherki (Manchester City)

Two goals in two games for a City side adjusting to life after Guardiola. With Bernardo Silva gone and Jack Grealish on loan at Everton, the creative burden has shifted, and Cherki has taken a share of it immediately.

3. Elliot Anderson (Manchester City)

Left Nottingham Forest for around £116m. That fee makes him one of the most expensive midfielders in English football history, and he arrives alongside Enzo Fernández. If Maresca's twin-£100m midfield works, Anderson is the player it is built around.

4. Carlos Baleba (Manchester United)

£65–70m from Brighton, into a United midfield that also added Andrey Santos and Youri Tielemans. United have conceded four in two games; Baleba is the player expected to stop that.

5. Morgan Rogers (Chelsea)

£117m from Aston Villa, the second-most expensive signing of the window. Chelsea are 1st, 6 points from 2, 7-5 and Villa, who sold him, have not scored a goal all season. The transfer market's clearest verdict on a single player.

6. Donyell Malen (Roma)

Five goals in two Serie A matches. Roma have won 4–0 twice and conceded nothing. Malen left Aston Villa for around €25m and is currently the most productive forward in Italy.

7. Emersonn (Ipswich Town)

£26.6m, the marquee arrival of Ipswich's seven-signing summer under Gary O'Neil. Promoted clubs rarely spend this on one forward. If he delivers, Ipswich stay up; if he does not, 11th, 3 points from 2, 4-6 will look optimistic by Christmas.

The one we would bet on

Hinshelwood. Two goals in one appearance is a small sample, but Brighton have the best attacking record in the league so far and a manager signed through 2029 who trusts young players with real minutes.

What "exploding" actually requires

Three conditions, in our experience: a guaranteed starting place, a side that creates chances, and a manager with a reason to be patient. Talent is the least reliable predictor — every squad in the Premier League is full of it.

That framework explains why Jack Hinshelwood tops our list ahead of more expensive names. Brighton have scored seven goals in two matches, Fabian Hürzeler is contracted through 2029 and has just rebuilt the squad around young players, and Hinshelwood is already scoring. All three boxes.

It also explains our caution about the £100m arrivals. Elliot Anderson and Enzo Fernández have the starting place and the chance-creating side, but a new manager at Manchester City has every incentive to win immediately rather than develop patiently.

The one nobody is discussing

Ronald Araújo, on loan at Liverpool from Barcelona with a reported option to buy. Defenders never appear on breakout lists because the evidence for them is invisible in the goal charts.

Liverpool have conceded in both matches and lost Ibrahima Konaté on a free. If Araújo settles, he transforms the season of a club that finished the summer looking diminished — and he does it in a shirt he may keep permanently.

Where this list will look wrong

Emersonn is the riskiest inclusion. £26.6m is a club-record outlay for a promoted side and Ipswich have already conceded six goals in two games. A striker in a team defending that badly spends the season chasing matches rather than leading them.

Morgan Rogers is the safest, and therefore the least interesting: a £117m player at the league leaders is not a breakout, he is an established one. We included him because Aston Villa have not scored since selling him, which is the most emphatic case any player has for his own importance.
