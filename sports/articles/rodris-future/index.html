The Rodri Question: How Manchester City Finally Answered It

City did not replace one irreplaceable midfielder. They spent £241m on Enzo Fernández and Elliot Anderson and changed the shape of the midfield instead.

BRYME Editorial2026-09-036 min read

The question in the title was asked in the summer, and the 2026 window answered it in a way nobody quite predicted: Manchester City did not find one Rodri replacement. They bought two players for a combined £241m and changed the shape of the midfield instead.

Enzo Fernández arrived from Chelsea on deadline day for £125m. Elliot Anderson came from Nottingham Forest for around £116m. Ayyoub Bouaddi arrived from Lille earlier in the window.

The strategic answer

A single holding midfielder is a single point of failure — which is exactly what the previous era discovered. Buying two elite central midfielders spreads the load rather than replacing one irreplaceable player.

Why the succession problem was so hard

A deep-lying midfielder who dictates tempo, breaks up play and never gives the ball away is the rarest profile in football. Clubs cannot simply buy one, which is why the question hung over the Etihad for two seasons.

Enzo Maresca's response has been to change the requirement. Two midfielders sharing the responsibility need not be as complete individually as one player doing it alone.

Has it worked so far?

City are 2nd, 6 points from 2, 6-2: two wins, six goals scored, two conceded, including a 4–1 at Crystal Palace. Rayan Cherki and Erling Haaland have two goals each.

Two games against Palace and Bournemouth is not evidence of much. The structure has not yet been tested by a side that presses well or by a European away leg.

What to watch for

The tell will be the games City do not control. Under the previous regime, one holding player let the full-backs push high. With two central midfielders and three senior centre-backs departed — John Stones, Manuel Akanji and Nathan Aké all left — the defensive structure behind them is different.

City host Coventry on Saturday, which will tell us nothing. The fixtures after that will tell us a great deal.

The cost of getting it wrong

Manchester City committed roughly £241m to two central midfielders in a single window. If the pairing works, it is the most important structural fix any English club made this summer. If it does not, City have spent a British-record fee on a player who does not fit alongside the other British-record fee they had already agreed.

That is the genuine risk in the approach. Enzo Fernández and Elliot Anderson are both progressive, ball-carrying midfielders. Neither is a natural defensive screen in the mould of the player they collectively replace.

Who actually shields the defence?

This is the unanswered question. With John Stones, Manuel Akanji and Nathan Aké all departed and no specialist holding midfielder signed, the protection in front of a rebuilt back line is being provided by two players whose strengths are in possession.

Ayyoub Bouaddi's arrival from Lille is the closest thing to an answer, and he is young enough that relying on him would be optimistic in a title season.

City have conceded two goals in two matches, against Crystal Palace and Bournemouth. Neither tested this.

The wider lesson for other clubs

Every side eventually faces the same problem: a player so specific to the system that no direct replacement exists. The conventional response is to search for a like-for-like signing, usually for years and usually unsuccessfully.

City's response — change the requirement rather than fill the gap — is the more interesting one, and if it works it will be copied. Liverpool are facing an equivalent problem after losing Mohamed Salah on a free, and they have not yet chosen an approach.

When we will know

By November. City's opening fixtures have been kind, and the structure will not be seriously examined until they face a side that presses their build-up and attacks the space behind their full-backs. Until then, six points from two games proves only that the plan has not failed yet.

The verdict

City have given the most convincing answer available to a question that had no clean solution. Buying two elite midfielders instead of hunting for one irreplaceable profile is expensive, imaginative and — on two matches of evidence — working.

It is also unfinished. The pairing solves possession and progression; it does not obviously solve protection, and City removed three senior centre-backs in the same window. That combination is the reason to withhold judgement until the fixtures get harder.

Ask again in November, after City have played someone who presses them.
