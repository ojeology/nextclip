The Newly Promoted Clubs: Three Very Different Approaches

Hull spent almost nothing and are fourth without conceding. Coventry spent £68.5m and have not scored. Ipswich changed manager and did both. Three promoted clubs, three strategies.

BRYME Editorial2026-09-036 min read

Three clubs came up: Coventry City, Ipswich Town and Hull City. Two matchweeks in they have taken three very different approaches, and produced three very different starts.

Hull City — spend little, concede nothing

Sergej Jakirović kept his job and spent almost nothing: Jack Butland for £3m, plus Matt Targett, Ben Robinson and Hidemasa Morita on free transfers. Ivor Pandur (£6m) and Kyle Joseph (£4m) left.

Hull are 4th, 6 points from 2, 3-0 — two wins, no goals conceded, including 2–0 against Manchester United on the opening weekend.

The approach

Continuity. Same manager, minimal turnover, a defence that already knew each other. It is the cheapest strategy on this list and currently the most effective.

Ipswich Town — back the new manager

Kieran McKenna stepped down after promotion and Gary O'Neil took over. Ipswich then made seven signings: Emersonn (£26.6m), Abdul Fatawu (£23m), Florentino (£19m), Kjell Scherpen (£11m) and Daizen Maeda (£10m) among them. Ashley Young retired at 41.

They are 11th, 3 points from 2, 4-6 — a 2–1 win over Sunderland and a 5–2 defeat at Manchester United. Aggressive recruitment, mixed early evidence.

Coventry City — spend big, wait for it to click

Frank Lampard's side spent the most: Caleb Yirenkyi (£26m), Carl Rushworth (£22.5m), Loum Tchaouna (£20m) and Aurèle Amenda (£17.2m), with Gustavo Hamer at £6m.

Coventry are 19th, 0 points from 2, 0-4 and have not scored a Premier League goal. They lost 3–0 at Arsenal and 1–0 at home to Hull.

What the three approaches tell us

The club that spent the least is fourth. The club that spent the most is 19th. That is a two-game sample and proves nothing on its own, but it points at something real: promoted squads that stay together tend to start better, because organisation transfers to a higher division faster than individual quality does.

The counter-argument arrives around November, when the schedule thickens and Hull's thin squad is asked to cover injuries that Coventry's £68.5m of new signings can absorb.

BRYME view

Hull are the story now; Coventry are the better bet for May. But if Coventry are still goalless in three weeks the mood at the club will change quickly — and they host Manchester City on Saturday.

What the historical pattern says

Promoted clubs that survive tend to share two traits: they concede fewer than sixty goals, and they take points at home against the sides around them. Neither requires spending heavily; both require organisation.

That is why Hull's start is more encouraging than Coventry's, despite the gap in outlay. A promoted side that keeps clean sheets has found the harder half of the formula. A promoted side that spends £68.5m and cannot score has found neither.

Coventry's case for patience

The counter-argument is straightforward: Coventry's two fixtures were away at Arsenal and at home to Hull — the division's two meanest defences, neither of which has conceded a goal to anyone. Failing to score against those two is not evidence of much.

Frank Lampard's signings also skew young and expensive, and Caleb Yirenkyi, Loum Tchaouna and Aurèle Amenda were bought for a season's development rather than an August impact. The £68.5m looks alarming now and may look shrewd in April.

Ipswich and the cost of changing manager

Kieran McKenna stepping down after promotion was the most disruptive thing that happened to any of the three. Gary O'Neil inherited a squad built by someone else and then added seven players to it, which means Ipswich are simultaneously integrating a new manager and a new spine.

They beat Sunderland 2–1 and lost 5–2 at Manchester United. Conceding five is the number that should worry them; Kjell Scherpen (£11m) arrived in goal and the defence in front of him is largely unchanged from the Championship.

Our survival call

Ipswich look the most likely of the three to stay up, because they have both spent money and retained the squad that won promotion. Hull's start is the best and their squad is the thinnest. Coventry have the best players and, at present, no way of scoring with them.
