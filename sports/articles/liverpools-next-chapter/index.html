Liverpool’s Next Chapter: Can They Challenge Again?

Salah, Robertson and Konaté all left on free transfers. Arne Slot left too. Andoni Iraola has two points from two games and the biggest rebuild at Anfield in a decade.

BRYME Editorial2026-09-037 min read

Mohamed Salah left on a free transfer. So did Andy Robertson. So did Ibrahima Konaté. Arne Slot left too, replaced by Andoni Iraola. Liverpool have started the season 13th, 2 points from 2, 4-4 — two draws, four scored, four conceded.

This is the most complete change of era Anfield has seen in a decade, and it happened in one summer.

Ronald Araújo's season-long loan from Barcelona, with a reported option to buy, is the headline. Jérémy Jacquet and Víctor Muñoz are longer-term bets. This is not a like-for-like replacement of what left; it is a different kind of squad.

The maths

Liverpool lost a forward with a decade of goals, a first-choice left-back and a first-choice centre-back, and received no fees. The replacements cost money the sales did not generate.

Two matches, two draws

Liverpool drew 2–2 at Newcastle and 2–2 at home with Nottingham Forest. Neither is a bad result in isolation. Together they are two points from six, and a defence that has conceded in both.

The pattern is the concern rather than the points: Iraola's teams press high and defend in transition, and Liverpool have twice been pegged back.

Can they challenge again?

The honest answer three weeks in is that they are not currently in the argument. Arsenal, Chelsea and Manchester City all have six points; Liverpool have two. That is a four-point gap in August, which is nothing, and a four-point gap plus a squad in transition, which is not nothing.

What would change our mind: Araújo settling quickly at centre-back, and someone other than the front line scoring. Liverpool travel to promoted Ipswich on Friday night — our predictions page backs them to win it, on the basis that two draws against Newcastle and Forest is still a higher floor than anything Ipswich have shown.

BRYME view

Top four is realistic. A title challenge in the first season under a new manager, having lost Salah, Robertson and Konaté for nothing, would be one of the more remarkable achievements in the club’s recent history.

What two 2–2 draws actually tell us

Liverpool drew 2–2 at Newcastle and 2–2 at home with Nottingham Forest. In both, they scored twice — which is more than Arsenal have managed in either game — and conceded twice.

That is a meaningful distinction. This is not a side that has stopped creating chances; the goals are still arriving. The problem is at the other end, where a defence missing Ibrahima Konaté has conceded in both matches against opponents currently sixth and fifteenth.

Ronald Araújo's loan from Barcelona is therefore the most important piece of business Liverpool did. If he settles, the attacking output already looks sufficient for a top-four campaign.

The financial reality of three frees

Salah, Robertson and Konaté left for nothing. Konaté alone would have commanded a substantial fee twelve months earlier, and Real Madrid took him without paying one.

The consequence is visible in the incoming column: Jérémy Jacquet and Víctor Muñoz arrived for undisclosed fees and Araújo on loan. Liverpool did not have transfer income to reinvest, which is why the rebuild looks modest next to Manchester City's £306m or Tottenham's £185m.

The Iraola question

Andoni Iraola built his reputation at Bournemouth on a high-intensity press and rapid transitions — a style that demands enormous running from the front three and disciplined defending behind it.

Liverpool's squad was assembled for a different manager. Adapting it takes a pre-season at minimum and realistically half a campaign. Two points from six is a poor return; it is also roughly what a stylistic transition looks like in its opening weeks.

The fixture at Ipswich on Friday is the sort Liverpool must win to keep the top four realistic, and our predictions page backs them to do it.

The three numbers that decide their season

Goals conceded. Two in two so far. Liverpool need this closer to one a game, and that depends almost entirely on Ronald Araújo settling at centre-back.

Goals from outside the front line. With Salah gone, a decade of guaranteed output has left the squad. Midfield and wide players have to replace roughly twenty goals between them.

Points by the end of October. Two from six is a slow start, not a crisis. Liverpool's fixture list is navigable through the autumn; if they are still averaging a point a game when the clocks change, the top four becomes genuinely difficult.
