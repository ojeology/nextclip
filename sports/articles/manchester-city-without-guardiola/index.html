Manchester City Without Guardiola: What Happens Next?

Pep Guardiola has gone, Enzo Maresca has arrived, and City spent roughly £306m on three midfielders to make the change invisible. Two matches in, here is what the new era looks like.

BRYME Editorial2026-09-037 min read

Pep Guardiola has gone. Enzo Maresca has replaced him. Manchester City are 2nd, 6 points from 2, 6-2 and have spent more than any club in England this summer to make the transition invisible.

Two matches is not enough to judge a era. It is enough to see what the new one is going to look like.

Bernardo Silva left for Real Madrid on a free. John Stones, Manuel Akanji and Nathan Aké all departed without confirmed destinations, and Jack Grealish went to Everton on loan. That is a defence substantially dismantled.

What has actually happened

City won 4–1 at Crystal Palace and 2–1 at home to Bournemouth. Six points, six scored, two conceded. Erling Haaland has two goals in two, and Rayan Cherki also has two.

The early read

The attack has transferred intact. The defence is the open question: three senior centre-backs left and the replacements are midfielders. Two goals conceded in two games is fine — the fixtures have not tested it.

What is different about a Maresca team

Guardiola's City controlled games through positional discipline and a single deep midfielder. Maresca has bought two £100m-plus central midfielders in one window, which suggests a different structure — more bodies in midfield, more rotation, less reliance on one holding player.

That matters because Rodri's succession was the unresolved problem of the previous regime. City have answered it by buying Enzo Fernández and Elliot Anderson rather than by finding one direct replacement.

What happens next

City host Coventry on Saturday — a promoted side that has yet to score a Premier League goal and sits 19th, 0 points from 2, 0-4. Our predictions page makes this the most one-sided fixture in Europe this weekend.

The real examination comes later: away trips, European nights, and the first time a well-organised side sits deep and asks the rebuilt defence to play out under pressure.

What the departures actually cost

The incoming business has dominated coverage, but the outgoing column is where the risk sits. John Stones, Manuel Akanji and Nathan Aké all left without confirmed destinations, and Bernardo Silva joined Real Madrid on a free.

That is three senior centre-backs and the squad's most versatile creator, replaced by two central midfielders and a winger. City have conceded two goals in two matches, so nothing has broken — but the structural change is larger than the results so far suggest.

The one thing Guardiola left behind

Erling Haaland. Two goals in two games, and the single biggest reason a managerial transition at the Etihad is survivable. A side that changes philosophy, loses four defenders and rebuilds midfield can still win matches if the centre-forward converts what arrives.

Rayan Cherki's two goals matter for the same reason: City's attacking output has not depended on the new signings settling, which buys Maresca weeks he would not otherwise have.

How other clubs have handled this

Replacing a long-serving, era-defining manager usually costs a season. The clubs that avoid it tend to do one of two things: promote from within to preserve continuity, or spend heavily enough that the squad improves faster than the system degrades.

City have chosen the second, decisively. £306m on three players is not a hedge; it is a bet that quality covers transition. The first evidence is encouraging and the sample is tiny.

The measurable test

Watch the goals-conceded column rather than the points column between now and November. City finished the previous era as one of the meanest defences in Europe. If they are still conceding around one a game in three months with this personnel, the transition has worked. If it drifts towards two, the missing centre-backs will be the story.
