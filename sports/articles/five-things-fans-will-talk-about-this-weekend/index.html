Five Things Football Fans Will Be Talking About This Weekend

Spurs have spent £185m and scored nothing, Hull City are fourth without conceding, and Chelsea sold their midfield to a title rival. The five arguments this weekend will produce.

BRYME Editorial2026-09-036 min read

Every weekend produces the same handful of arguments in group chats. Here are the five this one will produce, and the numbers you will want when the argument starts.

1. Tottenham have spent £185m and scored nothing

Sandro Tonali (£100m) and Mateus Fernandes (£85m) arrived to build the most expensive midfield in the club's history. Spurs are 20th, 0 points from 2, 0-5 — zero goals in two matches, five conceded, beaten 3–0 at Brentford and 2–0 at home by Newcastle.

The argument

Is this a squad problem or a striker problem? Roberto De Zerbi bought midfielders and loaned Mudryk. Nobody bought a number nine.

2. Hull City are fourth and nobody knows what to do about it

Promoted, 4th, 6 points from 2, 3-0, and they have not conceded a goal. They beat Manchester United 2–0 and won 1–0 at Coventry. Sergej Jakirović spent almost nothing — Jack Butland for £3m, Matt Targett, Ben Robinson and Hidemasa Morita on frees.

The debate is whether it is real. Our view on the predictions page is that it is real enough to back them against Aston Villa this weekend.

3. Chelsea sold Enzo Fernández to a title rival

£125m from Manchester City on deadline day, with no direct replacement signed. Chelsea are top of the table, so the decision has not hurt yet. They have also conceded five goals in two games, both of which they won by a single goal — 3–2 at Fulham and 4–3 against Brighton.

4. Liverpool lost Salah, Robertson and Konaté on frees

All three left for nothing: Mohamed Salah to Trabzonspor, Andy Robertson to Tottenham, Ibrahima Konaté to Real Madrid. Andoni Iraola replaced Arne Slot and brought in Jérémy Jacquet, Víctor Muñoz and Ronald Araújo on loan from Barcelona.

Liverpool are 13th, 2 points from 2, 4-4, having drawn both 2–2. Two points from six, and the argument about how a club lets three players of that standing run their contracts down will not go away quickly.

5. Bruno Fernandes is the league's top scorer

Three goals in two games from midfield, and Manchester United still lost 2–0 at Hull before beating Ipswich 5–2.

Michael Carrick's side are 10th, 3 points from 2, 5-4. Andrey Santos (~£50m) and Carlos Baleba (£65–70m) were bought to rebuild the midfield; so far the goals are coming from the man who was already there.

The argument nobody is having yet

Aston Villa have played two Premier League matches and scored zero goals. They sold Morgan Rogers to Chelsea for around €138m, Youri Tielemans to Manchester United and Donyell Malen to Roma — where he has since scored five goals in two games.

Unai Emery brought in Nicolas Jackson for £65m, Alejandro Garnacho on loan and Johan Manzambi for ~€60m. Villa are 18th with zero goals and five conceded, and almost nobody is talking about it because Tottenham are bottom and louder.

Why September arguments are usually wrong

Every point made above rests on two matches. Two matches is roughly five per cent of a season, and the sample is skewed by fixture difficulty: Arsenal have played the two lowest-scoring sides in the league, while Manchester United opened away at the division's only other unbeaten defence.

The useful way to hold these arguments is to name the thing that would change your mind. Ours: if Tottenham score twice in their next match, the £185m story dies. If Hull concede three, the clean-sheet story dies. If Liverpool win at Ipswich, the crisis framing dies.

What we would actually bet on

Of the five, the one most likely to still be true in December is the Liverpool point. Losing Salah, Robertson and Konaté for nothing in a single summer is a structural problem, not a form problem, and no amount of coaching from Andoni Iraola replaces a decade of goals.

The one least likely to survive is Hull City's position. Fourth is not sustainable for a side that spent £3m on its most expensive signing — but the clean sheets might be, and that is a different and more interesting claim.

One prediction to settle an argument with

Tottenham score at least twice in their next match. They have created chances in both games without converting, the squad is far too expensive to keep producing zeroes, and negative runs this extreme almost always break suddenly rather than gradually.

If we are wrong, the £185m story becomes the defining one of the Premier League season and De Zerbi's position starts being discussed seriously before October.
