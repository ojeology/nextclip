Five Matches We Cannot Wait to Watch This Weekend

Forty-eight fixtures across Europe’s top five leagues. Arsenal host Chelsea, Inter host Napoli and Monaco go to the Parc des Princes with a perfect record — these are the five that matter.

BRYME Editorial2026-09-036 min read

Matchweek 3 in England, Jornada 4 in Spain, Giornata 3 in Italy. Across Europe's top five leagues there are 48 fixtures this weekend. These are the five worth rearranging your Saturday for.

1. Arsenal v Chelsea — Sunday 6 September, 16:30 UK

First plays third, and the two biggest spenders in world football over the last five years meet at the Emirates. Arsenal are 3rd, 6 points from 2, 4-0, Chelsea 1st, 6 points from 2, 7-5.

The contrast is the appeal: Arsenal have not conceded a goal, Chelsea have conceded five while scoring seven. Chelsea sold Enzo Fernández to Manchester City for £125m on deadline day and Xabi Alonso is still settling a rebuilt midfield.

2. Inter Milan v Napoli — Saturday 5 September, 18:00 CEST

San Siro, and the best fixture in Italy this round. Inter have won both, scoring five and conceding one. Napoli beat Genoa 2–0 and then lost 2–1 at home to Como — the sort of result that makes an away trip to Inter look considerably harder.

3. Paris Saint-Germain v Monaco — Friday 4 September, 21:05 CEST

Monaco are the only side in Ligue 1 with a perfect record and no goals conceded, and they beat Marseille 2–0 last time out. PSG drew their opener 2–2 with Rennes. On current evidence the visitors are the better team, which is not a sentence Ligue 1 offers up often.

4. Valencia v Barcelona — Sunday 6 September, 16:15 CEST

Barcelona have won all three and scored twelve. Valencia have one goal in three matches and sit bottom of La Liga on goal difference. The Mestalla has historically been one of the hardest away days in Spain, which is the only reason this is a contest at all.

Raphinha has five goals in three games — the leading scorer in La Liga.

5. Juventus v AC Milan — Sunday 6 September, 20:45 CEST

Both have won both. Juventus have not conceded a goal; Milan have conceded one. Two of Italy's biggest clubs, six points each, and a combined one goal against. It may not be pretty, but it decides who stays with Roma at the top.

How to watch from Nigeria

Kick-off times in West Africa Time are the same as UK time during British Summer Time, so Arsenal v Chelsea at 16:30 UK is 16:30 in Lagos. Continental fixtures listed in CEST are one hour ahead of WAT — so Juventus v Milan at 20:45 CEST is 19:45 in Lagos.

That makes this a genuinely convenient weekend: the Friday night Ligue 1 game starts at 20:05 WAT and the biggest Sunday fixtures land in the early evening rather than late at night.

The fixture we nearly included

Hull City v Aston Villa on Saturday was the hardest omission. Hull are fourth with two wins and no goals conceded; Villa are 18th with no goals scored and five conceded. A promoted side and a club that finished in the European places last season, currently separated by fourteen places.

It missed the list because one-sided form lines rarely produce good matches, but it is the fixture most likely to produce a result nobody expects.

What makes a good weekend fixture

We pick these on three things: whether the two sides are close in the table, whether their styles genuinely conflict, and whether something is actually at stake this early in a season.

Arsenal v Chelsea satisfies all three — first against third, the best defence against a leaky attack, and an early marker in a title race. PSG v Monaco satisfies the second and third but not the first. Valencia v Barcelona satisfies none of them on paper and is included purely because the Mestalla has a habit of ignoring form.

That last one is the honest admission in any preview: some fixtures are worth watching because of where they are played rather than who is playing well.
