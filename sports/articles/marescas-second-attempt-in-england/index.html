Maresca’s Second Attempt in England: Can He Make It Work?

Enzo Maresca is back in the Premier League, this time replacing Pep Guardiola. Six points from two games, £306m spent on three signings, and a defence stripped of three senior centre-backs.

BRYME Editorial2026-09-036 min read

Enzo Maresca is back in the Premier League, and this time he has replaced Pep Guardiola. Manchester City are 2nd, 6 points from 2, 6-2 after two matches.

The question is not whether he can coach. It is whether the specific way he wants to play survives contact with the expectations at the Etihad.

The job

Guardiola left having redefined what the club expects. Maresca inherited a squad losing Bernardo Silva, John Stones, Manuel Akanji, Nathan Aké and Jack Grealish, and was given roughly £306m to spend on Enzo Fernández, Elliot Anderson and Iliman Ndiaye.

What that buying tells you

Two £100m-plus central midfielders in one window is not squad depth, it is a structural choice. Maresca wants control through numbers in midfield rather than through a single holding player.

Two matches

City won 4–1 at Crystal Palace and 2–1 at home to Bournemouth. Six points, six scored, two conceded. Comfortable at Selhurst Park, considerably less so against Bournemouth, who were the better side for a spell before losing to a late goal.

Erling Haaland has two goals; Rayan Cherki also has two.

What could go wrong

The defence. Three senior centre-backs left the club and none of the three headline signings is a defender. City have conceded two goals in two games, which is fine, but neither Crystal Palace (17th, 0 points from 2, 1-6) nor Bournemouth (14th, 1 point from 2, 2-3) has been a serious attacking test.

The second risk is patience. Maresca's approach relies on slow, deliberate build-up. At the Etihad, a run of 1–0 wins is tolerated; a run of 1–1 draws is not.

What could go right

If Enzo Fernández and Elliot Anderson work as a pair, City have solved the succession problem that hung over the previous regime without a transitional season. That would be the most valuable thing any manager in England achieves this year.

The next test

Coventry visit on Saturday — 19th, 0 points from 2, 0-4, and yet to score in the Premier League. Our predictions page has City winning comfortably. The genuine examination arrives when a side sits deep and dares the new-look defence to play through them.

What went wrong the first time

Maresca's previous spell in English football ended with a reputation for control that his critics called caution. The complaint was consistent: patient build-up produced territory and possession but not enough clear chances, and matches that should have been won comfortably were drawn.

The reason it matters now is that Manchester City is the least forgiving environment in which to relearn that lesson. City draws are treated as defeats.

Why this squad suits him better

The criticism of his approach loses force when the squad contains Erling Haaland. Patient build-up producing few clear chances is a problem when nobody converts them; it is a strategy when the centre-forward scores from almost anything.

Haaland has two goals in two matches. Rayan Cherki has two more. The system's weakness — chance volume — is precisely the weakness this squad is built to absorb.

The defensive gamble

Losing John Stones, Manuel Akanji and Nathan Aké in one window leaves a thin back line, and Maresca's structure asks defenders to hold a high line and defend large spaces behind it. That combination — high line, fewer senior centre-backs — is the clearest risk in his plan.

Gérónimo Rulli arrived from Marseille for around £1.7m as goalkeeping cover, which is sensible but does not address the outfield issue.

What success looks like by Christmas

Not necessarily top of the table. A reasonable measure would be: City within three points of the leaders, conceding around a goal a game, and the Enzo Fernández–Elliot Anderson pairing established as the first-choice midfield.

Hit those three and Maresca has done the hard part, because the hard part was never winning matches against Coventry. It was replacing a manager whose ideas had defined the club for a decade without the squad losing its shape while he did it.

The verdict

Yes, with one condition. Maresca has the striker his system has always needed, a midfield rebuilt to his specification and a squad deep enough to absorb a poor month.

The condition is the back line. Three senior centre-backs left and none were replaced, and a high defensive line without established defenders behind it is the one way this plan fails quickly.

City host Coventry on Saturday and will win comfortably. The season starts properly the first time somebody runs at that defence.
