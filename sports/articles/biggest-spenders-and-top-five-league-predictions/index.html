Football's Five Biggest Spenders — And Our Score Predictions For Europe's Top Five Leagues

Five clubs have spent more than €11 billion between them since 2021. Here is where that money went — and then a predicted scoreline for every single fixture in England, Spain, Italy, Germany and France this weekend.

BRYME Editorial3 September 20269 min read

The five clubs that spent the most

Gross transfer expenditure over the last five seasons, per Transfermarkt. This is money out of the door on incoming players — not net spend, so sales are not deducted. That distinction matters, and we come back to it below.

1Chelsea€3.12bn

No club in football history has spent at this rate. The Boehly–Clearlake era turned Chelsea into a volume business: long contracts, young players, enormous fees, and a squad big enough to field two teams. They have also recouped more than anyone, which is the only reason the accounts survive it.

2Manchester City€2.37bn

The most efficient spending on this list. City buy fewer players at higher prices and hit far more often than they miss. The Elliot Anderson deal and the Rodri succession problem are the current chapter.

3Manchester United€2.06bn

The cautionary tale. United's outlay is within touching distance of City's, but the return is not close. Several rebuilds, several managers, several sporting directors — and the squad still reads like it was assembled by five different people, because it was.

4Paris Saint-Germain€2.00bn

Two distinct phases inside one number. The galáctico years — Messi, Neymar, Mbappé wages and fees — and then the deliberate pivot to younger, cheaper, hungrier players. The second phase is the one that finally won them a European Cup.

5Juventus€1.91bn

The odd one out, because Juventus spent like a superclub while sliding out of the Serie A title picture. Enormous churn, heavy amortisation, a points deduction and a financial reset. The bill for the Ronaldo years took a long time to clear.

What the number does and does not tell you

Gross spend is the loudest statistic in football and one of the least useful on its own. Three things are worth holding in mind.

Sales are invisible here. Chelsea's €3.12bn looks reckless until you set it against academy sales and player trading that have brought back close to two-thirds of it. On a net basis the gap between Chelsea and the chasing pack narrows sharply.

Squad size inflates the figure. Chelsea and Juventus have signed roughly twice as many players as City over the period. A club that buys thirty players at €40m and a club that buys twelve at €90m can land on similar totals with completely different strategies behind them.

Spending predicts competence, not trophies. Manchester United are the third-biggest spender in world football and have not won a league title in the period. Money buys you the right to compete. It does not buy coherence, and coherence is what actually shows up on Saturday.

Five scorelines we would actually put our name to

Manchester City 3–0 Coventry. Coventry have not scored a league goal this season and have lost both games. City have won their last two by an aggregate of 6–2. The clearest evidence gap on the card.

Schalke 0–3 Bayern. Schalke were beaten 3–0 at Augsburg on matchday one. Bayern won 5–1. Enormous noise, very little resistance.

Valencia 1–3 Barcelona. One goal scored in three matches against ten scored in two. The Mestalla is a hard ground, but that is not a gap home advantage closes.

Hull City 1–0 Aston Villa. The pick we would defend hardest. Hull have six points and have not conceded a goal, including 2–0 against Manchester United. Villa have lost both, scored none, conceded five. Reputation says Villa; every number on the page says Hull.

Arsenal 2–1 Chelsea. The tightest call. Both on six points, but Arsenal have conceded nothing in two matches while Chelsea have shipped five in winning 3–2 and 4–3. Two of the top three spenders in world football, separated by a defence.
