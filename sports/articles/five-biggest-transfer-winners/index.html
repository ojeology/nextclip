The Five Biggest Transfer Winners of the 2026 Window

The window shut on 1 September. Spending big and improving are not the same thing — here are the five clubs whose squads are genuinely better, and the early evidence for each.

BRYME Editorial2026-09-037 min read

The 2026/27 window closed on 1 September. Some clubs spent enormous money; a smaller number actually improved. Those are different things, and two weeks of football has already started to separate them.

These are the five clubs we think won the window — judged not by outlay but by whether the squad is measurably better than it was in May.

1. Manchester City

2nd, 6 points from 2, 6-2. Enzo Maresca replaced Pep Guardiola and was handed the most expensive rebuild in the league.

Enzo Fernández at £125m from Chelsea and Elliot Anderson at ~£116m from Forest is a midfield rebuilt in a single window. Iliman Ndiaye for £65m on deadline day gave Maresca a wide forward as well. City lost Bernardo Silva and Jack Grealish and are still the deepest squad in England.

Why they won it

City replaced a departing generation without a transitional season. Two matches in, they have six points and a 4–1 win at Crystal Palace already banked.

2. Arsenal

3rd, 6 points from 2, 4-0 — and not a goal conceded. Bruno Guimarães for ~€87.5m solved the one position that had cost them in previous seasons, and Piero Hincapié at ~€49m added a defender comfortable in possession. A focused window rather than a loud one.

3. Tottenham Hotspur

The biggest spenders by headline fee: Sandro Tonali at £100m and Mateus Fernandes at £85m, with Andy Robertson and Martin Dúbravka added free. Roberto De Zerbi got everything he asked for.

And yet Spurs are 20th, 0 points from 2, 0-5. This is the honest entry on the list: the business was excellent, the results have not followed, and £185m of midfield has produced zero goals in two matches. Judge it in May, not now.

4. Nottingham Forest

Selling your best player for ~£116m is not usually a win. Forest turned Elliot Anderson into Liam Delap (£45–50m), Ousmane Diomande (~£34m), Daniel Muñoz (up to £22m) and Xaver Schlager on a free, then hired Oliver Glasner. That is a squad rebuilt from one sale with money left over.

5. Ipswich Town

Promoted clubs usually either freeze or panic. Ipswich did neither: seven arrivals led by Emersonn (£26.6m), Abdul Fatawu (£23m) and Florentino (£19m), after Kieran McKenna stepped down and Gary O'Neil took over. They have already won a Premier League match.

Four of our five are in the top half. The exception is Tottenham, and that is the reminder that a good window is a bet, not a result.

The one we may have got wrong

Chelsea. Nine in, ten out, and they top the table — but selling Enzo Fernández to a title rival on deadline day for £125m without replacing him is the kind of decision that looks very different in March.

The clubs that lost the window

A list of winners implies losers, and three stand out.

Aston Villa sold Morgan Rogers for around €138m, Youri Tielemans for ~€41m and Donyell Malen for ~€25m, then bought Nicolas Jackson (£65m), Johan Manzambi (~€60m) and João Gomes (~€40m). Enormous churn, and they have not scored a goal in two matches.

Newcastle United sold Sandro Tonali (~£100m), Bruno Guimarães (~€87.5m) and Anthony Gordon, and reinvested in four largely unproven signings. Matthias Jaissle has four points from two games, which makes this the loss that might not be a loss.

Crystal Palace banked £51m for Maxence Lacroix and up to £22m for Daniel Muñoz, and replaced them with free transfers and a swap deal. They are bottom four and have conceded six.

Why headline fees mislead

Tottenham spent £185m on two midfielders and are last. Hull City spent £3m on a goalkeeper and are fourth. Over a full season the money usually wins, which is why our list is weighted towards clubs that spent well rather than clubs that spent least — but the first fortnight is a useful reminder that a squad is a system, not a sum.

The clearest evidence for that is Manchester City. They did not simply spend the most; they spent it on two players who fill the same structural hole in different ways, which is what allows a side to lose four senior defenders and still look coherent.

What we will be checking in January

Three questions decide whether this list ages well: does Enzo Fernández make City better than Guardiola's final side; does Bruno Guimarães keep Arsenal's midfield injury-free; and do Tottenham score enough goals to make £185m of midfield relevant. We will revisit all three when the next window opens.
