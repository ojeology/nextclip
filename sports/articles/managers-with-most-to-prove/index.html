The Managers With the Most to Prove in 2026/27

Eight Premier League clubs changed manager this summer. Maresca replaced Guardiola, Alonso took Chelsea and Iraola inherited a Liverpool side that lost Salah for nothing.

BRYME Editorial2026-09-037 min read

Eight Premier League clubs changed manager over the summer. Two matchweeks in, some of those decisions already look inspired and others look expensive.

1. Enzo Maresca — Manchester City

Replacing Pep Guardiola is the hardest job in football management. Maresca was handed Enzo Fernández (£125m), Elliot Anderson (~£116m) and Iliman Ndiaye (£65m) to do it with, and lost Bernardo Silva, John Stones, Manuel Akanji, Nathan Aké and Jack Grealish.

2nd, 6 points from 2, 6-2. So far, so untroubled — but the fixture list has been kind and the expectation is absolute.

2. Xabi Alonso — Chelsea

Nine in, ten out, and Chelsea sit 1st, 6 points from 2, 7-5. The wins were 3–2 at Fulham and 4–3 against Brighton: five goals conceded in two victories. Alonso then lost Enzo Fernández on deadline day for £125m with no replacement.

The test

Chelsea are top and leaking. Alonso’s reputation was built on control at Leverkusen. Sunday at Arsenal — the only side yet to concede — is the first real examination.

3. Andoni Iraola — Liverpool

Took over from Arne Slot in the same summer Salah, Robertson and Konaté all left on frees. Two draws, 13th, 2 points from 2, 4-4. The most to prove of anyone on this list, with the least margin.

4. Matthias Jaissle — Newcastle United

Replaced Eddie Howe and immediately sold Sandro Tonali (~£100m), Bruno Guimarães (~€87.5m) and Anthony Gordon, reinvesting in Aladji Bamba, Bazoumana Touré, Sean Steur and Lukas Hornicek. Newcastle are 6th, 4 points from 2, 4-2 and won 2–0 at Spurs. A rebuild that is going better than it should be.

5. Oliver Glasner — Nottingham Forest

Left Crystal Palace for Forest and sold Elliot Anderson for ~£116m, spending it on Liam Delap, Ousmane Diomande and Daniel Muñoz. Forest are 15th, 1 point from 2, 2-3 but took a point at Anfield.

The others

Pierre Sage inherited a Crystal Palace side that lost Lacroix and Muñoz and sits 17th, 0 points from 2, 1-6. Álvaro Arbeloa at Fulham is 16th, 0 points from 2, 2-4. Marco Rose has Bournemouth 14th, 1 point from 2, 2-3. Gary O'Neil at Ipswich took over after Kieran McKenna stepped down post-promotion and has already won a match.

BRYME view

Jaissle has the best case so far — four points while selling the three best players at the club. Alonso has the most to fix, and the least time in which fixing it will look impressive.

The three who kept their jobs and still have something to prove

Not every manager under pressure is a new appointment.

Roberto De Zerbi was backed with £185m for Sandro Tonali and Mateus Fernandes and has Tottenham bottom of the table with zero goals scored. No manager in the division has a wider gap between investment and return.

Unai Emery sold Morgan Rogers for around €138m and has Aston Villa 18th, also without a goal. Emery has credit in the bank at Villa Park; he will need some of it.

Frank Lampard spent £68.5m-plus getting promoted Coventry ready and they have not scored a Premier League goal. Manchester City visit on Saturday.

How quickly these judgements change

Matthias Jaissle is the instructive case. He replaced Eddie Howe, immediately lost Tonali, Bruno Guimarães and Anthony Gordon, and by any reasonable pre-season assessment had the hardest job in the league. Newcastle then won 2–0 at Tottenham and sit sixth.

Two matches has moved him from most-pressure to least. It will move him back just as fast if the young signings need time, which most young signings do.

What we are actually measuring

The fair test for a new manager is not league position in September. It is whether the team looks like it is being coached towards something identifiable.

On that measure, Enzo Maresca and Oliver Glasner come out best — both have clear structures visible already. Xabi Alonso comes out worst, because a Chelsea side winning 4–3 and 3–2 does not resemble anything he has built before. Being top of the table buys him time to fix it, and the visit to Arsenal on Sunday will show whether he has started.
