Five Clubs That Still Need Signings — And Cannot Buy Until January

The window shut on 1 September. These five have holes they can no longer fill, and the league table is already showing exactly where.

BRYME Editorial2026-09-036 min read

The window is shut. These five cannot fix anything until January, which makes the gaps in their squads the story of their autumn.

We picked them on evidence rather than reputation: goals scored, goals conceded and points after two matchweeks.

1. Tottenham Hotspur — a striker

Spurs spent £100m on Sandro Tonali and £85m on Mateus Fernandes and have scored zero goals in two matches. 20th, 0 points from 2, 0-5. The midfield is among the most expensive ever assembled in England; the finishing is not there. Mykhailo Mudryk arrived on loan from Chelsea, which is a winger, not a solution.

The gap

Two games, no goals, five conceded. Spurs did not sign a recognised number nine and it is already the defining problem of their season.

2. Aston Villa — a replacement for Morgan Rogers

Villa sold Rogers to Chelsea for around €138m and have not scored since. 18th, 0 points from 2, 0-5. Nicolas Jackson (£65m) and Alejandro Garnacho on loan arrived to cover it, and João Gomes and Johan Manzambi refreshed the midfield, but Unai Emery has lost 0–4 at Brighton and 0–1 at home to Arsenal.

3. Coventry City — a Premier League forward

Promoted Coventry spent £68.5m-plus on Caleb Yirenkyi, Carl Rushworth, Loum Tchaouna and Aurèle Amenda. Frank Lampard's side have still not scored a top-flight goal: 19th, 0 points from 2, 0-4. They host Manchester City on Saturday.

4. Crystal Palace — a centre-back

Palace sold Maxence Lacroix to Chelsea for £51m and Daniel Muñoz for up to £22m, and replaced them largely with free transfers and a swap. Pierre Sage inherits 17th, 0 points from 2, 1-6. The rebuild is coherent on paper; the defence has conceded six already.

5. Fulham — depth anywhere

Álvaro Arbeloa's first window brought Gonzalo García (£34m) and César Palacios from Real Madrid and Shea Charles (£30m). Fulham are 16th, 0 points from 2, 2-4 having lost both. Raúl Jiménez left on a free and nobody of that profile replaced him.

What happens now

Nothing, until 1 January. That is the point. Four of these five sit in the bottom five of the table, and the only tool available between now and the new year is coaching. Spurs are the interesting case: their squad cost more than almost anyone's and their problem is the cheapest one to have — a finisher.

The free agent market is still open

One detail worth remembering: the transfer window closing does not stop clubs signing players without a contract. Every one of these five could add an unattached free agent tomorrow, and several will if injuries arrive.

It is an imperfect tool. The players available are, by definition, the ones nobody signed during the window. But Arsenal took Illan Meslier on a free, Brentford took Callum Wilson, Liverpool's rivals took Andy Robertson and Martin Dúbravka to Tottenham — useful players do move for nothing.

What coaching can actually fix

Of the five gaps we have identified, two are coachable and three are not.

Crystal Palace's defensive problem is largely structural: they lost two starters and are asking free signings to replicate them. A back line can be drilled into better shape, and Pierre Sage has four months to do it.

Fulham's lack of depth is similarly manageable if Álvaro Arbeloa rotates carefully and the squad stays healthy.

Tottenham, Aston Villa and Coventry all need goals, and goalscoring is the hardest thing to coach into a squad that does not have it. Spurs have the most talented squad of the three and the most obvious absence — no recognised number nine — which is why they are top of our list despite spending the most money.

The January consequence

Every one of these clubs will now pay a premium in January, because selling clubs know exactly how badly they need the position filled. A striker who cost £40m in August costs £55m in January to a side sitting in the relegation zone.

That is the real cost of an unfinished window, and it is why the sides on this list would have been better served by a less impressive summer that solved a duller problem.
